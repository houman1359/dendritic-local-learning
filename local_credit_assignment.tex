\begin{abstract}
Conductance-based shunting inhibition, long studied as a gain-control mechanism, also creates favorable conditions for local credit assignment in dendritic networks.
Starting from steady-state conductance equations, we derive exact gradients for compartmental dendritic trees and show that each synaptic gradient factorizes into a synapse-local eligibility term and a single non-local compartment error.
This factorization motivates a family of local learning rules (3F/4F/5F) that combine synapse-local variables with a somatic error broadcast.
On matched MNIST architectures, shunting raises the local-vs-backprop cosine from $0.006$ to $0.202$ and lowers scale mismatch from $1.053$ to $0.117$, corresponding to about $30\times$ relative improvement in direction and about $10\times$ lower scale distortion.
The advantage is regime-dependent, growing with inhibitory conductance and becoming largest on tasks that require robust credit signals under noise.
On a hard cue-integration benchmark where the model must discover separate dendritic pathways, per-soma broadcast remains unstable under shunting local learning, but a hierarchical pathway-vector teaching signal with router-derived branch-role plasticity stabilizes performance at $99.1 \pm 0.3\%$ test accuracy over 5 seeds.
Together, these results identify divisive normalization as a useful mechanism for improving local credit assignment in conductance-based dendritic networks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%=============================================================================

Credit assignment in deep networks usually depends on backpropagation: global error transport through symmetric pathways with no obvious biological substrate.
Dendritic compartments suggest an alternative.
Each synapse has access to rich local state such as driving force, membrane conductance, and branch voltage, while supervisory information could arrive as a low-bandwidth broadcast from the soma \cite{richards2019dendritic}.
The question in this paper is whether such local information is sufficient for useful learning.

We show that it can be, but only in a specific biophysical regime.
Starting from conductance-based dendritic voltage equations \cite{koch1999biophysics}, we derive exact gradients for dendritic trees (Theorem~\ref{thm:tree_backprop}) and show that each synaptic gradient factorizes into purely local terms and a single non-local compartment error.
Approximating that compartment error with a broadcast signal yields a family of local learning rules (3F, 4F, and 5F) that use only branch-local variables together with the broadcast.
For tasks where different branches must be credited differently, we further introduce a structured pathway-vector signal that preserves latent pathway identity during credit assignment.

The central empirical finding is that these local rules work much better with \emph{shunting inhibition} than with an additive control.
In the conductance model, synaptic sensitivity depends on both driving force and compartment input resistance $R_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$.
When inhibitory reversal is near rest, shunting increases total conductance and reduces both the magnitude and variability of $R_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$ across compartments.
In our experiments, this improves both the directional alignment and the scale stability of local credit signals relative to additive controls.
The gains are largest in regimes that stress credit assignment, particularly under strong inhibition and noisy inputs.
These results suggest a mechanistic link between divisive normalization \cite{carandini2012normalization} and the quality of local credit signals.

We quantify these effects using a gradient-fidelity diagnostic based on directional alignment (cosine similarity) and scale mismatch between local and backprop gradients (Table~\ref{tab:gradient_alignment_summary}, Fig.~\ref{fig:gradient_fidelity}).
The absolute cosine values remain modest, but they are consistently more positive under shunting and predict which local-learning regimes ultimately succeed.
The shunting advantage is regime-dependent: it grows with inhibitory conductance and is largest on tasks where credit signals must remain robust under noise (Fig.~\ref{fig:competence_regime}).
On standard classification benchmarks, simple per-soma broadcast is often sufficient.
On the hard cue-routing benchmark, however, scalar broadcast collapses distinctions between competing branches, motivating the structured pathway-vector rule studied in Sec.~\ref{sec:cue_routing}.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact gradients for conductance-based dendritic trees.}
We derive exact loss gradients for dendritic trees and show that each synaptic gradient factorizes into a synapse-local eligibility term and a compartment-specific error.
\item \textbf{A family of local learning rules.}
We use this factorization to define 3F/4F/5F local updates based on synapse-local variables and a somatic broadcast, and extend this family with PV-LocalCA when pathway identity must be preserved.
\item \textbf{A regime-dependent advantage for shunting.}
In matched architectures, shunting improves gradient fidelity and local learning relative to an additive control, with the largest gains in noisy or inhibition-dependent settings.
\item \textbf{A gradient-fidelity diagnostic.}
We use a component-wise local-vs-backprop diagnostic to link dendritic architecture and broadcast design to the quality of local credit signals.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Compartmental Voltage Model and Gradient Derivation}
\label{sec:model}
%=============================================================================

We use a steady-state conductance model derived from discretized passive cable dynamics \cite{koch1999biophysics,dayan2001theoretical}.
In normalized units, leak and inhibitory reversal are set to $0$, the excitatory reversal is set to $1$, and leak conductance is fixed at $1$.
Each compartment voltage is therefore a conductance-weighted average. Two properties of this form matter for what follows: local sensitivities depend on both driving force $(E{-}V)$ and input resistance $R^{\mathrm{tot}}$, and shunting inhibition corresponds to adding conductance when $E_{\mathrm{inh}}\!\approx\!0$.

\subsection{Voltage Equation and Local Sensitivities}

Consider compartment $n$ with synaptic inputs $j$ (activity $x_j$, reversal $E_j$, conductance $g_j^{\mathrm{syn}}\!\geq\!0$) and dendritic inputs from children (voltage $V_j$, conductance $g_j^{\mathrm{den}}\!\geq\!0$).
The steady-state voltage is:
\begin{equation}
V_n = \frac{\sum_j E_j x_j g_j^{\mathrm{syn}} + \sum_j V_j g_j^{\mathrm{den}}}{\underbrace{\sum_j x_j g_j^{\mathrm{syn}} + \sum_j g_j^{\mathrm{den}} + 1}_{g_n^{\mathrm{tot}}}},
\qquad R_n^{\mathrm{tot}} = 1/g_n^{\mathrm{tot}}.
\label{eq:voltage}
\end{equation}
$V_n$ is a convex combination of reversal potentials, child voltages, and leak. Writing $\mathcal{S}_n = \{0\} \cup \{E_j\}_j \cup \{V_j\}_j$, we have $\min\mathcal{S}_n \leq V_n \leq \max\mathcal{S}_n$ and $0 < R_n^{\mathrm{tot}} \leq 1$.
The local sensitivities follow directly:

\begin{proposition}[Local Sensitivities]
\label{prop:sensitivities}
\vspace{-4pt}
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial V_n}{\partial g_i^{\mathrm{syn}}} = x_i R_n^{\mathrm{tot}} (E_i - V_n),
\quad
\frac{\partial V_n}{\partial V_i} = g_i^{\mathrm{den}} R_n^{\mathrm{tot}},
\quad
\frac{\partial V_n}{\partial g_i^{\mathrm{den}}} = R_n^{\mathrm{tot}} (V_i - V_n).
\label{eq:sensitivities}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Shunting Inhibition as Divisive Gain Control}
\label{sec:shunting}

An inhibitory synapse with $E_{\mathrm{inh}}\!\approx\!0$ contributes current $(0{-}V_n)x_j g_j^{\mathrm{syn}}$ and increases $g_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$.
Its sensitivity is $\partial V_n / \partial g_j^{\mathrm{syn}} = -x_j R_n^{\mathrm{tot}} V_n$, which corresponds to multiplicative attenuation (divisive normalization).
Shunting is divisive at the voltage level, but its effect on firing rates can be subtractive in some regimes \cite{holt1997shunting}.
Inhibitory plasticity can balance excitation dynamically \cite{vogels2011inhibitory}; our learned inhibitory conductances serve an analogous role.

\subsection{Exact Gradients for Dendritic Trees}

Let $V_0$ be the somatic output, $\hat{y} = W_{\mathrm{dec}} V_0$ the decoder, and $\delta_0 = W_{\mathrm{dec}}^\top (\partial L / \partial \hat{y})$ the somatic error.

\begin{theorem}[Backpropagation on a Dendritic Tree]
\label{thm:tree_backprop}
For a rooted dendritic tree with soma at node $0$ and unique parent $p(n)$ for each non-somatic compartment, the loss gradient satisfies
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial L}{\partial V_n}
= \frac{\partial L}{\partial V_{p(n)}}\, R_{p(n)}^{\mathrm{tot}}\, g_{n\to p(n)}^{\mathrm{den}},
\label{eq:tree_recursion}
\end{equation}
with boundary condition $\partial L / \partial V_0 = \delta_0$. Because the graph is a tree, each compartment has a unique path to the soma. Defining the path gain
\begin{equation}
\alpha_n
= \prod_{(i\to k)\in \mathrm{path}(n\leadsto 0)} R_k^{\mathrm{tot}}\, g_{i\to k}^{\mathrm{den}},
\qquad
\frac{\partial L}{\partial V_n} = \alpha_n \delta_0,
\label{eq:path_sum}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_0 = 1$.
\end{theorem}
\vspace{-4pt}
\begin{proof}
Apply the chain rule on the tree-structured computation graph using Prop.~\ref{prop:sensitivities}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Local--Global Factorization]
\label{cor:factorization}
The exact synaptic gradient at compartment $n$ factorizes as:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial L}{\partial g_i^{\mathrm{syn}}} =
\underbrace{x_i\, R_n^{\mathrm{tot}}\, (E_i - V_n)}_{\text{synapse-local eligibility}} \;\cdot\;
\underbrace{\frac{\partial L}{\partial V_n}}_{\text{compartment error}},
\label{eq:factorization}
\end{equation}
where the eligibility term depends only on quantities available at synapse $i$ (presynaptic activity $x_i$, input resistance $R_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$, driving force $E_i - V_n$), and the compartment error $\partial L / \partial V_n$ is the sole non-local quantity.
\end{corollary}
\vspace{-2pt}
This factorization isolates the only non-local quantity: $\partial L / \partial V_n$. Any practical local rule therefore depends on how well a broadcast signal approximates that compartment error, which we evaluate in Sec.~\ref{sec:grad_fidelity}.
In the models studied here, shunting improves this approximation by reducing and stabilizing $R_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$, which narrows the spread of local sensitivities across the tree.

\section{Local Learning Rules}
\label{sec:local_rules}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Broadcast Error Approximation}

We approximate the exact compartment error $\partial L / \partial V_n$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:factorization}) with a broadcast signal $e_n$ derived from the somatic error $\delta_0 = W_{\mathrm{dec}}^\top (\partial L / \partial \hat{y})$.
We consider four broadcast modes of increasing locality:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scalar:} $e_n = \bar{\delta}\cdot\mathbf{1}$, where $\bar{\delta} = \mathrm{mean}(\delta_0)$ is a single scalar broadcast to all compartments.
\item \textbf{Per-soma:} $e_n = \delta_0$ when the layer dimension matches the output; layers with mismatched dimensions fall back to scalar.
\item \textbf{Local mismatch:} $e_n = (1{-}\alpha)\,\bar{\delta}\cdot\widetilde{m}_n + \alpha\,\bar{\delta}$, where $\widetilde{m}_n$ is the RMS-normalized, batch-centered parent--child voltage difference $P_n - V_n$ and $\alpha = 0.2$.
\item \textbf{Pathway-vector:} $e_n = \alpha\,\bar{\delta}\cdot\mathbf{1} + (1{-}\alpha)\,\Gamma_n(\delta_0)$, where $\Gamma_n$ projects the somatic error into router-inferred pathway roles, gates those roles by local pathway activity, and transports the resulting vector hierarchically through the block-structured dendritic tree.
\end{itemize}
We use per-soma broadcast by default. In our experiments, the local-mismatch variant performs much worse (Appendix~\ref{app:extra_results}), indicating that broadcast quality is critical. Under shunting, this simple broadcast is often sufficient on standard classification tasks, but the cue-routing benchmark in Sec.~\ref{sec:cue_routing} requires the structured pathway-vector form. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:model_mode_inventory} summarizes the broader set of implemented model families, training strategies, and broadcast modes used throughout the study.

\subsection{Three-Factor Rule (3F)}

\begin{definition}[3F Update]
For synaptic and dendritic conductances:
\begin{equation}
\Delta g_j^{\mathrm{syn}} = \eta \langle x_j R_n^{\mathrm{tot}} (E_j - V_n)\, e_n \rangle_B,
\qquad
\Delta g_j^{\mathrm{den}} = \eta \langle R_n^{\mathrm{tot}} (V_j - V_n)\, e_n \rangle_B,
\label{eq:3f}
\end{equation}
where $\langle\cdot\rangle_B$ denotes the batch average.
\end{definition}

The three factors are presynaptic activity $x_j$ (or a voltage difference), postsynaptic modulation through driving force and input resistance, and the broadcast error $e_n$.
The same rule applies to excitatory and inhibitory synapses. The sign difference comes from the driving force $(E_j - V_n)$.

\paragraph{Additive control.}
Rule~\eqref{eq:3f} is the local gradient of the \emph{shunting} voltage $V_n = \sum E_j g_j x_j / g_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$.
For the additive control ($V_n = \sum g_j x_j$, no divisive normalization), the correct local gradient is simpler:
$\Delta g_j^{\mathrm{syn}} = \eta \langle x_j\, e_n \rangle_B$,
with no driving-force or $R_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$ terms ($R_n^{\mathrm{tot}} \equiv 1$ by definition since there is no denominator).
Throughout, each architecture uses the learning rule derived from its own forward-pass dynamics. This keeps the comparison architecture-matched rather than applying a shunting-derived rule to an additive model.

\subsection{Higher-Order Rules: 4F and 5F}

\textbf{4F (heuristic attenuation proxy).}
In the exact gradient~\eqref{eq:path_sum}, the path gain $\alpha_n$ attenuates the somatic error differently at each compartment.
To compensate without explicitly computing that gain, we use an online correlation proxy
\[
\rho_n = \Cov(\bar{V}_n, \bar{V}_0) / (\sqrt{\Var(\bar{V}_n)\Var(\bar{V}_0)} + \varepsilon).
\]
High $\rho_n$ indicates that compartment and somatic activity covary strongly, making the broadcast $\delta_0$ a more plausible proxy for the local compartment error; low $\rho_n$ down-weights updates where that proxy is likely to be poor.
$\rho_n$ is estimated online via exponential moving average ($\alpha = 0.1$).

\textbf{5F (confidence-weighted 4F).}
The current 5F implementation adds a second heuristic factor
\[
\phi_n = \Var(V_n) / (\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2 + \varepsilon),
\]
where $\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2$ is the residual variance of $V_n$ after linear regression on the parent voltage.
This is an inverse residual-fraction or signal-to-noise proxy: it becomes large when compartment activity is strong and stable relative to residual fluctuations unexplained by the parent, and small when local fluctuations dominate.
Operationally, $\phi_n$ behaves as a branch-wise confidence gate rather than as a pure unique-information estimator.
The large empirical gap between 4F and 5F in our sweeps indicates that this confidence reweighting removes a major source of update noise: 3F/4F treat all compartments with similar broadcast quality similarly, whereas 5F suppresses branches whose local voltage is dominated by residual variability.
When $\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2$ is very small, $\phi_n$ would otherwise become degenerate, so we clamp it to a finite interval and interpret saturated values simply as ``high-confidence'' branches rather than as precise estimates of information content.
Clamped to $[0.25, 4.0]$ for stability.

\begin{proposition}[5F Update]
\begin{equation}
\Delta g_j^{\mathrm{syn}} = \eta\, \rho_n \phi_n \langle x_j R_n^{\mathrm{tot}} (E_j - V_n)\, e_n \rangle_B.
\label{eq:5f}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Gradient alignment under random broadcast.}
When the broadcast matrix $B_n$ has i.i.d.\ zero-mean entries with $\mathbb{E}[B_n^\top B_n]=\alpha I$, the expected cosine between local and exact gradients is positive: $\mathbb{E}[\cos\angle(g^{\mathrm{local}}, g^{\mathrm{exact}})] \geq c_n > 0$, by an argument analogous to feedback alignment \cite{lillicrap2016random}.
The constant $c_n$ depends on the correlation between local factors and the exact path gain \eqref{eq:path_sum}; shunting architecture increases this correlation by normalizing the scale of intermediate signals.

\subsection{Structured pathway feedback and branch-role plasticity}

For tasks with latent pathway structure, scalar broadcast can erase the distinction between different dendritic branches that should be credited differently.
We therefore augment the local rule with a structured feedback variant used only on the cue-routing benchmark.
We refer to this structured cue-routing rule as \emph{PV-LocalCA} (pathway-vector LocalCA).

\paragraph{Hierarchical pathway-vector broadcast.}
The encoder/router defines a low-dimensional role basis over latent pathways (for example, one role per cue stream together with shared context).
Instead of collapsing $\delta_0$ to a scalar or per-soma vector, we project it into this role basis, gate each role by the local pathway activity on the target branch, and propagate the resulting vector through the proximal-to-distal block tree.
Earlier dendritic layers therefore receive a pathway-specific teaching signal that remains aligned with their downstream descendants.
Writing the low-rank role coefficients as $c_k$ and the local branch role weights as $q_{n,k}$, the structured broadcast takes the form
\[
e_n^{\mathrm{PV}} = \sum_{k=1}^K q_{n,k} c_k,
\]
with $K$ latent pathways.
Scalar broadcast is the special case $K{=}1$, while per-soma broadcast corresponds to a dense output-aligned basis.

\paragraph{Router-derived branch roles.}
Rather than assigning hand-coded apical/basal labels, each branch $j$ receives a normalized role profile $r_j$ inferred from router assignments and the branch's incoming block weights.
Plasticity then depends on two additional local quantities: a branch selectivity factor that up-weights branches with concentrated role mass, and a synapse-specific alignment factor proportional to $\langle r_i, r_j\rangle$ for pre/post role profiles.
This biases learning toward pathway-consistent afferents without imposing a heuristic branch taxonomy.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Setup}

We evaluate the model in two experimental settings: a \emph{capacity-calibrated} regime, where the same architectures achieve strong performance under backpropagation, and a set of \emph{controlled sweeps} that isolate the effects of inhibition strength, broadcast mode, and architecture on credit-signal quality.
Our main datasets are MNIST, Fashion-MNIST \cite{xiao2017fashionmnist}, and four synthetic tasks: \emph{context gating} (context-dependent category boundaries), \emph{noise resilience} (learning under structured input noise), \emph{info shunting} (a task designed to require inhibition-mediated processing), and \emph{cue integration} (two competing sensory cues plus context indicating which cue is reliable).
The cue-integration benchmark is specifically designed to test whether dendritic pathway structure can support context-dependent reliability weighting rather than simple averaging: a fixed-pathway control duplicates context into each cue pathway, while a learned router must discover that separation from data.
Architectures include point MLP baselines and dendritic cores with either additive integration or shunting (conductance-based) inhibition.
Our primary credit-assignment comparisons are architecture-matched additive vs.\ shunting cores and local vs.\ backprop training; DFA is included as a supplementary feedback baseline where the layer geometry permits it.
Most headline MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and context-gating results use the 5F rule with per-soma broadcast.
The hard cue-routing benchmark instead uses PV-LocalCA: a structured 3F variant with pathway-vector broadcast, path propagation, inhibitory homeostasis, and router-derived branch-role plasticity.
For the inhibition sweeps, we additionally evaluate a transported-error oracle that recursively applies the exact path gains from Theorem~\ref{thm:tree_backprop}; this oracle is used only as an upper-bound control, not as the main biological proposal.
Because context gating benefits from a small HSIC auxiliary objective (about $3$ percentage points in Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:s4_verification}C), we treat it as a supporting structured benchmark rather than as the cleanest test of the base LocalCA rule.
We report means $\pm$ s.d.\ across 3--5 seeds for all headline results.
We will release code and configuration files after the review process.
The experiments are organized in three stages: we first evaluate how well different broadcasts track exact gradients, then test whether those fidelity differences translate into learning, and finally study a structured-feedback extension for settings where scalar broadcast fails.

\paragraph{Alignment dynamics over training.}
Figure~\ref{fig:gradient_fidelity}B tracks per-layer cosine similarity over epochs.
In shunting networks, proximal layers approach high positive alignment over training, whereas distal layers show smaller but still positive alignment.
Additive networks show near-zero or negative alignment at all layers and epochs.
Component-wise decomposition (Fig.~\ref{fig:gradient_fidelity}C) reveals that dendritic conductances and excitatory synapses carry the strongest alignment signal in shunting networks, consistent with the biophysical role of conductance-based driving forces.
Although the absolute cosines are still far from $1$, they are strongly predictive of local-learning competence: the same inhibition regimes that raise these cosines are the regimes in which local training succeeds.

\paragraph{Exact factorization sanity check.}
Using the exact compartment errors $\partial L/\partial V_n$ extracted by autograd, we reconstructed the corresponding synaptic and dendritic conductance gradients directly from the factorization in Corollary~\ref{cor:factorization}, using the exact piecewise denominator derivative of the implemented shunting layer.
Across the full 100-run inhibition sweep used for the mechanistic analysis, the reconstructed gradients match autograd to numerical precision (weighted cosine $1.000001 \pm 0.000001$, weighted scale mismatch $< 4\times 10^{-8}$).
This confirms that the theory is operational in code: the only approximation made by LocalCA is in the broadcast substitute for $\partial L/\partial V_n$, not in the local eligibility factors.

\subsection{Mechanistic evidence for the shunting advantage}
\label{sec:mechanistic}

We provide evidence for why shunting improves gradient fidelity and test broadcast robustness (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanistic}).

\paragraph{Path-gain concentration.}
For a tree, the exact compartment error is the somatic error multiplied by a unique path gain $\alpha_n = \prod_{k\in \mathrm{path}(n\to 0)} R_k^{\mathrm{tot}} g_k^{\mathrm{den}}$.
Panel~A measures the coefficient of variation of these path gains across compartments.
On MNIST, shunting keeps the path-gain distribution tightly concentrated across inhibition levels (CV about $0.21$--$0.27$), whereas the additive control shows much broader dispersion (CV about $0.53$--$1.08$).
This is the deterministic quantity that scalar or per-soma broadcast is implicitly trying to ignore: when the omitted path gains vary less, one shared broadcast is a better proxy for the true compartment-specific error.

\paragraph{Direct compartment-error fidelity.}
The theorem identifies $\partial L / \partial V_n$ as the sole non-local term, so we measure it directly.
Panel~B compares the local broadcast $e_n$ with the exact compartment error on the noise-resilience sweep, using cosine similarity at the compartment level.
Per-soma broadcast tracks the exact compartment error much better under shunting than under additive integration, rising from $0.20$ at $N_I{=}0$ to about $0.32$ near the best-performing inhibition range, while additive stays near zero or negative.
The transported-error oracle obtained by recursively applying the exact path gains improves fidelity further in both architectures, showing that the theorem is not merely descriptive: it defines an operational upper bound on low-bandwidth broadcast.
The large gap between the additive per-soma broadcast and its transported upper bound shows that omitted path gains are a major part of the additive failure mode.

\paragraph{Transported broadcast is an operational upper bound.}
Panel~C trains the same local rule using the transported-error oracle as its teaching signal.
On noise resilience, additive learning rises from $33$--$47\%$ under per-soma broadcast to $75.6\%$ at $N_I{=}0$ and $98.5\%$ at $N_I{=}40$ under transported broadcast.
Shunting improves from $71.7$--$90.3\%$ under per-soma to $98.5$--$99.3\%$ under transported broadcast across the same sweep.
On MNIST, transported broadcast reaches $94.6\%$ for additive and $96.8\%$ for shunting.
These oracle results show that broadcast fidelity is a major bottleneck for LocalCA, while the remaining additive--shunting gap under matched transported broadcast points to an additional role for conductance-shaped local sensitivities.

\paragraph{Low-bandwidth broadcast suffices.}
Testing degraded broadcast modes (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanistic}D), 4-bit quantization preserves full performance ($61.6\%$ vs.\ $61.7\%$; $p > 0.9$), while sign-only ($37.5\%$) and sparse top-30\% ($38.2\%$) degrade sharply.
Here ``effective bits per neuron'' means the precision of the broadcast channel after compression: $32$ for full-precision, $b$ for $b$-bit quantization, $1$ for sign-only, and a separate sparse code for the top-$30\%$ condition.
These results suggest that broadcast direction matters more than exact magnitude once local sensitivities are well scaled.

\paragraph{Normalization-only controls remain insufficient.}
Explicitly normalizing additive voltages improves local learning but does not close the gap to shunting (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:s7_additive_norm}).
DFA baselines (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:s5_fa_dfa}) likewise improve over point-MLP random-feedback baselines without matching the shunting conductance model.

The benefit of shunting varies across tasks.
On MNIST with matched per-soma broadcast, shunting outperforms additive by about $2$~percentage points; a similar pattern holds on Fashion-MNIST (81.1\% vs.\ 79.4\%).
On tasks that require credit signals robust to noise, the gap is much larger: $+50.3$~pp on noise resilience (with $N_I{=}10$ inhibitory synapses per branch) and $+24.8$~pp on info shunting ($N_I{=}0$).
Figure~\ref{fig:competence_regime}B--C shows that the advantage grows with inhibitory conductance strength. Additive cores can still perform reasonably on easier settings, reaching about $89\%$ on MNIST after tuning. However, they fail in regimes where inhibition-mediated normalization matters for gradient propagation.

\subsection{Scalability and generalization}

We test whether the shunting advantage persists under three stress tests: increased dendritic depth, noisy broadcast signals, and a second real-world benchmark.

\paragraph{Depth scaling.}
Varying dendritic depth from 1 to 4 layers (branch factors $[9]$ to $[3,3,3,3]$), shunting local learning degrades from $63.5\%$ to $57.4\%$ while additive local degrades more steeply from $54.9\%$ to $29.7\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalability}A).
Backprop ceilings remain stable at about $90$--$92\%$ for both architectures.
The shunting advantage grows with depth ($+8.5$~pp at depth~1 to $+27.7$~pp at depth~4), consistent with the path-gain attenuation in Theorem~\ref{thm:tree_backprop}: each additional layer introduces an attenuation factor of the form $R_k^{\mathrm{tot}} g_k^{\mathrm{den}} < 1$. In practice, shunting changes the distribution of these factors and yields better depth scaling under local learning.

\paragraph{Noise robustness.}
Adding Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ to the broadcast error (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalability}B), shunting local learning is robust to $\sigma \leq 0.1$ (about $62\%$) and degrades gracefully beyond, while additive drops from $46.5\%$ to chance at $\sigma{=}1.0$.
This pattern is consistent with the proposed mechanism: shunting produces better-scaled local sensitivities, so gradient direction is preserved more often even when the broadcast magnitude is corrupted.

\paragraph{Fashion-MNIST.}
On Fashion-MNIST (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalability}C), shunting local reaches $81.1\%$ vs.\ a $87.9\%$ backprop ceiling (gap $6.8\%$); additive local reaches $79.4\%$ vs.\ $87.4\%$ backprop (gap $8.0\%$).
The shunting advantage is modest (about $1.7$~pp) on this clean classification task, consistent with the regime-dependent pattern above: the benefit is largest on tasks that require robust credit signals under noise.

\subsection{Extension: structured feedback when scalar broadcast fails}
\label{sec:cue_routing}

The main paper focuses on low-bandwidth scalar or per-soma broadcast.
We now consider a harder cue-integration benchmark where that approximation is insufficient because different dendritic pathways must be credited differently.
The task asks the model to combine two noisy sensory cues using a context signal that indicates which cue is reliable on each trial.
In the fixed-pathway control, context is copied into both cue pathways.
In the learned-pathway setting, the router must instead discover that separation and align one latent pathway with each cue stream.

Figure~\ref{fig:cue_routing} summarizes five-seed runs for the hard benchmark together with a focused tuning sweep.
Fixed-pathway additive local learning remains near ceiling ($99.1 \pm 0.3\%$), fixed-pathway shunting local learning is strong but more variable ($92.8 \pm 6.1\%$), learned-routing additive local learning also succeeds ($98.1 \pm 2.4\%$), and standard learned-router training stays near $99.2 \pm 0.4\%$ for both additive and shunting cores.
Taken together, these controls show that the benchmark and router are learnable, while also indicating that shunting LocalCA is already somewhat stressed on this task even before learned routing is introduced.

The specific failure appears when shunting local learning uses the default per-soma broadcast.
At the original 90-epoch setting, learned-routing accuracy is only $82.6 \pm 14.2\%$.
Lowering router temperature to 0.3 or freezing the encoder for the first 20 epochs changes little ($87.6 \pm 13.3\%$ and $83.8 \pm 13.9\%$), even though the router already becomes highly specialized (mean max assignment $0.98$--$1.00$).
A focused optimization sweep shows that training budget alone is not the issue: extending training to 140 epochs leaves per-soma performance unchanged ($82.5 \pm 14.1\%$), and even lowering the learning rate to $10^{-3}$ reaches only $88.2 \pm 13.9\%$.

Replacing per-soma broadcast with PV-LocalCA changes the regime entirely.
The untuned 90-epoch version already improves to $94.0 \pm 11.0\%$, and the tuned setting (140 epochs, learning rate $10^{-3}$) reaches $99.1 \pm 0.3\%$ test and $99.4 \pm 0.2\%$ validation accuracy while maintaining strong specialization (mean max assignment $0.995 \pm 0.008$).
The failure is therefore not caused by pathway discovery itself; it arises because scalar broadcast does not preserve pathway identity once different cue branches must be credited differently.
When the feedback preserves that structure, learned-routing shunting local learning becomes reliable.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%=============================================================================

\paragraph{Dendritic models of credit assignment.}
Dendritic trees support nonlinear computation \cite{koch1983nonlinear,poiarazi2003pyramidal,london2005dendritic} and have inspired biologically plausible learning schemes: segregated dendrites \cite{guerguiev2017segregated}, dendritic prediction errors \cite{sacramento2018dendritic,urbanczik2014dendritic} (reported about $98\%$ on MNIST in an abstract-compartment setting), burst-dependent plasticity \cite{payeur2021burst,greedy2022burstccn}, latent equilibrium \cite{haider2021latent} ($98.9\%$ MNIST with abstract compartments), and dendritic localized learning \cite{lv2025dll}.
We differ in deriving rules from conductance-based equations rather than abstract compartment surrogates and in using gradient-fidelity diagnostics to ask when a low-bandwidth somatic broadcast is accurate enough to support learning.

\paragraph{Feedback alignment and local learning.}
Random feedback \cite{lillicrap2016random}, DFA \cite{nokland2016dfa}, forward-forward \cite{hinton2022forward}, PEPITA \cite{dellaferrera2022pepita}, and PAL \cite{pal2024local} established strong local-learning baselines on standard MLP benchmarks.
Recent supervised local-learning systems have also raised the empirical bar in deeper settings: AugLocal scales auxiliary-network local learning to deep CNNs including ImageNet-sized models \cite{ma2024auglocal}, DLL reports strong MLP/CNN/RNN performance under explicit plausibility criteria \cite{lv2025dll}, CCL introduces a biologically plausible dual-network training framework \cite{kao2024ccl}, and EBD provides a complementary theory of direct error broadcast through decorrelation \cite{erdogan2025ebd}.
Our aim is therefore not to claim the strongest benchmark accuracy among all local-learning methods, but to identify a biophysical mechanism that explains when low-bandwidth credit signals become useful in conductance-based dendritic networks.
Our broadcast modes generalize FA/DFA to dendrites while additionally exploiting conductance-based signals unavailable to standard architectures.
Standard FA fails entirely on dendritic layers, while DFA is compatible and shunting yields a smaller backprop--DFA gap (24.9~pp) than point MLPs (39.5~pp; Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:s5_fa_dfa}).

\paragraph{Target propagation and energy-based methods.}
DTP \cite{lee2015dtp}, DFC \cite{meulemans2021dfc}, equilibrium propagation \cite{scellier2017equilibrium}, and predictive coding \cite{whittington2019theories,millidge2022predictive} solve weight transport through diverse mechanisms.
Our contribution is orthogonal: conductance-based biophysics provides an additional route to local credit.
We do not benchmark these methods directly here because their iterative inference or controller dynamics are not architecture-matched to the steady-state conductance cores studied in this paper.
We therefore treat them as complementary solutions to locality rather than as drop-in baselines for the same model family.

\paragraph{Divisive normalization.}
Shunting implements divisive normalization \cite{carandini2012normalization}, though its effect on rates can be subtractive \cite{holt1997shunting}.
Silver \cite{silver2010neuronal} showed that inhibitory conductance modulates gain and SNR.
Beniaguev et al.\ \cite{beniaguev2021single} showed single neurons are computationally equivalent to 5--8 layer DNNs.
To our knowledge, prior work has not directly examined gradient alignment between local updates and backpropagation in the context of shunting inhibition.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%=============================================================================

We started from exact gradients for a conductance-based dendritic tree and asked when a somatic broadcast can serve as a useful proxy for compartment-specific errors.
In the models studied here, the answer depends strongly on the inhibitory regime.
Shunting changes the conductance denominator and stabilizes effective local sensitivities, improves agreement between local and backprop gradients, and leads to better local learning than an additive control, especially in noisy or inhibition-dependent settings.
Explicit normalization recovers part of this benefit but not all of it, suggesting that shunting changes more than just overall scale.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our best accuracy (91.4\% MNIST, 81.1\% Fashion-MNIST) is below several recent local-learning methods that operate in abstract compartments or standard activation spaces \cite{ma2024auglocal,lv2025dll,kao2024ccl,erdogan2025ebd}.
This reflects the constraints of operating in conductance-based voltage space: bounded voltages, positive conductances, and a denominator-heavy computation graph.
In the conservative flattened-CIFAR-10 sweep shown in Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:s6_cifar10}, shunting local learning reaches 21.2\% vs.\ 40.1\% backprop, indicating that the gradient-fidelity mechanism alone is insufficient for harder tasks without architectural advances (e.g., convolutional encoders).
That trade-off reflects our emphasis on mechanism rather than absolute benchmark performance: the gradient-fidelity analysis helps explain why some architectures support local learning better than others.
Second, naive local broadcasts remain much weaker than per-soma on MNIST, and the hard cue-routing benchmark requires an even more structured pathway-vector signal (Sec.~\ref{sec:cue_routing}).
Our claims are therefore specific to the broadcast family studied here rather than arbitrary local mismatch heuristics.
Per-soma broadcast should be understood as low-bandwidth but still neuron-global: it assumes access to the full somatic error vector and therefore sets the current locality boundary of the method.
Third, scaling to deeper architectures degrades local learning more than backprop (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalability}A; Appendix~\ref{app:depth_noise}), indicating that depth-dependent credit attenuation remains challenging.
At depth $4$, shunting local learning is still roughly $33$ percentage points below backprop, so the present rules improve deep credit assignment but do not solve it.
Fourth, standard FA is incompatible with dendritic layer geometry (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:s5_fa_dfa}), limiting the space of applicable feedback methods.
We also do not directly compare against iterative target-propagation or equilibrium-style methods, because a clean comparison would require re-implementing their inference dynamics inside the same conductance-based dendritic architecture rather than benchmarking against separate abstract models.
Finally, the weight-statistics analysis is secondary to the main credit-assignment claim and is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:weight_distributions}; it should be read as descriptive consistency rather than as core evidence for the learning mechanism.

\paragraph{Broader relevance.}
More broadly, the results are relevant to neuroscience, machine learning, and neuromorphic hardware. For neuroscience, they suggest a connection between divisive normalization and local credit fidelity, extending its known roles in gain control \cite{silver2010neuronal} and sensory coding \cite{carandini2012normalization}. For machine learning, they show that conductance-based inductive biases can shape gradient geometry in ways that benefit local learning. For neuromorphic implementations, the update rules remain strictly local and parallelizable.

\paragraph{Potential harmful consequences.}
The work is primarily methodological and does not introduce a deployable decision system or a new human dataset. Still, more efficient local learning rules for adaptive hardware could eventually be incorporated into surveillance, autonomous, or other safety-critical systems. We therefore view the most plausible risks as downstream misuse of efficient on-device learning rather than harm from the present experimental setup itself.

\paragraph{Testable predictions.}
Our framework predicts that (1)~stronger perisomatic inhibition yields more precise synaptic plasticity; (2)~GABA\textsubscript{A} blockade selectively impairs multi-layer credit tasks; (3)~the dendritic--somatic voltage correlation ($\rho_n$) increases during learning; (4)~learned excitatory weight distributions follow log-normal statistics whose width depends systematically on inhibitory conductance (Appendix~\ref{app:weight_distributions}).
These predictions can help distinguish our account from models where dendrites mainly serve as computational substrates.

\section{Implementation Details}

\subsection*{Biological Plausibility Assumptions}

Each synapse has access to: presynaptic activity $x_j$ (local), compartment voltage $V_n$ (local membrane potential), reversal potential $E_j$ (fixed by receptor type), and total input resistance $R_n^{\mathrm{tot}} = 1/g_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$ (computable from local conductances).
The 4F modulator $\rho_n$ requires online estimation of voltage covariance (biologically plausible via slow calcium signals); the 5F factor $\phi_n$ requires a linear regression proxy (implementable via eligibility traces).
The only non-local quantity is the broadcast error $e_n$, which requires a top-down or neuromodulatory signal from the soma to dendritic compartments.
We assume per-neuron resolution (one error per output neuron) for the per-soma broadcast mode.

\section{Morphology-Aware Extensions}
\label{app:morphology}

\paragraph{Path-integrated propagation.}
Modulate broadcast error by $\pi_n = \pi_{n-1} \cdot R_{n-1}^{\mathrm{tot}} \cdot \bar{g}_{n-1}^{\mathrm{den}}$, approximating depth attenuation from Eq.~\ref{eq:path_sum}.

\paragraph{Depth modulation.}
Per-branch scaling $\rho_j = \rho_{\mathrm{base}} / (d_j + \alpha)$, mirroring cable attenuation.

\paragraph{Dendritic normalization.}
$\Delta g_j^{\mathrm{den}} \leftarrow \Delta g_j^{\mathrm{den}} / (\sum_k g_k^{\mathrm{den}} + \varepsilon)$, analogous to homeostatic scaling \cite{turrigiano2008homeostatic}.

\paragraph{Pathway-vector broadcast.}
For tasks with latent pathway structure, the broadcast becomes a low-dimensional vector over router-inferred pathway roles.
This vector is gated by local pathway activity and propagated through the block-structured tree so that earlier branches receive feedback aligned with their downstream descendants.

\paragraph{Router-derived branch roles.}
Each branch receives a role profile inferred from router assignments and incoming block weights rather than from a hand-coded apical/basal label.
Plasticity is then modulated by branch selectivity and a synapse-specific role-alignment factor, emphasizing pathway-consistent updates without imposing a heuristic branch taxonomy.

\section{HSIC Auxiliary Objectives}
\label{app:hsic}

Following \cite{gretton2005hsic}, we use kernel-based HSIC objectives.
Self-decorrelation: $\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{self}} = B^{-2}\tr(\mathbf{K}_Z\mathbf{H}\mathbf{K}_Z\mathbf{H})$.
Target-correlation: $\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{target}} = -B^{-2}\tr(\mathbf{K}_Z\mathbf{H}\mathbf{K}_Y\mathbf{H})$.
Moderate weights ($0.01$--$0.1$) help on context gating; negligible on MNIST.
Online statistics ($\rho_n$, $\phi_n$) use Welford's algorithm \cite{welford1962note}.

\section{Online Variant with Eligibility Traces}
\label{app:eligibility}

Continuous-time eligibility: $\tau_e \dot{e}_j^{\mathrm{syn}} = -e_j^{\mathrm{syn}} + x_j(E_j - V_n)R_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$.
Update: $\Delta g_j^{\mathrm{syn}} \propto \int e_j^{\mathrm{syn}}(t) m_n(t)\,\mathrm{d}t$ \cite{fremaux2016three,bellec2020eprop}.

\section{Weight Statistics and Biological References}
\label{app:weight_distributions}

As a secondary analysis, we examine whether the conductance-based update naturally induces characteristic weight statistics.
Because synaptic updates scale with input resistance $R_n^{\mathrm{tot}} = 1/g_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$, the voltage equation implies multiplicative weight dynamics, creating a regime analogous to exponentiated gradient descent \cite{saxena2024lognormal}.
Such multiplicative dynamics suggest log-normal weight distributions in trained networks \cite{song2005lognormal,buzsaki2014logdynamic}.
We compare the resulting distributions against two biological references: electrophysiological data from Song et al.\ \cite{song2005lognormal} (rat V1 L5 pyramidal, $\sigma = 0.94$) and the MICrONS mm$^3$ connectome \cite{microns2021functional} (mouse V1 synapse sizes).

\paragraph{Model weight distributions are log-normal.}
We fit log-normal distributions to the active (top-$K$ masked) excitatory synaptic weights after training on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST.
Table~\ref{tab:weight_sigma} shows the fitted $\sigma$ (standard deviation of $\log w$) for each condition.
Shunting consistently produces narrower distributions than additive integration, and backpropagation produces narrower distributions than local learning.
These two factors combine approximately additively: BP+Shunting $\to$ narrowest, Local+Additive $\to$ broadest.

\paragraph{Shunting is closest to the biological references considered here.}
BP+Shunting produces $\sigma = 0.94$ on MNIST, very close to the Song et al.\ reference ($\Delta = 0.008$).
Local+Shunting produces $\sigma = 1.13$--$1.22$, close to MICrONS E$\to$E synapse sizes ($\sigma = 1.14$; $\Delta \leq 0.08$).
By contrast, Local+Additive produces $\sigma = 1.43$--$1.69$, broader than any biological reference considered.
This pattern is consistent across datasets (MNIST and Fashion-MNIST), suggesting it reflects structural properties of the learning rule rather than task-specific features.

\paragraph{The match depends on inhibitory conductance.}
We test sensitivity to the E/I synapse ratio using a sweep over $N_I \in \{0, 2, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40\}$ inhibitory synapses per branch (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:weight_sensitivity}).
Without inhibition ($N_I{=}0$), all conditions produce $\sigma > 2.0$, far from biology.
With biologically constrained ratios ($N_I{=}10$--$20$, corresponding to E/I ratios of $4{:}1$ to $2{:}1$; \cite{megias2001total}), shunting networks enter the biological range ($\sigma \approx 0.9$--$1.2$), while the effect of $N_I$ on additive networks is less systematic.
The weight distribution shape is also robust to dendritic depth (branch factors $[9]$ through $[3{,}3{,}3{,}3]$; see Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:weight_sensitivity}A): BP+Shunting converges toward Song et al.\ with increasing depth, while Local+Shunting remains near MICrONS E$\to$E across depths.

\paragraph{MICrONS connectome analysis.}
We extracted synapse size distributions from the MICrONS mm$^3$ condensed connectome (v1181; 9.0M connections, 71.9K neurons; 89\% excitatory).
Splitting by pre/post cell type: E$\to$E connections ($n{=}3.85$M) show $\sigma_{\mathrm{size}}{=}1.14$ and CV${=}1.01$; I$\to$E ($n{=}3.53$M) show $\sigma{=}0.92$ and CV${=}1.03$; E$\to$I ($n{=}1.11$M) show $\sigma{=}0.83$; I$\to$I ($n{=}0.51$M) show $\sigma{=}0.85$.
Inhibitory connections are more frequently multi-synaptic (I$\to$E: 37.5\% multi-synapse; E$\to$E: 11.5\%), and inhibitory synapses are on average smaller (median 3532 vs.\ 4676 for E$\to$E).

\paragraph{Sensitivity to ee\_synapses.}
Using the E/I grid sweep ($N_E \in \{20, 40, 80\} \times N_I \in \{0, 2, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40\}$; 168 models), excitatory $\sigma$ increases modestly with $N_E$ in both architectures ($+0.4$--$0.6$ from $N_E{=}20$ to $80$), but the shunting--additive ordering is preserved at all $N_E$ values.
The dominant driver of $\sigma$ is $N_I$: going from $N_I{=}0$ to $N_I{=}10$ reduces shunting $\sigma$ by approximately $1.0$--$1.5$ units, while the effect on additive is smaller and less systematic.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to depth.}
From the depth-scaling sweep ($[9], [3{,}3], [3{,}3{,}3], [3{,}3{,}3{,}3]$; 80 models, 5 seeds each), BP+Shunting excitatory $\sigma$ decreases with depth ($1.06 \to 0.90$), converging toward Song et al.\ ($0.94$).
Local+Shunting $\sigma$ increases slightly ($1.18 \to 1.33$) but remains in the biological range at shallow depths.
BP+Additive shows the strongest depth sensitivity ($1.38 \to 0.79$), while Local+Additive diverges with depth ($2.01 \to 2.44$).
The morphology-aware modulators (path propagation, depth/centrality weighting) have negligible effect on $\sigma$.

\paragraph{Mechanistic interpretation.}
The shunting conductance $g_n^{\mathrm{tot}} = g_n^{\mathrm{leak}} + \sum g^{\mathrm{exc}} + \sum g^{\mathrm{inh}}$ appears in the update factor $R_n^{\mathrm{tot}} = 1/g_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$.
As inhibitory conductance increases, $g_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$ grows and $R_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$ shrinks, compressing the multiplicative scale of weight updates.
This is analogous to a learning rate that self-adjusts via the total conductance: strong synapses contribute more to $g_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$, reducing their own future update magnitude. This acts as a natural form of weight-dependent regularization.
The resulting equilibrium distribution is log-normal, consistent with Loewenstein et al.\ \cite{loewenstein2011multiplicative} (multiplicative spine dynamics) and Saxena et al.\ \cite{saxena2024lognormal} (exponentiated gradient).
Additive integration lacks this self-normalizing mechanism, producing broader distributions that fall outside the biological range.

\section{Depth Scaling and Noise Robustness}
\label{app:depth_noise}

\paragraph{Depth scaling.}
Varying dendritic depth from 1--4 layers (branch factors $[9]$ to $[3,3,3,3]$): shunting local degrades from 63.5\% to 57.4\%; additive local degrades more steeply from 54.9\% to 29.7\%. The shunting advantage grows with depth ($+8.5 \to +27.7$~pp). Backprop ceilings remain stable at about $90$--$92\%$. See Fig.~\ref{fig:scalability}A.

\paragraph{Noise robustness.}
Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ on broadcast error: shunting is robust to $\sigma\!\leq\!0.1$ (about $62\%$) while additive drops from 46.5\% to chance at $\sigma{=}1.0$, confirming that shunting credit signals carry useful learning information beyond the broadcast magnitude. See Fig.~\ref{fig:scalability}B.
