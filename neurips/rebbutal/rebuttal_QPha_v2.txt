Summary

The exact-gradient reconstruction verifies the factorization with relative error below $1.9\times10^{-7}$. With exact path transport, the theorem-derived $3F$ rule reaches $97.18$ to $97.21\%$. This matches backpropagation at $97.03\%$ and $5F$ at $97.12$ to $97.20\%$. Dense compartment feedback is also unnecessary in the regular tree. One teaching coordinate shared across the $13$ compartments of each modeled neuron brings $3F$ to $97.15\%$ in shunting and $97.14\%$ in additive networks. All $15$ paired seeds improve. These results identify feedback routing, rather than local eligibility, as the main bottleneck.

1. Constrained feedback form

The unconstrained rank-$1$ SVD and the implemented broadcast answer different questions. The revision now separates them clearly.

The original `per_soma` mode reuses the full soma teaching vector coordinate by coordinate when a dendritic stage has the same number of units. At a wider stage, it averages that vector to one scalar per example and broadcasts the scalar to all compartments. We call this matched-width/scalar-fallback feedback. It is different from both a global scalar at every stage and a soma coordinate repeated over that neuron's descendants.

The requested constrained calculation shows that, after one optimal global rescaling, this implemented field retains $2.3\%$ of dendritic exact-error energy in shunting checkpoints and $0.6\%$ in additive checkpoints. This makes the constructive result especially informative. Repeating one soma coordinate over each modeled neuron's $13$ compartments raises $3F$ MNIST accuracy from $90.75\%$ to $97.15\%$ in shunting and from $91.58\%$ to $97.14\%$ in additive networks. The gains are $6.40$ and $5.56$ percentage points. They occur in all $15$ pairs, with two-sided signed-rank $p=6.1\times10^{-5}$ in both architectures.

Changing only the diagnostic feedback field raises branch-gradient cosine from $0.007$ to $0.667$ in shunting and from $0.024$ to $0.599$ in additive checkpoints. Again, every pair improves. Thus low dimensionality becomes effective when the teaching coordinate has the correct neuron-indexed spatial basis. The dendritic tree then supplies the routing map.

2. Three-, four-, and five-factor rules

The exact-transport factorial directly tests the theorem-derived rule. Across five seeds, $3F$ reaches $97.21\pm0.14\%$ with a backpropagation-updated decoder and $97.18\pm0.12\%$ with a locally updated decoder. Matched backpropagation reaches $97.03\pm0.08\%$. The corresponding $5F$ values are $97.20\pm0.16\%$ and $97.12\pm0.14\%$. Therefore, accurate feedback makes $3F$ sufficient to reach the architecture's backpropagation ceiling.

The neuron-wise experiment gives the biologically restricted intermediate result. It reaches $97.15\%$ with one teaching coordinate per modeled neuron, rather than a separately specified error at every compartment.

Within the matched archived shunting cohort, $3F$, $4F$, and $5F$ reach $89.90\%$, $90.05\%$, and $91.19\%$, respectively. These runs share feedback, decoder, optimizer, schedule, and effective parameter-group learning rates. The like-for-like $3F$ to $5F$ difference is $1.29$ points.

We also ran a current-code $2\times2$ factorial over architecture and initialization policy with $15$ paired seeds. Under analytical initialization, shunting and additive $3F$ both reach $90.92\%$, with paired $p=0.98$. Under occupancy calibration, they reach $90.99\%$ and $91.66\%$. This shows that the small cross-core difference depends on the operating point. The main result is stable across both cores: restoring neuron-indexed routing brings each to about $97.15\%$.

The factorial matches feedback, decoder, optimizer, schedule, and effective learning rates. Forward voltage states still depend on the architecture and initialization. We therefore use the within-core feedback interventions for the causal conclusion. Norm-matched one-step updates reduce held-out loss in every seed. The $5F$ residual is now defined as a bounded predictability preconditioner, rather than a confidence measure.

3. What shunting contributes

Shunting has an exact branch-selective role. For inhibitory conductance at compartment $k$,

$\displaystyle \frac{\partial\log\alpha_n}{\partial G_k^I}=-R_k$

for every descendant $n$ whose route passes through $k$. Unrelated branches have no corresponding path effect. This provides a local mechanism for regulating the strength of credit delivered to a selected subtree.

The effect on field dispersion depends on inhibitory placement. If $z=\log\alpha$ and $\beta$ is the inhibitory log-gain change, dispersion decreases when

$2\,\mathrm{Cov}(z,\beta)>\mathrm{Var}(\beta)$.

This gives a conditional answer to the effective-rank question. Shunting makes the field more compressible when inhibition preferentially attenuates routes with larger gains. It is not determined by conductance alone. It also depends on route geometry and the trained operating point.

The experiments identify several useful regimes. At $N_I=5$, path-gain CV is about $80\%$ lower under shunting in all five pairs. Under restricted $5F$ feedback, shunting is higher on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, figure-ground MNIST, and compact CIFAR-10. The respective comparisons are $91.1$ versus $89.4\%$, $83.8$ versus $83.1\%$, $80.3$ versus $78.7\%$, and $29.7$ versus $25.5\%$.

Depth and noise give stronger separations. Across one to four dendritic layers, shunting changes from $63.5$ to $57.4\%$, while additive changes from $54.9$ to $29.7\%$. Here $\sigma$ is the standard deviation of zero-mean Gaussian noise added to feedback. At $\sigma=0.1$, mean test accuracy is $61.8\%$ for shunting and $38.4\%$ for additive. At $\sigma=1.0$, it is $46.9\%$ and $11.4\%$.

A fixed-state intervention further defines the operating point. Removing inhibitory conductance only from backward resistance increases distal path gain by $7.0\%$ on MNIST and $28.1\%$ in the higher-inhibition noise population. The cosine alignment between the restricted update and exact branch gradient changes by less than $0.002$. Larger path interventions are readily detected: setting local resistance to one gives cosine $0.948$ with a $5.56$-fold norm change, while omitting parent derivatives gives cosine $0.385$. These results show that inhibition affects learning through forward voltage, local eligibility, and path gain together.

4. Geometry, active dendrites, and biological interpretation

The exact product holds for any rooted feedforward tree. It does not require a symmetric morphology. Path statistics depend on route depth, branch-specific conductance, and local transfer derivatives. In a directed acyclic graph, node error becomes a sum over parent paths. Recurrent circuits require an adjoint or implicit gradient.

All $40$ depth-sweep checkpoints retain every nominal stage. The minimum stage-median coupling is $0.716$. The depth result therefore reflects transport through longer routes.

A differentiable active nonlinearity enters the effective path gain through its local derivative. Identity-transfer and parametric-tanh controls show that the selected nonlinearity is not required for task competence. Calcium, spikes, and multi-state channels would add temporal eligibility or adjoint terms. They may amplify, gate, or retain credit over time.

Prior dendritic learning models posit apical teaching compartments, burst signals, or local targets. Our contribution is complementary. We derive the exact credit required by a conductance tree. We identify its local and transported factors. We then measure which restricted feedback structures recover it. The neuron-wise result sharpens the biological requirement from one error per compartment to one coordinate per modeled neuron. Apical, burst, plateau, and neuromodulatory signals are plausible carriers of this coordinate.

5. Presentation and reproducibility

The revision introduces the voltage equation before implementation conventions. It defines every feedback mode and all abbreviations before use. It removes unclear terms and rebuilds the figures with standalone captions and no overlapping elements.

Every diagnostic now states its axes. Covariance is computed over examples after reducing non-batch coordinates. Path-gain CV is computed over compartments within examples. SVD uses an examples-by-compartments matrix. Gradient metrics compare matched parameter vectors.

Conclusion

The new controls answer both central concerns. The $3F$ rule reaches the backpropagation ceiling when the required error structure is preserved. One coordinate per neuron recovers almost all of dense transport's benefit. Shunting provides an exact, branch-selective mechanism for regulating that credit, with clear benefits in deep, noisy, and inhibition-sensitive regimes. This mechanism suggests testable neuroscience experiments and new path-aware feedback designs for AI.

We hope the exact reconstruction, the $15$-seed routing result, the matched $3F$ controls, and the clearer presentation show that the two central technical concerns have been addressed while strengthening the conceptual contribution you identified.
