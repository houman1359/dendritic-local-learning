Thank you for recognizing the novelty of our work, which asks whether neuronal biophysics can make exact credit easier to approximate. We agree that the goal and the evidence for practical relevance should be stated directly, and we have substantially revised the presentation to make both clear.

Bottom line. This is a mechanism-first NeuroAI paper, not a claim of ImageNet-scale efficiency. It derives the exact credit equation of a conductance tree, verifies it numerically to relative error below 1.9e-7, and identifies feedback routing as the limiting component. With exact transported errors, the theorem-derived 3F rule reaches 97.18 to 97.21% on MNIST, matching backpropagation at 97.03%. With only one teaching coordinate shared across the 13 compartments of each modeled neuron, 3F reaches 97.15% in shunting and 97.14% in additive networks. On the harder compact CIFAR-10 control, exact transport raises shunting LocalCA from 29.7% to 47.2%, close to the matched 49.5% backpropagation ceiling. These are quantitative tests of the proposed credit mechanism.

1. Goal and novelty

The paper makes a direct bridge from a biophysical component to a learning computation:

1. derive the exact gradient of a conductance-based dendritic tree;
2. separate it into synapse-local eligibility and path-transported compartment error;
3. measure how E/I conductance, shunting, and branching transform that error;
4. test which restricted feedback fields preserve enough information for learning.

Other approaches such as FF, PEPITA, SPELA, Trifecta, NoProp, and LLS primarily design layer-local or forward-only objectives. Our work instead starts from the exact conductance-tree gradient and examines which factors are local and how neuronal structure changes the required teaching signal. A comparison table distinguishes feedback source, within-neuron biophysics, objective, and relation to exact credit.

For neuroscience, the framework predicts descendant-selective plasticity changes after focal shunting, set by input resistance and path geometry, and can be applied to reconstructed morphologies. For AI, it identifies neuron-indexed and path-aware feedback as structures to preserve at scale. The code implements matched cores, E/I and morphology interventions, six feedback families, and exact-gradient checks.

2. What is learned, and what shunting controls

The path gain alpha_n is not a separately learned parameter. It is the instantaneous derivative `dV_soma/dV_n`, determined by the current branch-to-parent conductances, input resistances, and local transfer derivatives. On a rooted tree it is the product of these local gains along the unique route. Backpropagation and the transported-error analysis compute it from the current state; practical LocalCA deliberately does not, allowing us to measure the cost of restricted feedback.

Shunting has an exact theoretical role. Increasing inhibitory conductance at compartment k changes every descendant path gain according to

`d log(alpha_n) / d G_k^I = -R_k`,

with no corresponding path effect on unrelated branches. This gives a biologically grounded, compartment-local control over both synaptic eligibility and descendant credit gain. It does not imply that every placement of inhibition helps; the benefit depends on the affected route and task.

The experiments provide several positive signatures. At N_I=5, conductance-stage path-gain CV is about 80% lower under shunting, with the same ordering in all five pairs and at every nonzero inhibitory count. Under restricted 5F feedback, shunting exceeds additive on MNIST (91.1% versus 89.4%), Fashion-MNIST (83.8% versus 83.1%), figure-ground MNIST (80.3% versus 78.7%), and compact CIFAR-10 (29.7% versus 25.5%). In the depth stress test, shunting changes from 63.5% to 57.4% across one to four dendritic layers, while additive degrades strongly from 54.9% to 29.7%. Here broadcast-noise sigma is the standard deviation of zero-mean Gaussian noise added to the feedback signal. At sigma=0.1, mean test accuracy is 61.8% for shunting versus 38.4% for additive; at sigma=1.0, it is 46.9% versus 11.4%, near chance for additive. These results show why shunting is worth studying: it provides a selective conductance mechanism and is useful when paths are deep, feedback is noisy, or the task is inhibition-sensitive.

A 15-seed architecture-by-initialization factorial gives equal 3F accuracy under analytical initialization, 90.92% in both cores, and a modest additive advantage under occupancy calibration. At fixed trained states, removing inhibitory conductance only from the resistance factors used for backward transport increases distal path gain by 7.0% on MNIST and 28.1% in the higher-inhibition noise population. Even with these gain changes, the cosine alignment between the restricted-feedback branch update and the exact branch gradient changes by less than 0.002. Thus the learning effects involve forward voltage, local eligibility, and path gain together, not isolated backward attenuation.

3. Three-factor learning and feedback routing

The two strongest controls show that 3F is not the limiting factor.

First, in a five-seed factorial with exact path-transported errors, 3F reaches 97.21+/-0.14% with a backprop-updated decoder and 97.18+/-0.12% with a locally updated decoder. The corresponding 5F values are 97.20+/-0.16% and 97.12+/-0.14%, and matched backpropagation is 97.03+/-0.08%. Therefore, empirical 5F preconditioning is unnecessary when the theorem's error factor is supplied accurately.

Second, dense compartment errors are not necessary in the regular tree. Replacing scalar fallback with one soma-error coordinate repeated over each modeled neuron's 13 compartments raises 3F accuracy from 90.75% to 97.15% in shunting and from 91.58% to 97.14% in additive networks. The gains of 6.40 and 5.56 points occur in all 15 pairs, with signed-rank p=6.1e-5. The corresponding branch-gradient cosine increases from 0.007 to 0.667 and from 0.024 to 0.599. This result turns the feedback question into a constructive design principle: preserve neuron identity and use the tree as a routing map.

We corrected the feedback terminology. At a stage with the same number of units, the original mode reuses the full soma teaching vector coordinate by coordinate. At a wider stage, it averages that vector to one scalar per example and broadcasts the scalar to all compartments. It is not ancestry-shared feedback, and unconstrained rank-1 SVD does not test a uniform broadcast. The revised analysis separates intrinsic geometry from implemented-field compatibility.

4. Scale and benchmark evidence

We agree that scale is important, but the current CIFAR-10 ladder already tests the mechanism on a harder input distribution. Under the same compact passive architecture, restricted shunting LocalCA reaches 29.7+/-1.3%, rank-4 feedback reaches 35.0+/-1.0%, exact transported error reaches 47.2+/-0.6%, and matched shunting backpropagation reaches 49.5+/-0.4%. The 17.5-point recovery from restricted to transported feedback, reaching within 2.3 points of the architecture's ceiling, isolates credit quality rather than forward capacity. Additive exact transport reaches 32.4+/-1.5%, providing an additional architecture control.

We added a directly implementable DFA reference on matched MNIST models. DFA reaches 66.1% in shunting, 62.2% in additive, and 51.8% in a point MLP; their respective gaps to matched backpropagation are 24.9, 27.6, and 39.5 points. This is not a comprehensive leaderboard, but it positions the mechanism beside a standard broadcast alternative without changing the within-unit question.

The next test is to insert the unit into a competitive CNN or transformer and compare feedback modes under matched augmentation and compute. In CNNs, patches provide local eligibility and shared parameters sum over positions; in transformers, dendritic units can replace feed-forward or value/output projections. Credit between blocks remains separate.

5. Compute, memory, and energy

With the same MNIST model, batch size, hooks, evaluation, and 180 epochs, LocalCA takes 190 s versus 125 s for backpropagation. Peak allocated memory is 1.70 GiB for both (2.03 versus 1.97 GiB reserved). The research implementation is feasible but not yet an efficiency method. Hardware energy was not measured.

6. Biological scope and generality

The model is a rooted conductance tree, not a complete pyramidal-cell circuit. With multiple parents, local eligibility still factorizes from node error, but the error sums over parent paths. Recurrence requires an adjoint; Hodgkin-Huxley channels, spikes, calcium, and interneurons add temporal state and eligibility traces and may change credit substantially.

The classification labels do not stand for neurotransmitters. Inhibitory neurotransmission is represented by input-driven conductance changing local input resistance, forward voltage sensitivity, and path gain. Classification and synthetic tasks are controlled probes of the resulting learning geometry. A complete biological implementation also requires a source of neuron-indexed teaching signals; apical, burst, plateau, and neuromodulatory signals are candidates rather than established mechanisms here.

The revised presentation brings the factorization, feedback distinction, principal quantitative controls, biological predictions, and limitations into the main argument. The resulting contribution is a new mechanistic framework with a validated local rule, an identified feedback bottleneck, regime-specific evidence for shunting, and a concrete path to reconstructed-neuron and larger-backbone tests.
