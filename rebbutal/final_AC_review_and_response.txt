Metareview:
This paper studies local credit assignment in dendritic branches in conductance-based neuron models. It derives that the gradient factorizes into a local eligibility term and a non-local compartment error scaled by a path gain. Eventually, the authors claim the path gain can be modulated via shunting inhibition, an observation in dendrites. The study tests its models on MNIST and CIFAR-10. Reviewers acknowledge the conceptual contribution of this paper, including reformulating the local credit assignment as an approximation problem and the usage of shunting inhibition to facilitate backpropagation.

Nevertheless, there are some serious concerns raised by reviewers, which need to be thoroughly addressed during the rebuttal and discussion period.

Technical concerns:
Optimal rank-1 approximation is not equivalent to the uniform spatial broadcast used by the algorithm.

The theoretically derived three-factor rule performs much worse than the 5F modifier, making the reviewer doubt the main claim of the paper about whether the shunting and a scalar broadcast are sufficient for local learning.

Writing clarity

================ AUTHOR RESPONSE ================

Thank you for identifying the two issues most consequential for evaluating the paper. Our additional controls change the interpretation in a useful way.

**Summary.** (i) The submitted feedback was described inaccurately: it preserves the soma vector only at a width-matched stage and otherwise falls back to one scalar. (ii) A global scalar is inadequate, but corrected neuron-wise feedback and exact path transport allow the theorem-derived 3F rule to reach the matched-backpropagation ceiling. (iii) Shunting changes path gain exactly, but does not confer a general directional or learning advantage across operating points. (iv) The resulting contribution is a clearer mechanistic bridge between dendritic biophysics and credit assignment, with testable consequences for neuroscience and concrete design principles for NeuroAI.

**1. The rank-1 objection is correct and led to an operational correction.**

Although the submitted configurations use `error_broadcast_mode: per_soma`, the code preserves the soma/core vector only when a dendritic-stage width matches it. Wider proximal and distal stages receive one scalar per example. This is a matched-width/scalar-fallback hybrid: neither a global scalar everywhere nor one soma coordinate repeated over every compartment descended from that neuron. The manuscript's “rank-1” and “per-soma” labels therefore did not accurately describe the implemented operation.

An unconstrained rank-1 SVD is also not a test of compatibility with an all-ones spatial broadcast. A stage-resolved audit further showed that our reported compatibility summaries pooled a somatic stage whose reconstruction is exact by construction and whose error-energy share differs between architectures. We withdraw the claim that shunting makes the exact error field more compatible with restricted feedback. On dendritic compartments, the submitted field is weakly aligned in both architectures and there is no reliable cross-core ordering. Unconstrained SVD is retained only as intrinsic error geometry, not as validation of the implemented feedback.

**2. The theorem-derived 3F rule is sufficient when supplied with an adequate error factor.**

The submitted top-of-sweep comparison that suggested an approximately 30-point 3F-to-5F deficit mixed heterogeneous configurations and should not support a rule claim. After matching feedback, decoder, optimizer, schedule, and effective parameter-group rates within the submitted-code shunting cohort, 3F reaches 89.90+/-1.13% and 5F 91.19+/-0.49% (three seeds). We treat this archived cohort as descriptive: it predates the current gate-calibration machinery and is not a matched cross-core comparison with the fifteen-seed policy factorial below.

Two stronger controls answer the blocker directly:

- In a five-seed exact-transport factorial, 3F reaches 97.21+/-0.14% with a backprop-updated decoder and 97.18+/-0.12% with a locally updated decoder, versus matched backpropagation at 97.03+/-0.08%. The corresponding 5F values are 97.20+/-0.16% and 97.12+/-0.14%. Thus 5F is not required by the theorem or by learning with the exact path-transported error; it is an empirical bounded preconditioner used in some approximate-feedback regimes.
- In a fifteen-seed control, one soma coordinate is repeated over the 13 compartments of that modeled neuron's regular tree. Replacing scalar fallback with this neuron-wise field raises 3F accuracy from 90.75% to 97.15% in shunting models and from 91.58% to 97.14% in additive models. The gains are 6.40 and 5.56 points, occur in all 15 pairs, and have two-sided signed-rank p=6.1e-5 in both architectures. At the corrected-feedback checkpoints, substituting only the diagnostic field raises branch-gradient cosine from 0.007 to 0.667 and from 0.024 to 0.599, again in every pair.

These results locate the submitted 3F gap in feedback construction rather than in the local eligibility factorization. They also refine the biological requirement: exact compartment-wise teaching signals are unnecessary in this regular-tree test, but neuron identity must be preserved.

**3. The shunting claim is now positive but bounded.**

The theoretical statement is local and exact. Increasing inhibitory conductance at compartment k changes the conductance-stage path gain of a descendant n according to

`d log(alpha_n) / d G_k^I = -R_k`,

and has no such path effect on unrelated branches. Shunting therefore provides a branch-selective gain mechanism; it does not automatically minimize rank or improve learning. Compression requires inhibitory attenuation to covary appropriately with the original path-gain field.

The empirical controls support the same boundary. Conductance-stage path-gain CV is lower under shunting in all five matched pairs at the reported operating point, but a fresh five-seed cohort does not reproduce a general gradient-direction advantage. A current-code 2x2 factorial crossing architecture with explicit initialization policy over fifteen paired seeds finds equal 3F accuracy under analytical initialization (90.92% in both cores) and a modest additive advantage under occupancy calibration (90.99% shunting vs. 91.66% additive). We therefore make no general 3F shunting-advantage claim.

A fixed-forward-state dose response explains why isolated backward attenuation is not the operative explanation at the trained states. Removing all inhibitory conductance only from backward parent-resistance factors increases distal gain by 1.070x on MNIST and 1.281x in the higher-inhibition noise population, while branch-gradient cosine changes by less than 0.002. The diagnostic does respond to larger path interventions: setting local resistance to one gives cosine 0.948 with a 5.56x norm change, and omitting parent transfer derivatives gives 0.385. Thus inhibition measurably regulates path gain, but forward voltage, local eligibility, complete path derivatives, and feedback identity jointly determine the update.

The supported learning evidence is consequently regime-specific: shunting is useful under the submitted restricted 5F setting on the reported tasks and degrades more gracefully in the depth and feedback-noise stress tests, while corrected neuron-wise feedback removes the cross-core difference on MNIST. This is a mechanistic boundary, not a universal architecture ranking.

**4. Novelty, neuroscience relevance, and applications.**

The contribution is not another heuristic replacement for backpropagation. We first define the exact credit object for a conductance-based dendritic tree and then ask which parts are locally available, which require a teaching signal, and how conductance and branching transform that signal. This makes neuronal biophysics experimentally relevant to the learning computation rather than using dendrites only as additional forward-model capacity.

The framework yields direct neuroscience predictions: a focal shunt should preferentially modulate plasticity signals at descendant synapses; its magnitude should scale with local input resistance; branch position and morphology should determine which synapses share modulation; and attenuation can help or hurt depending on whether the affected path is task-relevant. These predictions connect compartment-level voltage, inhibition, morphology, and plasticity measurements. The same analysis can be applied to reconstructed morphologies with fitted conductances to compare credit-field transport across cells or cell types.

For AI, the work supplies a design principle rather than a current efficiency claim: structured within-unit conductance can regulate local credit, while neuron-indexed or path-aware feedback preserves the identity lost by a global scalar. The accompanying research code supports matched additive/conductance cores, E/I and morphology interventions, 3F/4F/5F rules, six feedback families, exact-gradient reconstruction, and controlled task sweeps. This enables hypothesis-driven evaluation before inserting such units into CNN or transformer backbones. We report that the present diagnostic implementation is about 1.5x slower than matched backpropagation and has no measured memory advantage; large-scale efficiency remains future work.

**5. Presentation and scope.**

The working revision defines every feedback object and metric axis, introduces the voltage equation before implementation conventions, removes unresolved abbreviations and unusual terminology, rebuilds the figures and captions, and brings the central results, assumptions, limitations, and biological predictions into the main argument. It explicitly distinguishes the theorem from 4F/5F preconditioning, transported-error oracles, decoder choices, and auxiliary objectives.

The corrected central claim is therefore: exact dendritic gradients factor into synapse-local eligibility and path-transported compartment error; feedback construction is the practical bottleneck in these experiments; and shunting provides a principled way to regulate credit paths without guaranteeing a general learning advantage. We believe this resolves both technical blockers while preserving—and sharpening—the conceptual NeuroAI contribution recognized across the reviews.
