Summary:
This paper studies local credit assignment (LocalCA) in a tree-structured conductance-based model. The main insight of the paper is that for the considered tree-structured graph the gradient of the loss wrt to the local synaptic conductances factorises into a term that the authors call local eligibility (product of presynaptic activity, total input resistance of compartment, and the difference between the synaptic reversal potential and current compartment voltage (the driving force)) and a non-local compartment error. This compartment error is the somatic error scaled by a "path gain" through the tree. The authors propose that shunting inhibition multiplicatively modulates these path gains, which sometimes makes the exact error field more compressible and therefore easier to approximate with a low-bandwidth broadcast.

The main contribution made by this submission is the reinterpretation of local credit assignment as an approximation problem. More precisely, the authors propose that a rank-1 broadcast can replace the exact compartment-specific field. The authors report that shunting inhibition reduces path-gain dispersion, makes exact compartment errors more low-rank, improves LocalCA-to-back-propagation alignment, and improves local-learning performance relative to an additive E/I control.

Significance. The main contribution of the paper is conceptual. It proposes mechanism by which dendritic biophysics could alter credit assignment. Rather than treating inhibition only as a way to suppress neural activity, the paper proposess that conductance-based inhibition changes local input resistance, therefore changes both local synaptic sensitivity and the gain with which errors from distal branches transport toward the soma. This implies that inhibition can select which dendritic compartments receive strong versus weak credit signals. This is an intresting contribution regarding the biological credit assignment problem.

Contribution Type: General: Most submissions will fall into this type.

Strengths And Weaknesses:

Strengths

I find the factorisation of the loss an intresting contribution

The implemented experiments aim to distinguish the underlying mechanism by measuring gradient reconstruction, local-to-back-propagation alignment, norm mismatch, path-gain coefficient of variation, error-matrix singular values, inhibitory interventions, morphology dependence, and feedback-bandwidth dependence.

Weaknesses

Optimal rank-1 approximation is not equivalent to the uniform spatial broadcast used by the algorithm. The approximation assumes scalar broadcast (the rank-1 approximation to have an all one spatial vector), while the experiments test for rank-1 compressibility, which is not the same, because in a general rank-1 approximation one vector is not necessarily uniform, neither can it always be transformed into one.

The result is established for a directed feedforward conductance tree.

The more well performing results depend on the empirical 5F modifier, while the theorem-derived 3F rule performs much worse. Thus I would say that the paper does not show (yet) that shunting plus a scalar broadcast are sufficient for local learning. It rather shows that this combination is useful with additional empirically chosen local preconditioning.

Minor

There is some overlap of text and other objects/arrows in some figures of the paper.

Please explain why the residual-variance factor should be considered as a confidence measure.

The abstract mentions abbreviations (LocalCA, 5F etc) that are not resolved before, so the reader has no idea what these mean, without reading the full text.

Figure 1 caption: "gold-ringed soma" I think with this phrase the authors refer to the schematic illustrating soma with a yellow/gold ring. However, I find the word "gold-ringed" odd, while the sentence as it is structured, does not indicate that the authors refer to the illustration.

Phrase "synapse banks" sounds odd to me, not sure whether it is often used.

I would start first with introducing the equation 1 and then writing the paragraph currently in lines 78 - 105 that mentions the scaling of parameters etc. Otherwise the reader encounters objects/variables without having a concrete idea yet how these relate or fit together in your model. Current version first describes how equations behave, and then reports the equations which is uncomfortable to read, because the reader has to skim through the text to find the relations you are referring to.

AUTHORS' RESPONSE
======================

Thank you for the careful review. Your rank-1 objection revealed an important mismatch between our description and the submitted operation.

**Summary.** The submitted feedback is a matched-width/scalar-fallback hybrid, and a strict global scalar is inadequate. Corrected neuron-wise feedback and exact path transport let the theorem-derived 3F rule reach the matched-architecture backpropagation reference, showing that feedback construction—not local eligibility—is the principal bottleneck. An architecture-by-initialization factorial finds no 3F core difference under analytical initialization and a modest additive advantage under occupancy calibration; we therefore infer no general shunting advantage. We narrow the inhibition claim to the exact, useful statement that shunting regulates path gain but does not guarantee faithful credit.

**1. Constrained feedback form and correction of the headline condition**

We agree that an unconstrained SVD rank-1 approximation need not have an all-ones spatial vector and does not establish compatibility with a uniform scalar broadcast.

`error_broadcast_mode: per_soma` preserves the soma/core vector only when the dendritic-stage width matches it; wider stages fall back to one scalar per example. It is therefore a matched-width/scalar-fallback hybrid, neither a global scalar everywhere nor one soma coordinate repeated over a neuron's compartments. The “rank-1” and “per-soma” descriptions were inaccurate.

A stage-resolved audit showed that the reported constrained reconstruction and actual-field cosine pooled a somatic stage whose reconstruction is exact by construction and which holds unequal error energy across architectures. On dendritic compartments, after one optimal global rescaling, the actual submitted field captures only 2.3% of exact-error energy in shunting and 0.6% in additive checkpoints (five seeds, three diagnostic splits; descriptive). We therefore withdraw the claim that shunting creates a more compatible restricted field; there is no reliable cross-core conclusion. Unconstrained rank-1 SVD is retained only as intrinsic geometry. The fifteen-seed result below instead attributes improvement to feedback construction, not shunting-specific compression.

**2. Three-, four-, and five-factor rules**

You are also right that the submitted rule comparison was not fair. The approximately 62% 3F value mixed configurations and should not have been compared with the selected 5F condition. We withdraw it. In the configuration-matched submitted-code shunting cohort, 3F, 4F, and 5F reach 89.90%, 90.05%, and 91.19% (three seeds; descriptive). The small cross-core sign changes with learning rate, so it is not architecture evidence. In the current fifteen-seed matched-width/scalar-fallback cohort, 3F reaches 90.75% shunting and 91.58% additive; the explicit policy factorial below is the valid causal comparison. We do not have a matched additive 4F/5F factorial and therefore make no unsupported cross-core claim for those rules.

We then crossed architecture and explicit initialization policy in current code over fifteen paired seeds. With analytical initialization, shunting and additive are identical (90.92+/-0.42% vs. 90.92+/-0.51%; paired p=0.98). Occupancy calibration improves additive by 0.74 points but not shunting, producing 90.99+/-0.59% vs. 91.66+/-0.79% (shunting/additive; paired p=0.022, signed-rank p=0.035) and an architecture-by-policy interaction (p=0.036; signed-rank p=0.041). Thus the cross-core sign is policy-dependent, with no general 3F shunting advantage.

We reanalyzed matched 3F gradients on train/validation/test batches, averaging splits within checkpoint. At the three submitted checkpoint pairs, non-somatic weighted cosine is 0.160 shunting vs. 0.030 additive, equal-block cosine 0.238 vs. 0.042, and local/exact norm ratio 0.261 vs. 0.097; all three pairs have the same ordering, but n=3 is descriptive. Whole-model concatenation reverses (0.096 vs. 0.350) because an exactly aligned soma block contains 13.1% of additive exact-gradient energy but 0.2% of shunting energy. A fresh same-code five-seed cohort does not reproduce the branch-direction contrast (0.155 vs. 0.164), so we treat it as operating-point-specific rather than general.

Post-hoc calibration to common activation quantiles leaves branch cosine higher under shunting but changes predictions, so it is sensitivity evidence. In a stricter fixed-state decomposition, recorded forward variables and exact teaching signals are held fixed: omitting parent transfer derivatives reduces branch cosine to 0.385, while the submitted feedback gives 0.190. Thus Fig. 3A's lower conductance-stage path-gain CV is robust but is insufficient for final gradient alignment: feedback identity and the complete effective path matter.

A five-seed exact-transport factorial separates the theorem from preconditioning. With exact compartment errors, 3F reaches 97.21+/-0.14% with a backprop-updated decoder and 97.18+/-0.12% with a local decoder, versus matched backprop at 97.03+/-0.08%; 5F reaches 97.20+/-0.16% and 97.12+/-0.14%. Thus theorem-derived eligibility is sufficient with its exact error factor; 5F is an empirical preconditioner for approximate feedback, not part of the theorem.

Most directly, a fifteen-seed control repeats one soma coordinate over that neuron's compartments. Replacing scalar fallback raises 3F MNIST from 90.75% to 97.15% in shunting and 91.58% to 97.14% in additive networks. The paired gains (6.40/5.56 points) occur in all 15 pairs (signed-rank p=6.1e-5). At corrected-feedback checkpoints, changing only the field raises branch cosine from 0.007 to 0.667 and from 0.024 to 0.599, again in all pairs. This locates the submitted gap in feedback construction, not eligibility. It is a regular-tree control; within-architecture substitutions are causal and cross-architecture contrasts descriptive.

The 5F residual-variance factor is not statistical confidence. It is a bounded heuristic preconditioner: lower residual variance means a scalar parent/soma proxy predicts more branch variance in the online ridge model. The working revision calls it a predictability factor, defines its axes, and makes no confidence interpretation.

**3. What inhibition does and does not guarantee**

The exact theoretical statement is local, not an effective-rank minimization theorem. For inhibitory conductance G_k^I on a path, d log(alpha_n)/dG_k^I = -R_k for descendants of k. Thus inhibition attenuates affected paths multiplicatively. Whether this reduces dispersion or reconstruction error depends on how attenuation covaries with the original gains. In log-gain form, compression requires 2 Cov(z,beta) > Var(beta); it is not automatic. A five-seed backward-only dose response reinforces this boundary: full removal changes distal gain by 1.070x on MNIST and 1.281x in the higher-inhibition noise population, but branch cosine changes by less than 0.002. This is not an insensitive diagnostic: unit local resistance gives cosine 0.948 with a 5.56x norm change, while omitting parent transfer derivatives gives 0.385. At the trained operating points, inhibition's isolated backward contribution is therefore too small to move credit direction; visible changes require nonphysical over-removal with clipping. The working revision removes language implying that inhibition minimizes rank.

**4. Scope, geometry, and relation to prior work**

The product assumes a rooted, steady-state tree; DAGs need a path sum and cycles an adjoint. Path statistics depend on the number and gains of edges along each route, and the derivation permits asymmetric trees; reconstructed cells with fitted conductances remain future work. Unlike approaches that posit teaching compartments or local targets, we derive the exact credit object and then test its approximability. This derivation-to-intervention chain is the novelty: in neuroscience it yields descendant-specific shunting predictions and per-cell morphology diagnostics; in AI it identifies neuron-indexed and path-aware feedback as structured learning primitives. Somatic error remains assumed, but the neuron-wise result sharpens the requirement to one coordinate shared within a modeled neuron, not one signal per compartment. Apical, burst, plateau, and neuromodulatory signals are candidates, not mechanisms established here.

**5. Active nonlinearities**

A differentiable nonlinearity contributes its derivative to path gain. In a fifteen-seed identity-transfer control, shunting improves gradient direction while additive learning is slightly better; therefore the derivative difference is not necessary for the directional effect, and direction alone does not guarantee learning. Calcium and channel dynamics remain outside scope.

**6. Metrics and presentation**

The working revision defines every axis: covariance is over examples after reducing each recorded layer/soma tensor over non-batch coordinates; path-gain CV is over compartments within an example and then summarized over examples/checkpoints; SVD uses an examples-by-compartments matrix; and gradient cosine/norm compare matched parameter vectors. It introduces Eq. 1 before its conventions, removes unresolved abbreviations and unusual phrases, and rebuilds the overlapping figures and captions.

The corrected claim is: exact gradients factor into local eligibility and path-transported error; feedback construction is the practical bottleneck; and shunting changes path gain but provides no general learning or directional advantage across operating points.
