1. Optimal rank-1 is not a uniform broadcast

We agree and have corrected this point. The implemented `per_soma` alias preserves the soma/core vector only at a width-matched stage and falls back to one scalar per example at wider stages. It is now described as matched-width/scalar-fallback feedback. It is neither global scalar everywhere nor one soma coordinate repeated over all descendants of that neuron.

An unconstrained rank-1 SVD likewise does not test compatibility with an all-ones spatial vector. Stage-resolved analysis showed that the earlier pooled field metrics included a width-matched stage whose reconstruction is exact by construction and whose error-energy share differs across architectures. On dendritic compartments, after optimal global rescaling, the implemented field captures only 2.3% of exact-error energy in shunting and 0.6% in additive checkpoints, with no reliable cross-core ordering. We therefore retain unrestricted SVD only as descriptive intrinsic geometry and make no shunting-specific compatibility claim from it.

The correction produces a stronger positive result. When one soma coordinate is repeated over each modeled neuron's 13 compartments, 3F MNIST accuracy rises from 90.75% to 97.15% in shunting and from 91.58% to 97.14% in additive networks. The paired gains are 6.40 and 5.56 percentage points, occur in all 15 pairs, and have two-sided signed-rank p=6.1e-5. At these checkpoints, substituting only the diagnostic feedback field raises branch-gradient cosine from 0.007 to 0.667 and from 0.024 to 0.599, again in all pairs.

Thus the relevant constrained family is not an arbitrary rank-1 field or a global scalar. It is a low-dimensional neuron-indexed field whose spatial routing is supplied by dendritic ancestry. One teaching coordinate serves 13 compartments and recovers almost all of the learning obtained with dense path transport.

2. The 3F rule and the role of 5F

The exact-transport factorial directly answers whether the theorem-derived 3F rule can learn. Across five seeds, 3F reaches 97.21+/-0.14% with a backprop-updated decoder and 97.18+/-0.12% with a locally updated decoder. Matched backpropagation reaches 97.03+/-0.08%. The corresponding 5F results are 97.20+/-0.16% and 97.12+/-0.14%. Thus 5F is not required by the theorem or by learning when the exact error factor is supplied.

The 15-seed neuron-wise result above is the more biologically restricted intermediate case. It shows that independently specifying every compartment error is also unnecessary: preserving neuron identity is enough to reach 97.15% in both architectures.

The apparent approximately 30-point 3F-to-5F deficit came from a heterogeneous top-of-sweep comparison. After matching feedback, decoder, optimizer, schedule, and effective parameter-group rates within the archived shunting cohort, 3F, 4F, and 5F reach 89.90%, 90.05%, and 91.19% (three seeds, descriptive). The like-for-like 3F-to-5F difference is therefore 1.29 points, not approximately 30 points. We now present 5F as a bounded empirical predictability preconditioner used in approximate-feedback regimes, separate from the theorem.

For a current-code cross-core comparison, we crossed architecture and explicit initialization policy over 15 paired seeds. Under analytical initialization, 3F accuracy is identical at 90.92% in both cores. Under occupancy calibration, additive is modestly higher, 91.66% versus 90.99%, with an architecture-by-policy interaction. We interpret cross-core 3F accuracy as operating-point-dependent and do not claim a universal shunting advantage.

Together, the results give a constructive answer to both blockers: local eligibility is sufficient, 5F is not necessary when feedback is adequate, and the practical limitation is the spatial organization of the teaching signal.

3. Positive and bounded evidence for shunting

The exact theoretical statement is local and causal. Increasing inhibitory conductance at compartment k changes the conductance-stage path gain of every descendant n according to

`d log(alpha_n) / d G_k^I = -R_k`,

with no corresponding path effect on unrelated branches. Shunting therefore supplies branch-selective control of credit gain. Whether this improves a task depends on the route and operating point; it is not an automatic rank-minimization theorem.

Several positive empirical signatures survive all scope corrections. At N_I=5, conductance-stage path-gain CV is about 80% lower under shunting, with the same ordering in all five pairs and at every nonzero inhibitory count. Under the restricted 5F condition, shunting exceeds additive on MNIST (91.1% versus 89.4%), Fashion-MNIST (83.8% versus 83.1%), figure-ground MNIST (80.3% versus 78.7%), and compact CIFAR-10 (29.7% versus 25.5%). Exact transport raises shunting CIFAR-10 to 47.2%, close to its 49.5% matched-backpropagation ceiling, while additive transport reaches 32.4%. Across one to four dendritic layers, shunting changes from 63.5% to 57.4%, whereas additive changes from 54.9% to 29.7%. Shunting is also stable through broadcast noise sigma=0.1, while additive reaches chance at sigma=1.0.

The fixed-state analysis clarifies why the advantage is regime-specific. Removing inhibition only from backward resistance increases distal gain by 1.070x on MNIST and 1.281x in the higher-inhibition noise population, while branch cosine changes by less than 0.002. The diagnostic responds strongly to larger path perturbations: unit local resistance gives cosine 0.948 with a 5.56x norm change, and omitting parent transfer derivatives gives 0.385. At the trained states, inhibition influences learning through its combined effects on forward voltage, local eligibility, and path gain rather than isolated backward attenuation.

This refined claim is scientifically useful: shunting provides a precise spatial control mechanism and measurable benefits when paths are deep, feedback is noisy, or inhibition is task-relevant, without implying that it must outperform an additive model in every matched setting.

4. Novelty and neuroscience relevance

This is not another heuristic local objective. We first derive the exact credit object for a conductance-based dendritic tree and then determine which terms are synapse-local, which require a teaching signal, and how conductance and branching transform that signal. Exact reconstruction, fixed-state interventions, feedback substitutions, and task learning test each link in this chain.

The framework yields direct neuroscience predictions. A focal shunt should preferentially modulate plasticity signals at descendant synapses; its magnitude should scale with local input resistance; proximal and distal placement should affect different route families; and the same perturbation can help or hurt depending on task relevance. The analysis can be applied to reconstructed morphologies with measured or fitted conductances to compare cells or cell types.

For NeuroAI, the 15-seed result provides a concrete design principle: a global scalar discards essential identity, whereas neuron-indexed or path-aware feedback can support near-backprop learning with far fewer independently specified signals than dense compartment feedback. The research code supports matched additive/conductance cores, E/I and morphology interventions, 3F/4F/5F rules, six feedback families, exact-gradient reconstruction, and reproducible task sweeps.
