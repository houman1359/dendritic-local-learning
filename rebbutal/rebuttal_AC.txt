Thank you for identifying the two technical issues most consequential for evaluating the paper.

**Summary.** (i) The submitted feedback label was inaccurate: the implementation preserves the soma vector only at a width-matched stage and otherwise falls back to one scalar. (ii) A strict global scalar is inadequate, while corrected neuron-wise feedback and exact path transport allow the theorem-derived 3F rule to reach the matched backpropagation ceiling. (iii) An explicit architecture-by-initialization factorial finds no 3F core difference under analytical initialization and a modest additive advantage under occupancy calibration, so we do not infer a general shunting advantage. (iv) We narrow the shunting claim to the path-gain and task regimes directly supported by the controls below.

**1. Operational feedback clarification.** Although configurations use `error_broadcast_mode: per_soma`, code preserves the soma/core vector only at a dendritic stage whose width matches it; wider proximal/distal stages fall back to one scalar per example. The condition is therefore a matched-width/scalar-fallback hybrid, not a global scalar everywhere and not one soma coordinate repeated over all compartments of a neuron. The manuscript's “rank-1” and “per-soma” labels did not describe this operation accurately, and the reviewer is correct that unconstrained rank-1 SVD does not establish compatibility with an all-ones broadcast.

A stage-resolved audit showed that our reported error-field compatibility metrics pooled a somatic stage whose reconstruction is exact by construction and which holds unequal error energy across architectures. We withdraw the claim that the shunting error field is more compatible with restricted feedback; on dendritic compartments there is no reliable cross-core ordering. This is consistent with our fifteen-seed learning result, where neuron-wise 3F reaches 97.15% in shunting and 97.14% in additive networks. We attribute that improvement to feedback construction, not to shunting-specific error compression.

**2. The submitted 3F deficit was not a failure of the theorem-derived eligibility.** The submitted top-of-sweep average combined heterogeneous configurations and produced the misleading impression that 3F was roughly 30 points below 5F. A configuration-matched analysis within the submitted-code cohort instead places shunting 3F only 1.29 points below shunting 5F (three seeds). Because this small cohort predates the current gate-initialization machinery, while the current fifteen-seed cohort applies occupancy-quantile calibration to additive models and analytical initialization to shunting models, the two cohorts are not a replication pair. We therefore do not infer a general cross-architecture 3F learning advantage in either direction from them.

We resolved this directly with a current-code 2x2 factorial crossing architecture and explicit initialization policy over fifteen paired seeds. Under analytical initialization, shunting and additive are identical (90.92+/-0.42% vs. 90.92+/-0.51%; paired p=0.98). Occupancy calibration improves additive by 0.74 points but not shunting, yielding 90.99+/-0.59% vs. 91.66+/-0.79% (shunting/additive; paired p=0.022, signed-rank p=0.035); the architecture-by-policy interaction is also supported (p=0.036, signed-rank p=0.041). Thus the cross-core sign depends on the operating policy, and neither cohort supports a general 3F shunting advantage.

We evaluated matched 3F gradients on train/validation/test batches, averaging splits within checkpoint and pairing seeds. At non-somatic dendritic parameters, parameter-count-weighted cosine is 0.160 shunting vs. 0.030 additive, equal-block macro cosine is 0.238 vs. 0.042, and the local/exact norm ratio is 0.261 vs. 0.097. The ordering holds in all three pairs but is descriptive at this sample size. The weighted metric is equivalent to concatenating blocks after separately normalizing each to equal RMS, preventing one high-energy tensor from determining the comparison. The earlier whole-model reversal (0.096 vs. 0.350) is caused by an exactly aligned soma block carrying 13.1% of additive exact-gradient energy but 0.2% of shunting energy. Whole concatenation therefore conflates branch direction with architecture-dependent energy allocation.

However, a fresh same-code five-seed scalar-fallback cohort does not reproduce the directional contrast: weighted branch cosine is 0.155 shunting vs. 0.164 additive, and concatenated branch cosine is 0.143 vs. 0.134. We therefore treat the submitted three-pair result as descriptive and operating-point-specific.

In a separate fifteen-seed identity-transfer control, the branch transfer derivative is exactly one in both architectures. Shunting has higher block-weighted (0.190 vs. 0.111, p=1.2e-6) and concatenated (0.152 vs. 0.109, p=1.0e-4) 3F cosine, but additive test accuracy is slightly higher (90.93% vs. 90.36%, p<0.001). Thus the derivative difference is not required for the directional effect, but direction alone does not yield a learning advantage; forward-voltage and update-scale distributions remain unmatched.

Activation derivatives also differ across architectures. In a five-seed backward-only dose response, we held recorded voltages, derivatives, couplings, soma errors, and eligibility fixed. Full removal of inhibition from error transport increased distal gain by 1.070x on MNIST and 1.281x in noise-resilience population 0, yet submitted-feedback branch cosine changed only 0.1422->0.1426 and 0.0653->0.0649; neuron-wise cosine changed 0.4957->0.4968 and 0.1748->0.1761. This is a measured operating-point boundary: inhibition changes backward gain, but its isolated contribution is too small to move credit direction at the trained states. The diagnostic is sensitive to larger path interventions: unit local resistance gives cosine 0.948 with a 5.56x norm change, omitting parent transfer derivatives gives 0.385, and submitted feedback gives 0.190. Thus Fig. 3A's lower conductance-stage path-gain variability is robust but insufficient for end-to-end alignment; the complete path and feedback identity matter.

We also norm-matched the submitted and exact branch updates and measured one-step loss change. Both architectures give descent in all five seeds over the stable finite-step range, but shunting has no reliable advantage at any tested step. This rules out global update scale as the explanation for the non-replicating directional contrast.

A post-hoc operating-point sensitivity recalibrates every trained gate so its 10th/90th voltage quantiles map to common activation targets. Branch cosine remains higher for shunting, but this changes predictions and does not equalize the full derivative distribution, so we treat it as sensitivity evidence rather than a clamped causal test.

A new five-seed factorial gives a cleaner separation. With exact path-transported compartment errors, 3F reaches 97.21+/-0.14% with a backprop-updated decoder and 97.18+/-0.12% with a locally updated decoder, compared with a matched backprop ceiling of 97.03+/-0.08%. The corresponding 5F results are 97.20+/-0.16% and 97.12+/-0.14%. Therefore 5F is not required by the theorem or by learning with exact transported errors; it is an empirical bounded preconditioner that helps in some approximate-feedback regimes.

A fifteen-seed factorial implements the neuron-wise feedback operation that the submitted `per_soma` label had implied: one soma coordinate is repeated over the compartments of that modeled neuron. Replacing scalar fallback by corrected feedback raises 3F MNIST from 90.75+/-1.01% to 97.15+/-0.12% in shunting and from 91.58+/-0.61% to 97.14+/-0.10% in additive models. The paired gains are 6.40/5.56 points, occur in all 15 pairs, and have p=1.3e-12/1.3e-14 (signed-rank p=6.1e-5 for both). At the corrected-feedback checkpoints, changing only the diagnostic field raises branch cosine from 0.007 to 0.667 and 0.024 to 0.599, again in all 15 pairs. This locates the submitted 3F gap in feedback implementation rather than eligibility. It is a regular-tree feedback control, not a reconstructed-morphology claim; feedback substitutions within each core are causal, whereas the cross-core contrast is descriptive.

The corrected central claim is: exact gradients factor into local eligibility and path-transported error; the practical bottleneck is constructing an adequate neuron- and compartment-level feedback field; and shunting changes path gain but does not confer a general learning or directional advantage across operating points. We do not claim that one global scalar is sufficient, inhibition always helps, or inhibition theoretically minimizes rank.

It defines all feedback objects and metric axes; reorganizes the theory-to-experiment chain; replaces overlapping figures and unresolved abbreviations; moves limitations and biological predictions into the main discussion; and adds constrained-feedback, layer-soma, exact-transport, activation, additive-gain, compute, and reproducibility controls.
