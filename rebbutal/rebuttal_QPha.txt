**1. Constrained feedback form and correction of the headline condition**

We agree that an unconstrained SVD rank-1 approximation need not have an all-ones spatial vector and does not establish compatibility with a uniform scalar broadcast.

`error_broadcast_mode: per_soma` preserves the soma/core vector only when the dendritic-stage width matches it; wider stages fall back to one scalar per example. It is therefore a matched-width/scalar-fallback hybrid, neither a global scalar everywhere nor one soma coordinate repeated over a neuron's compartments. The “rank-1” and “per-soma” descriptions were inaccurate.

A stage-resolved audit showed that the reported constrained reconstruction and actual-field cosine pooled a somatic stage whose reconstruction is exact by construction and which holds unequal error energy across architectures. We therefore withdraw the claim that the shunting error field is more compatible with restricted feedback; on dendritic compartments there is no reliable cross-core ordering. Unconstrained rank-1 SVD is retained only as intrinsic geometry and is not used to validate the implemented broadcast. This is consistent with the fifteen-seed learning result below: neuron-wise 3F reaches 97.15% in shunting and 97.14% in additive networks. We attribute that improvement to feedback construction, not shunting-specific error compression.

**2. Three-, four-, and five-factor rules**

You are also right that the submitted rule comparison was not fair. The approximately 62% 3F value mixed configurations and should not have been compared with the selected 5F condition. We withdraw that comparison. A small submitted-code reanalysis places shunting 3F only 1.29 points below shunting 5F, but its cross-core sign changes with learning rate, so we do not use its three-seed architecture contrast. In the current fifteen-seed matched-width/scalar-fallback cohort, 3F reaches 90.75+/-1.01% shunting and 91.58+/-0.61% additive. Because the standard aliases also use different gate-initialization policies, this is descriptive; the explicit policy factorial below is the causal comparison.

We then crossed architecture and explicit initialization policy in current code over fifteen paired seeds. With analytical initialization, shunting and additive are identical (90.92+/-0.42% vs. 90.92+/-0.51%; paired p=0.98). Occupancy calibration improves additive by 0.74 points but not shunting, producing 90.99+/-0.59% vs. 91.66+/-0.79% (shunting/additive; paired p=0.022, signed-rank p=0.035) and an architecture-by-policy interaction (p=0.036; signed-rank p=0.041). Thus the cross-core sign is policy-dependent, with no general 3F shunting advantage.

We investigated matched 3F gradients on train, validation, and test batches of all three paired checkpoints, averaging splits before pairing seeds. The negative whole-model cosine was an aggregation artifact:

The ordering holds in all three pairs but is descriptive at this sample size. Each block is normalized to equal RMS before concatenation, preventing one high-energy tensor from determining the result.

We also ran a fresh same-code five-seed scalar-fallback cohort and averaged train/validation/test diagnostics within seed. It does not reproduce the directional contrast: weighted branch cosine is 0.155 shunting vs. 0.164 additive, and concatenated branch cosine is 0.143 vs. 0.134. We therefore treat the three submitted checkpoints as an operating-point-specific descriptive result, not evidence of a general directional advantage.

Post-hoc calibration to common activation quantiles leaves branch cosine higher under shunting but changes predictions, so it is sensitivity evidence. In a stricter fixed-state decomposition, recorded forward variables and exact teaching signals are held fixed: omitting parent transfer derivatives reduces branch cosine to 0.385, while the submitted feedback gives 0.190. Thus Fig. 3A's lower conductance-stage path-gain CV is robust but is insufficient for final gradient alignment: feedback identity and the complete effective path matter.

A five-seed exact-transport factorial separates the theorem from preconditioning. With exact compartment errors, 3F reaches 97.21+/-0.14% with a backprop-updated decoder and 97.18+/-0.12% with a local decoder, versus matched backprop at 97.03+/-0.08%; 5F reaches 97.20+/-0.16% and 97.12+/-0.14%. Thus theorem-derived eligibility is sufficient with its exact error factor; 5F is an empirical preconditioner for approximate feedback, not part of the theorem.

Most directly, a fifteen-seed control repeats one soma coordinate over that neuron's compartments. Replacing scalar fallback raises 3F MNIST from 90.75% to 97.15% in shunting and 91.58% to 97.14% in additive networks. This locates the submitted gap in feedback construction, not eligibility. The 5F residual-variance factor is not statistical confidence. It is a bounded heuristic preconditioner: lower residual variance means a scalar parent/soma proxy predicts more branch variance in the online ridge model. **3. What inhibition does and does not guarantee**

The exact theoretical statement is local, not an effective-rank minimization theorem. For inhibitory conductance G_k^I on a path, d log(alpha_n)/dG_k^I = -R_k for descendants of k. Thus inhibition attenuates affected paths multiplicatively. Whether this reduces dispersion or reconstruction error depends on how attenuation covaries with the original gains. In log-gain form, compression requires 2 Cov(z,beta) > Var(beta); it is not automatic. A five-seed backward-only dose response reinforces this boundary: full removal changes distal gain by 1.070x on MNIST and 1.281x in the higher-inhibition noise population, but branch cosine changes by less than 0.002. This is not an insensitive diagnostic: unit local resistance gives cosine 0.948 with a 5.56x norm change, while omitting parent transfer derivatives gives 0.385. At the trained operating points, inhibition's isolated backward contribution is therefore too small to move credit direction; visible changes require nonphysical over-removal with clipping. The working revision removes language implying that inhibition minimizes rank.

**4. Scope and relation to prior work**

The product assumes a rooted, steady-state tree; DAGs need a path sum and cycles an adjoint. Controlled trees allow asymmetric geometry, but reconstructed cells remain future work. Unlike models that posit teaching compartments, we derive the exact credit object and test its approximability. Somatic error remains assumed, but the neuron-wise result sharpens the requirement: one neuron-indexed coordinate can be shared within the modeled cell rather than requiring an independent signal at every compartment. Apical, burst, plateau, and neuromodulatory signals are candidates, not mechanisms established here.

**5. Active nonlinearities**

A differentiable nonlinearity contributes its derivative to path gain. In a fifteen-seed identity-transfer control, shunting improves gradient direction while additive learning is slightly better; therefore the derivative difference is not necessary for the directional effect, and direction alone does not guarantee learning. Calcium and channel dynamics remain outside scope.

The corrected claim is: exact gradients factor into local eligibility and path-transported error; feedback construction is the practical bottleneck; and shunting changes path gain but provides no general learning or directional advantage across operating points.
