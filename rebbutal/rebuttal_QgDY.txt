The paper in four statements

1. A synaptic gradient in the modeled dendritic tree is exactly a local sensitivity multiplied by a compartment-specific error.
2. The local sensitivity uses presynaptic activity, synaptic driving force, and local input resistance.
3. The compartment error is a somatic error transported through the branches between that synapse and the soma.
4. Shunting changes input resistance and therefore regulates the gain of selected credit paths; restricted-feedback experiments test whether enough of the required error survives for learning.

Exact-gradient reconstruction tests statements 1 through 3. Path-gain, inhibition-intervention, morphology, feedback, and transported-error controls test statement 4. Accuracy is the learning consequence of this chain, not a substitute for the derivation.

1. Presentation and organization

The revision begins with a dendritic-tree schematic and voltage equation, then defines eligibility, compartment error, and feedback. Results follow five steps: exact factorization, feedback fidelity, conductance interventions, task competence, and rule/feedback controls. Assumptions, predictions, and limitations are now in the main discussion; proofs and secondary sweeps remain in the supplement.

We removed terms such as “synapse banks,” “identity upward transfer,” and “gold-ringed soma,” define all abbreviations before use, and rebuilt figures with non-overlapping elements and standalone captions.

Every diagnostic now defines its axes: the 4F proxy estimates covariance over examples after reducing non-batch coordinates; path-gain CV is over compartments within examples; SVD uses examples by compartments; and gradient metrics compare matched parameter vectors. The 5F residual term is now a bounded predictability factor, not confidence.

2. Feedback correction produces a stronger learning result

The original `per_soma` alias preserves the soma vector only at matching width and otherwise uses one scalar; it is now called matched-width/scalar-fallback feedback. An unconstrained rank-1 SVD does not test this uniform broadcast, so pooled SVD and field metrics are no longer used to claim shunting-specific compatibility.

The important result is what happens when neuron identity is preserved. Repeating one soma coordinate over the 13 compartments of each modeled neuron raises 3F MNIST from 90.75% to 97.15% in shunting and from 91.58% to 97.14% in additive networks. The gains are 6.40 and 5.56 percentage points, occur in all 15 pairs, and have signed-rank p=6.1e-5. Changing only the diagnostic feedback field raises branch-gradient cosine from 0.007 to 0.667 and from 0.024 to 0.599, again in every pair.

A five-seed exact-transport factorial provides the ceiling: 3F reaches 97.18 to 97.21%, 5F 97.12 to 97.20%, and backpropagation 97.03%. Thus one coordinate per neuron captures almost all of dense transport's benefit: preserve neuron identity and let ancestry supply the routing map.

3. Significance and positive shunting evidence

The significance is that conductance and morphology enter the exact credit equation in a measurable and experimentally testable way. For inhibitory conductance at compartment k,

`d log(alpha_n) / d G_k^I = -R_k`

for every descendant n whose path traverses k, with no corresponding path effect on unrelated branches. This predicts a spatially selective change in plasticity signals, with strength set by local input resistance and route geometry.

Positive regimes are quantitative. At N_I=5, path-gain CV is about 80% lower under shunting, with the same ordering in all five pairs. Under restricted 5F feedback, shunting exceeds additive on MNIST (91.1 versus 89.4%), Fashion-MNIST (83.8 versus 83.1%), figure-ground MNIST (80.3 versus 78.7%), and compact CIFAR-10 (29.7 versus 25.5%). Across one to four dendritic layers, shunting changes from 63.5 to 57.4%, while additive changes from 54.9 to 29.7%. Shunting is stable through broadcast-noise sigma=0.1, while additive reaches chance at sigma=1.0.

These results support a regime-dependent claim. A matched initialization factorial finds no universal 3F architecture advantage, and neuron-wise feedback brings both cores to about 97.15%. The positive conclusion is therefore not “shunting always wins,” but that shunting gives a mathematically defined, branch-selective control of credit and improves learning in deeper, noisier, and inhibition-sensitive restricted-feedback regimes.

The main text now predicts that a focal shunt preferentially alters descendant plasticity, scales with input resistance, and affects route families according to proximal/distal location and task relevance. These connect voltage, inhibition, morphology, and plasticity measurements.

4. Passive equilibrium and temporal dynamics

The theorem is steady-state, not a claim that time is unimportant. Capacitance, spikes, channel kinetics, calcium, neuromodulation, and recurrence add state and may change credit substantially. A temporal extension replaces the path product with an adjoint or eligibility trace; the current result is its equilibrium starting point.

Local nonlinearities enter path gain through their derivatives. Replacing parametric tanh by identity gives 90.81+/-0.33% versus 90.88+/-0.77% across five seeds, so competence is not created by the selected nonlinearity. A 15-seed identity-transfer control also separates gradient direction from final learning.

Active calcium or sodium conductances can amplify or gate credit; calcium may create branch eligibility traces and neuromodulation may scale or consolidate them. These are direct extensions, not completed experiments.

5. Relevance to real morphology

The derivation permits arbitrary depth, asymmetric branching, and branch-specific conductances. Geometric differences matter when they alter resistance or task-relevant routes. Multiple parents introduce a path sum; recurrence requires an adjoint.

The depth experiment already shows route-length sensitivity. All 40 checkpoints retain every stage (minimum stage-median coupling 0.716), excluding stage pruning. The morphology grid remains descriptive because some couplings initialize near a floor.

For reconstructed cells, one can insert the parent-child graph and fitted conductances, compute path gains and feedback fields, and compare credit routing across morphologies. The code already supports arbitrary rooted trees, E/I interventions, exact-gradient checks, and multiple feedback families.

6. Limitations in the main argument

The main discussion now states steady-state non-spiking dynamics, a rooted tree, nonnegative conductances, input-driven inhibition, and access to a soma teaching signal. It separates theorem terms from reactivation, decoder choices, top-k selection, 4F/5F preconditioning, and auxiliary losses.

The supported conclusion is positive and precise: conductance and branching determine the exact geometry of dendritic credit; the local 3F rule reaches the backpropagation ceiling when feedback preserves the required structure; neuron-indexed feedback achieves this with one coordinate per modeled neuron; and shunting supplies an exact, spatially selective control mechanism with measurable benefits in defined learning regimes. The passive model is therefore a tractable foundation for temporal and reconstructed-neuron work, not merely a simplified classifier.
