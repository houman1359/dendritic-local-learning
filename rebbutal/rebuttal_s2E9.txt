Thank you for recognizing the conceptual shift from proposing another replacement for backpropagation to asking whether neuronal biophysics can make exact credit easier to approximate. We agree that the paper's goal and evaluation scope should have been stated much more clearly.

**Bottom line.** This is a mechanistic NeuroAI contribution: it derives the exact credit equation of a conductance tree and uses controlled learning experiments to identify when restricted feedback succeeds or fails. Large-scale benchmarking is an important next stage, but it answers a different question from the mechanism isolated here.

**Goal and novelty**

The primary contribution is a testable bridge between a biophysical component and a learning computation:

1. derive the exact gradient of a conductance-based dendritic tree;
2. separate it into synapse-local eligibility and a path-transported compartment error;
3. ask how E/I conductance, shunting, and morphology alter the compatibility of that error with restricted feedback;
4. expose when that mechanism helps, fails, or requires higher-bandwidth feedback.

This differs from FF, PEPITA, SPELA, Trifecta, NoProp, and LLS. Those works primarily construct scalable layer-local or forward-only training algorithms: FF uses layer-local goodness; PEPITA uses an output-error perturbation and second forward pass; SPELA embeds class vectors as local targets; Trifecta improves deeper Forward-Forward networks; NoProp uses local denoising targets; and LLS uses activity-synchronization-inspired errors. Our work instead derives the exact credit object of a conductance tree and tests whether biophysics changes its approximability. A comparison table now separates feedback source, within-neuron biophysics, relation to exact credit, and objective.

We also added directly implementable feedback-alignment controls on matched MNIST models. DFA reaches 66.1% in shunting, 62.2% in additive, and 51.8% in point-MLP models, with backpropagation gaps of 24.9--27.6 versus 39.5 points. FA is not dimensionally defined for the block-structured tree updates without changing the method. These are mechanism controls, not a comprehensive benchmark against every cited algorithm.

This bridge has two applications. In neuroscience, it predicts that focal shunting should alter plasticity signals preferentially at descendant synapses, with strength set by local resistance and path geometry; the diagnostics can compare reconstructed cells with fitted conductances. In AI, it identifies neuron-indexed or path-aware feedback as the component to preserve when dendritic units are scaled. The codebase supports matched cores, E/I and morphology interventions, six feedback families, and exact-gradient diagnostics. This mechanism-first route—derivation, causal tests, then scale—develops neuroscience-derived ideas without attributing effects to an uninterpreted large architecture.

**Path gain is not learned**

In Eq. (4), alpha_n is the instantaneous derivative dV_soma/dV_n determined by forward conductances and branch-to-parent couplings. On a tree it is the product of local gains along the unique path. Backpropagation and the transported-error oracle compute it from current state; it is not learned separately. Practical LocalCA does not estimate alpha_n: it replaces alpha_n delta_soma by a restricted field. This is the measured bottleneck.

**What is theoretically justified about shunting**

The theorem does not say inhibition always helps or minimizes rank. It gives a causal local derivative: increasing inhibitory conductance at compartment k reduces every descendant conductance-stage path gain according to d log(alpha_n)/dG_k^I = -R_k. This is why shunting is worth studying: it is a biologically grounded, compartment-local mechanism with exact leverage over both synaptic eligibility and descendant credit gain. Whether that leverage helps depends on placement relative to high-gain and task-relevant paths. The dose-response is non-monotonic, and the morphology grid is descriptive because some shunting couplings begin near an initialization floor. Shunting is therefore a controllable mechanism, not a guarantee of better learning.

The audit also corrected the feedback description. The submitted mode preserves the soma/core vector only at a width-matched stage and otherwise falls back to one scalar; it is neither global scalar everywhere nor ancestry-shared feedback. The reported compatibility contrast pooled an exact-by-construction somatic stage with unequal error energy across architectures. We withdraw the claim that shunting creates a more compatible restricted field; on dendritic compartments there is no reliable ordering. The revision distinguishes all modes and attributes the neuron-wise improvement below to feedback construction.

A current-code fifteen-seed architecture-by-initialization factorial further narrows the empirical claim. With analytical initialization, 3F shunting and additive accuracy are identical (90.92% each); occupancy calibration improves additive but not shunting, producing 90.99% vs. 91.66% (shunting/additive) and a supported interaction. Thus shunting regulates path gain but has no general 3F MNIST learning advantage.

**Scale and benchmark interpretation**

We agree that scale matters. Our CIFAR-10 experiment is a harder-data mechanism ladder under a compact passive core: matched shunting backprop reaches 49.5%, submitted restricted LocalCA 29.7%, and exact transported error 47.2%. Recovery toward the same architecture's ceiling isolates the credit mechanism. The appropriate scale-up is to insert the unit into a competitive backbone and compare feedback modes at matched forward architecture, augmentation, and compute; we state this as future work rather than implying current ImageNet competitiveness.

**Compute, memory, and energy**

The submitted runs use one GPU per seed and no distributed training. In matched 180-epoch MNIST profiles with the same model, batch size, hooks, and evaluation, LocalCA took 190 s versus 125 s for backpropagation; peak CUDA allocation was 1.70 GiB for both (2.03 vs. 1.97 GiB reserved). The current research code therefore shows no speed or memory advantage and is about 1.5x slower. We have not measured hardware energy and make no energy-efficiency claim.

**Generality beyond one rooted pyramidal tree**

The model captures a generic conductance-based rooted dendritic tree rather than a full pyramidal-cell circuit. The unique-parent assumption gives a single closed-form path product. In a directed acyclic morphology with multiple parents, the local eligibility-times-node-error factorization remains, but the error is a sum over all parent paths. Recurrent morphologies require an adjoint or implicit gradient; Hodgkin-Huxley channels and spikes require temporal state and eligibility traces. These are meaningful extensions, not second-order details.

Biologically, shunting inhibition in the model represents input-driven inhibitory conductance changing local input resistance and therefore both forward voltage sensitivity and backward path gain. The classification labels do not represent neurotransmitters; the task is a controlled probe of the resulting learning geometry. A complete circuit model would also need a biologically generated teaching signal and interneuron dynamics. The main text now states these assumptions.

**CNN and transformer extension**

The within-unit theorem is compatible with weight sharing. In a CNN, a dendritic unit can replace a channel-mixing affine operation: presynaptic patches provide local eligibility, shared synaptic parameters sum eligibility over spatial positions, and feedback must be defined in output-channel/soma coordinates. In a transformer, dendritic units could replace feed-forward projections or value/output projections; the same local eligibility applies within each unit, while credit between blocks remains a separate feedback problem. We have not demonstrated these extensions and label them as a concrete design route rather than a result.

**Three-factor versus practical five-factor learning**

A five-seed factorial locates the implementation issue. With exact path-transported errors, theorem-derived 3F reaches 97.18--97.21% across local and backprop-updated decoders, 5F reaches 97.12--97.20%, and matched backprop reaches 97.03%. Thus 5F is not required for exact-gradient learning; it is empirical preconditioning for approximate feedback.

A complementary fifteen-seed control shows that exact compartment-wise errors are not necessary on MNIST. Replacing scalar fallback by intended neuron-wise feedback---one soma-error coordinate repeated over that modeled neuron's compartments---raises 3F accuracy from 90.75% to 97.15% in shunting and from 91.58% to 97.14% in additive networks. The paired gains are 6.40 and 5.56 points, occur in all 15 pairs, and have signed-rank p=6.1e-5. This locates the submitted performance gap in feedback construction rather than theorem-derived eligibility. It controls the first paper's regular-tree implementation, not reconstructed morphology, and narrows the shunting claim because the corrected cross-architecture difference is negligible and not activation-matched.

Finally, the reorganized presentation places the central factorization, feedback distinction, exact reconstruction, mechanism chain, competence boundary, biological predictions, and limitations in the main paper. The appendix holds derivations, configurations, and supporting controls. This makes the contribution visible as a new learning principle and experimental framework, rather than only another small-scale classifier.
