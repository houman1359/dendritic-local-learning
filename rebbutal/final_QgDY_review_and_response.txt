Summary:
The paper approximates single neurons as conductance-based dendritic networks to tackle the problem of how neurons could implement credit assignment without backprop. In this conductance-based network, they derive the 'conductance-stage path gain'. They factorize the synaptic gradient in terms of presynaptic activity, driving force, and input resistance, and one non-local compartment error. They then perform a series of experiments with MNIST, Fashion-MNIST data to highlight the regimes where shunting modulated input resistance can make localCA better.

Contribution Type: General: Most submissions will fall into this type.

Strengths And Weaknesses:

Strengths

The topic is interesting and important for neuroAI.

There is not much work in my knowledge that incorporates the role of morphology in credit assignment and plasticity, and novel theoretical frameworks might be needed with new datasets that are able to image local dendritic activity and plasticity.

Weaknesses

Paper is very hard to follow. Specific comments below:

It took me a couple of reads for context to understand what they mean. I see that a lot of these terms are properly elaborated in the appendix - so either the authors provide an intuitive, abbreviated understanding of these in the main text or link to the relevant section in the appendix.

The simplifying assumptions are not elaborated on, or explained well.

Paper organization and structuring is not great. ======================
AUTHORS' RESPONSE
======================

Thank you for emphasizing that an interesting NeuroAI question is not useful if the reader cannot follow the theory-to-experiment chain. We agree that the submission placed too much terminology and too many assumptions before the central idea was made intuitive.

**Bottom line.** The exact factorization is the central contribution; the experiments test how well restricted feedback approximates its path-specific error. We have reorganized the presentation around that chain, made its neuroscience and learning applications explicit, and narrowed the shunting claim where the controls do not support it.

The paper can be summarized without its implementation vocabulary:

1. A synaptic gradient in the modeled dendritic tree is exactly a local sensitivity multiplied by a compartment-specific error.
2. The local sensitivity uses presynaptic activity, synaptic driving force, and local input resistance.
3. The compartment error is a somatic error transported through the branches between that synapse and the soma.
4. Shunting inhibition changes local input resistance and therefore can regulate the gain of selected credit paths; the experiments ask when restricted feedback remains faithful enough for learning.

This is the intended link between the derivation and the experiments. Exact-gradient reconstruction tests statements 1–3. Path-gain, inhibition-intervention, morphology, feedback-fidelity, and transported-error experiments test statement 4. Task accuracy is then a competence and boundary test, not the derivation's proof.

**Presentation and organization**

We have prepared a substantially reorganized working revision, while recognizing that the original submission remains the basis for review. It introduces the voltage equations and a plain-language tree schematic before implementation conventions; defines “local eligibility,” “compartment error,” and each feedback mode once; removes unresolved abbreviations and phrases such as “identity upward transfer,” “synapse banks,” and “pathway-broadcast mixing weight” from the opening; and rebuilds the figures with non-overlapping elements and standalone captions. The exact factorization and inhibitory path derivative remain in the main argument, while the covariance and path-gain/cosine proofs move to the supplement.

The results are reorganized into: exact factorization; feedback approximation and gradient fidelity; mechanistic evidence; task competence and operating regimes; and rule/feedback controls. Every diagnostic now specifies its axes. The 4F proxy first averages each recorded layer and soma tensor over non-batch coordinates, then estimates covariance over examples; path-gain CV is over compartments within an example and then summarized over examples/seeds; SVD uses an examples-by-compartments matrix; gradient cosine and norm metrics compare matched parameter vectors. We now call the 5F quantity a bounded predictability factor and the SVD residual an unexplained-energy fraction.

There is an important substantive correction behind this clearer organization. The submitted mode preserves the soma/core vector only at a width-matched stage and falls back to one scalar at wider branch stages. It is neither global scalar everywhere nor one soma coordinate repeated over descendants. The manuscript's “rank-1” and “per-soma” labels were both inaccurate. A stage-resolved audit further showed that our reported compatibility metrics pooled the exact-by-construction somatic stage and unequal cross-architecture error energy. We withdraw the claim that the shunting error field is more compatible with restricted feedback; on dendritic compartments there is no reliable ordering. The working revision distinguishes all feedback modes explicitly and treats SVD only as intrinsic geometry.

The corrected distinction also produces a clearer control of the learning rule. In a fifteen-seed comparison paired within each architecture, replacing scalar fallback by intended neuron-wise feedback---one soma coordinate repeated over that modeled neuron's compartments---raises 3F MNIST from 90.75% to 97.15% in shunting and from 91.58% to 97.14% in additive networks. The gains occur in all 15 pairs (signed-rank p=6.1e-5) and approach exact transport and matched backpropagation. At corrected-feedback checkpoints, changing only the feedback field raises branch-gradient cosine from 0.007 to 0.667 and from 0.024 to 0.599, again in every pair. This regular-tree control, not a reconstructed-morphology result, shows that the weak submitted 3F result arose largely from collapsing neuron identity.

**Significance and biological predictions**

The significance is that morphology and conductance enter the exact credit equation in a measurable way. The model predicts that a focal increase in inhibitory conductance should attenuate learning signals preferentially at synapses whose paths traverse that compartment; the magnitude should scale with local input resistance; distal/proximal placement and branch depth should change the effect; and the same intervention need not help if it suppresses task-relevant paths. These predictions connect branch-level voltage, inhibition, and plasticity measurements rather than only classification accuracy. A compact version now appears in the main text beside the limitations.

The codebase makes the framework useful for hypothesis-driven NeuroAI investigation. It provides matched conductance/additive dendritic cores, E/I and morphology controls, several local-rule and feedback families, causal model interventions, exact-gradient diagnostics, and reproducible task sweeps. The neuroscience application is to compare how reconstructed cells or cell types route plasticity signals; the AI application is to design dendritic units with neuron-indexed or path-aware feedback. Establishing those computational properties before scale is itself the paper's mechanism-first contribution.

**Passive equilibrium versus temporal dynamics**

The current theorem is a steady-state equilibrium result, not a claim that time has only a second-order effect. Membrane capacitance, channel kinetics, spikes, and recurrent feedback introduce new state variables and can change both the magnitude and timing of credit substantially. A temporal extension would write the compartment dynamics explicitly and replace the static path product by a time-dependent adjoint or eligibility trace. The static factorization is the zero-frequency/equilibrium limit and a controlled starting point, not a complete neuronal model.

Differentiable local voltage nonlinearities already enter the effective path gain through their derivatives. A five-seed control replacing the parametric tanh branch transfer by identity gives 90.81+/-0.33% versus 90.88+/-0.77%; the near-equal descriptive values show that the reported competence is not created by this selected nonlinearity. Active calcium or sodium conductances are richer: they may amplify, attenuate, or gate credit depending on operating point, and multi-state channels produce local Jacobians rather than a single scalar gain.

Calcium and neuromodulatory processes can be incorporated as slower local state variables. Calcium could create branch-specific eligibility traces or plateau-gated plasticity; neuromodulation could scale, sign, or consolidate those traces. The learning update would then combine a fast transported error with slower local eligibility/reliability state. This is a natural extension of the factorization, but it is not tested here and should be presented as such.

**Relevance to real morphology**

The rooted-tree derivation does not require equal branch factors or symmetry: arbitrary depths, branch-specific conductances, and asymmetric parent-child structure can be inserted directly. Minute geometric changes can matter because the path gain is a product of local gains; their importance depends on whether they appreciably change resistance or the path traversed by task-relevant synapses. Thus the framework is suitable for comparing reconstructed cells by computing their path-gain and feedback-compatibility fields.

However, the submitted experiments use controlled factorized trees, not detailed reconstructions with fitted channel densities. A coupling audit confirms that all nominal stages remain active in the depth sweep, so its depth dependence is not caused by stage pruning. The morphology-by-inhibition grid is less controlled because analytical initialization places some shunting couplings at a near-zero floor; we therefore treat that grid as descriptive and do not use it for a causal morphology claim. Applying the same diagnostics to reconstructed morphologies with measured or fitted conductances is an important next test.

**Limitations moved to the main argument**

The working revision now states in the main discussion that the model assumes steady-state non-spiking dynamics, a rooted feedforward tree, nonnegative conductances, input-driven rather than recurrent lateral inhibition, and access to a soma teaching signal. It also distinguishes exact theorem terms from implementation-specific reactivation, decoder, top-k selection, 4F/5F statistics, and auxiliary losses. Finally, it states that inhibitory interventions establish forward necessity but do not alone isolate a causal credit-assignment mechanism.

These changes make the supported claim precise: conductance and morphology shape the exact geometry of dendritic credit, and shunting can regulate that geometry in selected regimes. The current passive model is a tractable foundation for temporal and reconstructed-neuron studies, not a replacement for them.
