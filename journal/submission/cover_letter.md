Dear Editors,

We submit “Dendritic morphology as a dictionary for local credit assignment” for consideration as an Article in *Nature Communications*.

The paper asks which spatial distinctions a learning signal must preserve for a dendritic tree to learn a task. We describe delivered credit as a combination of spatial profiles constrained by morphology, with conductance controlling their gain. Exact conductance gradients provide the reference against which restricted credit is tested.

The experiments follow a progression in task structure. Image classification benefits chiefly from preserving neuron identity. Context-gated conflict favors branch selection, with an analytically derived interference threshold. Hierarchical distractors favor ancestry routes at one matched bandwidth, following the class-signal profile specified by the generator. In a compatible scalar multi-affine tree, pairwise and quartic targets have identical input-sensitivity spectra but develop different credit geometry: a fixed spatial profile supports pairwise learning while performing poorly on quartic interactions. Fresh paired experiments, matched tuning budgets and independent checkpoint analysis establish this distinction. A separate conductance-tree experiment makes the connection concrete: context-dependent opposing branch tuning is learned with three spatial patterns whose coefficients come from the exact field, but poorly with a fixed broadcast profile. This result persists across two optimizers, extended training and much wider conductance bounds. Serial-depth experiments also show where longer training removes an apparent credit advantage.

Reconstructed arbors provide a separate anatomical test. When every dictionary receives the same budgeted broadcast component, ancestry routes capture additional modeled spatial variation in held-out reconstructed cohorts. An exact passive-tree identity places a focal shunt's adjoint effect in a baseline-weighted ancestry partition, linking conductance to the dictionary's gains. Electrical calibration identifies the conditions supporting selective effects. Measured visual responses show no preferential ancestry alignment; they do not yet establish endogenous use of the proposed routes.

The claim is a task-dependent account of useful credit resolution. We explicitly retain the failed prospective initialization selector, distinguish input sensitivity from learned parameter credit, and provide the representability theorem and noisy-query structure estimator as supporting analyses within this same Article.

The related-manuscript statement discloses the predecessor preprint and the inheritance of equations, local rules and regular-tree controls. The prior NeurIPS 2026 submission still awaits an official decision according to the author's current account. The author intends this Nature Communications Article as the sole publication from the credit project; the draft does not assert a rejection or withdrawal that has not occurred.
