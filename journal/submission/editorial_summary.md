# Editorial summary

**Title:** Dendritic morphology as a dictionary for local credit assignment

This Article asks which spatial distinctions dendritic credit must preserve for different tasks. Morphology supplies candidate delivery patterns; conductance sets their gain. The main evidence follows neuron identity on image tasks, branch selection under context conflict, ancestry grouping with hierarchical distractors, and more resolved credit for higher-order interactions.

The central new comparison holds the forward tree and input-sensitivity spectrum fixed. Pairwise and quartic targets are both representable, but their trained exact credit fields differ in their best rank-one capture. Fresh, paired learning experiments show that a fixed spatial profile supports pairwise learning yet leaves a large quartic error gap. Capture is an oracle diagnostic of the learned field, not a prospective selector or a claim that each compartment requires an independent external teaching signal. A separate conductance-tree comparison uses identical inputs with aligned or opposed branch tuning. A local inhibitory gate supplies distal branch selection while retaining ungated proximal credit. Twenty new paired seeds recover near-exact learning without oracle path coefficients; swapped and proximal-gated controls fail. Physical-depth comparisons show a budget-dependent change in accuracy ranking while exact credit retains lower cross-entropy; all deep fits remain at the cap.

Reconstructed anatomy supplies sparse spatial dictionaries beyond a common broadcast; ancestry and alternative spatial profiles are compared at matched total column budgets. Modeled shunt-response fields test compression of an ancestry-favoring passive response family across separate cohorts; actual-versus-surrogate anatomy is the main specificity control. A focal shunt applies exact gains to the baseline-weighted ancestry partition in a passive tree, while electrical calibration limits the range of spatial selectivity. Measured responses provide no evidence of preferential ancestry alignment, with reliability-aware sensitivity quantified under an explicit model; the separate offline learning comparison diagnoses transfer geometry.

The failed initialization selector remains explicit. Forward representability, noisy-query tree estimation and learning dynamics are distinguished in the supporting analyses without replacing the credit-first argument. The paper does not infer optimal morphology from a scalar task rank.

The predecessor preprint is cited and its inherited material disclosed. The author intends this Article as the only publication from the credit project, but the prior conference submission has no official final decision recorded here. Author declarations and final consideration status must be verified before upload.
