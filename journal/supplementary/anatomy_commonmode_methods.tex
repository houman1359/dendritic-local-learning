\subsection{Anatomical capacity after equalizing the common broadcast}

The original eight-cell analysis exposed a common-mode mismatch: depth bins included a constant field, whereas selected subtree profiles did not. We therefore gave every family an identical budgeted broadcast column. The eight cells were development data for this correction. The protocol and input hashes were frozen before computing the new independent-generator outcomes in 47 disjoint v661 cells and the Pinky cohort. Both follow-up cohorts had previously been used for route-generated positive controls; this is a frozen follow-up on existing anatomy, not external preregistration or new animal sampling. Stable nucleus identifiers verify that v661 excludes the original eight cells; the original eight cells and the 47-cell v661 extension belong to one mouse, while Pinky supplies a second animal.

Every dictionary has one all-site constant and $K-1$ spatial columns, for $K=1,2,4,8,16$ when site counts permit. The focus budget is eight. Ancestry columns retain the existing ordering by local inhibitory fraction times excitatory-area-weighted descendant fraction, without inspecting the response. Random routes sample inhibitory sites without replacement. Site shuffling independently permutes each ancestry column, preserving its values and nonzero count while leaving the constant untouched. Surrogates preserve node depths and parent out-degrees while reassigning parent slots, then recompute ancestry and its ranking. The depth dictionary contains a constant and the first $K-1$ inherited depth bins, giving exactly the original $K$-bin span. Its additional nonzero count is retained. Equal column budgets need not give equal numerical rank: random and overlapping routes can be redundant.

The response matrix $H$ contains finite-difference changes in the logarithm of absolute excitatory synaptic gradients after focal shunts, with the soma reclamped. It uses the inherited dimensionless cable model, excitatory and inhibitory scales 0.35, reversal potentials 1 and $-0.2$, and derivative dose 0.001. Let $Y=W^{1/2}H$, with $W$ proportional to excitatory contact area, and let $q$ be the normalized weighted constant. The residual field is $R=(I-qq^{\mathsf T})Y$. Total capture is $\|P_DY\|_F^2/\|Y\|_F^2$; residual capture is $\|P_DR\|_F^2/\|R\|_F^2$, where $P_D$ projects onto the weighted dictionary. Since every dictionary contains $q$, total capture equals broadcast capture plus $(1-\text{broadcast capture})$ times residual capture, cell by cell. The common-constrained oracle adds the leading $K-1$ left singular vectors of $R$.

We average 200 randomized controls within each cell before inference. Paired cell means and 95\% bootstrap intervals use 20,000 draws; Holm adjustments cover four spatial controls separately for each endpoint and cohort at $K=8$. Other budgets are descriptive. All cohorts remain separate. Pinky retains ten of twelve originally selected cells under the inherited typed-contact criterion; eight qualify at $K=8$, and none at $K=16$. The other two qualifying cells remain in lower-budget outcomes. Their small input-bearing domains explain near-saturation at eight columns.

The analysis retains 65 response operators and 149,331 dictionary evaluations, including 148,200 randomized realizations. Validation checks frozen inputs, original ancestry-capture reproduction, weighted energy identities, oracle ceilings, depth-span equivalence, numerical ranks, common-mode coverage and exact ancestry--shuffle nonzero matching. Five independent numerical tests pass. All per-draw results, operator matrices, eligibility records and bootstrap summaries are available in \texttt{source\_data/anatomy\_commonmode/}. Squared-energy capture here differs from the one-minus-residual-norm score used in the older route-generated replication, whose endpoint is labeled separately.
