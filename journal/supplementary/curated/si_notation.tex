\subsection*{Reading conventions across experiments}

Bold symbols are vectors or matrices; $\mathsf T$ denotes transpose,
$\|\cdot\|$ the Euclidean norm, $\|\cdot\|_F$ the matrix Frobenius norm,
and $\mathbf1[\cdot]$ an indicator. Hats denote supplied approximations or
estimates; primes denote post-intervention values in the cable experiments.
An \emph{oracle} uses an exact field or target-dependent quantity that is
not available to the restricted learner. A seed is one independent draw of
the paired training problem, not one neuron, trial or checkpoint.

Morphology $[b_1,\ldots,b_D]$ lists daughter counts from soma to tips, with
$\sum_{d=1}^D\prod_{r=1}^db_r$ nonsomatic compartments. D1--D4 label physical
stage counts, but not a universal compartment inventory: main Fig.~6 fixes
eight modules, whereas Supplementary Fig.~S9 fixes sixteen terminals.
Feedback budget $K$ counts delivery profiles and is not physical depth.
Lower-case $k$ in a fitted spectrum counts its leading singular directions.

Scalar feedback shares one coefficient \emph{across neurons}. Per-neuron
(or within-neuron) broadcast instead shares each neuron's own coefficient
\emph{within its arbor}; it does not make all neurons receive the same
error. Projected broadcast obtains its coefficient from the exact
compartmental field, unlike somatic broadcast. A local gate changes this
within-arbor delivery using supplied inhibitory activity. The hard,
shunt-proportional and relative-resistance gates are distinct rules,
defined in Section~\ref{note:conductance_learning}.

For the DendriNet selection cohort, $b$ indexes four sensory streams,
$z_{bj}$ is a latent feature, $c_b$ is its one-hot selection cue,
$a_b^{\rm I}=4(1-c_b)$ is inhibitory activity, and $s$ is distractor severity.
$D_p^0$ denotes a proximal compartment's leak-plus-coupling conductance;
$h_p=D_p^0/(D_p^0+g_p^{\rm I}a_b^{\rm I})$ is its relative-resistance gate. These task cues are
distinct from the delivery coefficients $\bm c_u$ in $A_u\bm c_u$.
In main Fig.~8B only, $B$ is the ancestry-block indicator matrix,
$\bm h$ the baseline cable transfer, and $\bm\eta$ the block-gain vector;
this $\bm\eta$ is not the scalar learning rate $\eta$.

NMSE is mean squared prediction error divided by the specified reference.
The algebraic interaction tasks use clean target variance despite noisy
labels; noiseless conductance tasks use the evaluation target variance.
The measured-response prediction study instead divides by the held-out error
of the training-mean predictor (Section~S9). These are not one shared normalization.
Accuracy is a fraction or percent of correctly classified examples; a
percentage-point contrast subtracts two percentages. Capture is a squared-
energy projection ratio in the named field and coordinate system, not an
accuracy, gradient cosine or fraction of gradient surviving context averaging.
