\caption{\textbf{A first conductance task produces a small precision benefit from exact credit.}
\textbf{A,B}, Test normalized mean squared error (NMSE) at fixed checkpoints under Adam, for ungated independent inputs and gated conflicting inputs in the original 16-conductance model. Curves show means and 95\% paired-seed bootstrap intervals across 20 fresh seeds; the vertical dotted line marks the original 4,096-update budget. All fits continue to 16,384 updates with unchanged optimizer and minibatch state. Unit and calibrated profiles differ here because global gradient clipping can break Adam's invariance to fixed positive coordinate scaling.
\textbf{C}, Gated-task NMSE differences between the initial calibrated profile and exact credit, evaluated at each fit's best validation checkpoint in the extended window. Points are paired seed differences; symbols and bars show their means and 95\% bootstrap intervals. The Adam gap is $0.000502$ [$0.000267$, $0.000772$], positive in all 20 seeds; under Adam, mean broadcast error nevertheless remains below 0.0006 NMSE.
\textbf{D}, Diagnostics at the exact-rule best-validation states distinguish the best rank-one capture of the six-compartment path field from capture by each student's fixed initial profile after weighting by local parameter eligibility. Both are squared-energy projection ratios with one freely fitted amplitude per example. High eligibility-weighted capture helps explain the small learning difference. The gated and ungated tasks change both inhibition and distractor dependence, so this first study is a task-family comparison. All 216 development fits, 240 fresh fits and their continuations are retained in Source Data.}
\label{fig:supp-conductance-small-effect}
\end{figure}

\caption{\textbf{The opposed-tuning conductance result persists across optimization and parameter-range controls.}
\textbf{A}, Fixed-checkpoint test NMSE under SGD for the opposed-tuning task, showing the four credit rules, including the explicit three-profile oracle. Curves and shading are means and 95\% bootstrap intervals over 20 fresh seeds; the dotted line marks 4,096 updates.
\textbf{B}, Paired task-by-credit interactions, defined as the calibrated-minus-exact NMSE gap for opposed tuning minus the corresponding gap for aligned tuning. All 320 fresh conditions were restarted from their original initial parameters and minibatch streams for 16,384 updates with either the original log-conductance bounds $[-7,7]$ or wider bounds $[-20,20]$. Rates were not retuned. Endpoints use the best validation checkpoint in the same window. The 320 original-bound replays reproduce every final parameter exactly. Wider-bound Adam and SGD interactions are $0.626180$ [$0.462459$, $0.773066$] and $0.714963$ [$0.584518$, $0.837830$], respectively, each positive in all 20 seeds. Symbols and bars show means and 95\% paired bootstrap intervals. Some broadcast parameters also reach the wider bounds; these are finite-budget robustness comparisons.
\textbf{C}, At each seed's last saved checkpoint before any parameter of the opposed-task calibrated Adam fit first reaches an original bound, the calibrated-minus-exact NMSE gap is already positive. Both rules are evaluated at that same checkpoint; all 20 seeds are shown. First bound contacts occur between updates 384 and 8,326 and involve proximal inhibitory gains or daughter couplings. Exact and three-profile oracle fits never reach the parameter bounds. Parameter projection is distinct from gradient-norm clipping, which occurs in none of the fresh or wider-bound fits.
\textbf{D}, All four retained development regimes under Adam, using each rule's lowest mean validation NMSE among its three equally budgeted learning rates. These are development summaries over three seeds, not additional confirmatory seed blocks. Strong and moderate gating use inactive-parent inhibitory activities of 10 and 1, respectively; ungated uses zero. The strong opposed regime was selected using teacher geometry and exact-rule learnability, without selecting on the broadcast gap.}
\label{fig:supp-conductance-robustness}
\end{figure}
