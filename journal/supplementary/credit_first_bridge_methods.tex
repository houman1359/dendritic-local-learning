\subsection{Matched fixed-profile learning and credit-field geometry}

This experiment separates forward compatibility, spatial profile calibration and learning. The cut construction specializes hierarchical tensor representations to scalar multi-affine messages \citep{hackbusch2009tensor,grasedyck2010hierarchical}. Each algebraic tree has seven binary multi-affine units, four coefficients per unit, and only a root readout. The six nonroot units receive the manipulated credit field. The root derivative is one in every condition. Pairwise and quartic targets share a compatible balanced tree and a seed-specific permutation of eight independent Rademacher inputs. Their coefficients have magnitude one-half and independently sampled signs. Their input-sensitivity second moments both equal $I_8/4$, but their clean output variances are one and one-half. A nested target combines products over prefixes of sizes two, four, six and eight on its separately compatible tree; it is not part of the input-isospectral comparison.

Let $q_n(x)=\partial u_0/\partial u_n$ be the exact path derivative for unit $n$, and let $\delta(x)$ be the scalar derivative of normalized half-squared error with respect to root output. Local coefficient eligibilities are $1,u_L,u_R,u_Lu_R$. Exact credit multiplies these by $\delta q_n$. Unit broadcast replaces $q_n$ by one. An initially calibrated profile uses $a_n=256^{-1}\sum_x q_n(x)$ over 256 unlabeled initialization examples, then keeps $a_n$ fixed. The sign control retains only $\operatorname{sign}(a_n)$. Profiles require no target labels. Learning-rate choices are separate from the profile calculation. Fixed positive coordinate scaling largely cancels under Adam, so calibrated and sign-only Adam trajectories are expected to be nearly identical; they are not independent mechanistic confirmations.

The protocol and code were frozen before development. Three disjoint development seeds tested the same three rates for every rule and optimizer. The selected rate minimized final validation NMSE averaged across the three task families, separately for each model, optimizer and rule. A common rate per model and optimizer was also selected. Both choices were sealed before twenty fresh paired seed blocks. Matching and quartic conditions share initial weights, calibration inputs, example streams, additive label noise and minibatch order. Both SGD and Adam were retained; the primary contrast was predefined for Adam as the quartic-minus-pairwise difference in calibrated-minus-exact held-out NMSE. The experiment contains 432 development and 1,200 fresh fits, with the selected and common-rate union retained without endpoint exclusions. Forty-eight short benchmark fits were excluded from selection and inference.

Algebraic training, validation and test sets contain 2,048, 1,024 and 4,096 examples, with minibatches of 64 and gradient-norm clipping at ten. Initial nonbias coefficients are independent $\mathcal N(0,0.5^2)$ draws and biases are zero. Adam uses $\beta=(0.9,0.999)$ and $\epsilon=10^{-8}$. Both algebraic optimizers test rates 0.003, 0.01 and 0.03. The conductance teacher uses 1,024 training, 1,024 validation and 4,096 test examples without label noise, constraining log-conductances to $[-5,5]$. Its SGD rates are 0.001, 0.003 and 0.01, and Adam rates are 0.003, 0.01 and 0.03.

Algebraic fits stop after 1,024 steps, retaining coefficient bounds $[-2,2]$ and all recorded bound contacts. Saved checkpoints occur at steps 0, 1, 16, 64, 256, 512 and 1,024. Additive label noise has standard deviation 0.15, giving expected noise-only NMSE of 0.0225 for matching and nested targets and 0.045 for quartic targets. The gradient divides the residual by each family's own clean variance. Clean full-domain population error is a secondary endpoint. All twenty endpoint seeds, including poorly fitted exact quartic cases, remain in the figures and means. Bootstrap intervals use 10,000 whole-seed resamples and are descriptive 95\% intervals; the common-rate comparison tests sensitivity to tuning, not a new independent cohort.

For each saved state, let $Q$ have one row per evaluation example and six columns containing $q_n$. For a dictionary $A$, energy capture is $\|QP_A\|_F^2/\|Q\|_F^2$, where $P_A$ is its orthogonal projector. The best fixed rank-one profile is the leading right singular vector of $Q$, with capture $\sigma_1(Q)^2/\sum_j\sigma_j(Q)^2$. Its profile and per-example amplitudes are oracle fits. The uncentered second moment is $C=Q^{\mathsf T}Q/N$, and participation-ratio effective rank is $(\operatorname{tr}C)^2/\operatorname{tr}(C^2)$. We additionally evaluated unit broadcast, the fixed initial profile, ancestry spans, full-rank capture, and loss-error-weighted fields $\operatorname{diag}(\delta)Q$. The latter has its own optimal profile; applying the path-optimal profile after error weighting answers a different question.

Eligibility-weighted reconstruction multiplies exact and projected path fields by their local coefficient features before comparing parameter updates. Site-normalized spectra divide each path coordinate by its root-mean-square magnitude, exposing sensitivity to fixed coordinate scaling. Raw capture energies are not invariant to arbitrary hidden-unit rescaling. At identical weights and inputs, $Q$ is independent of the target; a scalar residual also cancels from normalized per-example projection capture whenever it is nonzero. Eighty common-state relabeling controls verify that identity. These diagnostics characterize learned-state geometry, rather than infer a task-only credit rank at initialization.

An independent analysis recomputed 5,040 saved states in the matched cohort, checked 20,160 metrics to maximum absolute discrepancy $8.7\times10^{-15}$, and verified 120 matching--quartic pairing identities. The earlier 600 selected compatible-tree fits had metric records but no saved parameter states. Faithful replay reconstructed 3,600 checkpoints; all 28,800 archived deterministic metrics matched to $8.9\times10^{-16}$. Those reconstructions provide replication and trajectory comparisons, not previously stored checkpoints. A separate independent learning audit recomputed held-out NMSE for 138 task files with maximum discrepancy $2.22\times10^{-16}$.

The positive-conductance comparison uses the prior compatible teacher across three paired input permutations and the same credit definitions. It does not implement the algebraic quartic target. All four rules learn it accurately with Adam; exact credit has mean NMSE $9.39\times10^{-6}$, compared with $1.35\times10^{-6}$ for the fixed-profile controls. Positive path derivatives make sign and unit broadcasting identical. Both models' local updates were independently checked by finite differences. Protocols, selections, checkpoints, full trajectories, numerical audits and scripts are supplied in \texttt{source\_data/credit\_rule\_bridge/}, \texttt{source\_data/credit\_resolution\_bridge/} and their corresponding script directories.
