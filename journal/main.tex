\begin{abstract}
Dendritic trees compartmentalize synaptic inputs, but which spatial distinctions a learning signal must preserve is unclear. We describe delivered credit as combinations of spatial profiles constrained by morphology. In image classifiers with one dendritic neuronal layer, preserving neuronal identity accounts for most of the feedback benefit. Contextual conflict favors branch selection, while hierarchical distractors favor ancestry grouping at a bandwidth predicted by the task. Targets with matched input-sensitivity spectra develop different learned credit spectra and tolerance of fixed profiles; a local inhibitory gate also supports learning of opposing branch tuning in a conductance tree. Serial compartments help when nuisance-factor information is distributed across modules. Reconstructed arbors compress modeled passive perturbation fields through ancestry profiles, and focal shunts regulate their gains with selectivity set by electrotonic state. Measured visual responses provide no evidence of preferential ancestry alignment in the sampled cells. The framework links task structure to useful credit distinctions while separating available delivery patterns from the mechanisms that generate their coefficients.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}

Learning must link a synapse's local activity to its contribution to a circuit outcome. Cable filtering, nonlinear integration and spatially organized inhibition make locations within a dendritic tree functionally distinct \citep{rall1962dendrites,koch1999biophysics,poirazi2003pyramidal,london2005dendritic,spruston2008pyramidal,branco2010single,larkum2013cellular}. These differences expand computation. We ask when branching also helps distribute useful learning signals.

Credit assignment asks how changing a synapse would affect task error. Backpropagation (BP) answers this by calculating the loss gradient---the sensitivity of task error to each parameter \citep{rumelhart1986learning}; related sensitivity calculations apply to recurrent and steady-state networks \citep{almeida1987asynchronous,pineda1987recurrent}. For a point neuron, each weight gradient combines a local sensitivity, called eligibility, with a neuron-specific learning signal. Hebbian plasticity captures the association between local pre- and postsynaptic variables \citep{hebb1949organization}, while three-factor rules combine that eligibility with an outcome-dependent signal \citep{fremaux2016threefactor}. We call a rule local when a synapse combines its own eligibility with an available learning signal, whether or not that signal is computed locally.

Biologically motivated learning theories differ in how they obtain such signals. Feedback alignment communicates errors through separate pathways \citep{lillicrap2016feedback,nokland2016dfa}; equilibrium, predictive and prospective methods infer target states or errors from dynamics \citep{scellier2017equilibrium,whittington2017predictive,song2024prospective}; eligibility propagation combines temporal traces with neuron-specific signals \citep{bellec2020eprop}. One number shared across neurons carries less task information than separately assigned neuronal signals, although each synapse still has its own eligibility \citep{werfel2005learning,whittington2019theories,lillicrap2020backprop}. Recent causal evidence for distinct instructive signals across neuronal populations motivates asking what additional distinctions are useful within a neuron's arbor \citep{francioni2026vectorized}.

A \emph{route dictionary} describes the spatial profiles available for delivering learning signals. A route identifies the locations reached; its profile specifies their relative amplitudes, and a trial-dependent coefficient scales that profile. Each dictionary column stores one profile. A shared profile broadcasts throughout an arbor; combining several profiles can distinguish subtrees. We use \emph{basis} for linearly independent profiles and \emph{dictionary} when profiles may be redundant. Morphology constrains the available routes; the task and teaching mechanism determine useful coefficients.

Conductance changes the gains of anatomical delivery routes. Excitatory and inhibitory contacts open nonnegative conductances whose reversal potentials determine current; inhibition can lower input resistance and attenuate other inputs even when its own current is small \citep{koch1983nonlinear,holt1997shunting,chance2002gain,gidon2012inhibition,lovettbarron2012regulation}. Rescaling a delivery channel preserves its spatial profile, whereas altered cable transport can reshape how local voltage affects somatic output and task error.

Dendritic models already connect nonlinear compartments and structural plasticity to memory capacity \citep{poirazi2001capacity}, and branch-specific plasticity can organize nonlinear feature combinations \citep{legenstein2011branch}. Successive computations in artificial trees and detailed cortical models also establish substantial forward capacity \citep{jones2021successive,beniaguev2021single}. Dendritic learning theories supply mechanisms for somatic prediction, segregated teaching, interneuron-mediated errors, bursts and prospective dynamics \citep{urbanczik2014dendritic,schiess2016somatodendritic,guerguiev2017segregated,sacramento2018dendritic,payeur2021burst,ellenberger2026gle,richards2019dendritic}; cable-learning models optimize reconstructed neurons using synaptic sensitivities \citep{bicknell2021synaptic}. We ask which spatial distinctions these mechanisms must preserve within an arbor.

The conductance-tree gradient factorization and LocalCA rule were introduced in our predecessor preprint \citep{safaai2026localcredit}. This Article extends those foundations with controlled task-to-credit comparisons, matched-spectrum and conductance learning experiments, and anatomical transport and response tests.

We test a progression from neuronal identity to branch selection, ancestry grouping and example-dependent credit across interacting branches. Controlled learning tasks establish useful distinctions; forward-compatibility tests separate learning gaps from representational limits. Reconstructed arbors then test anatomical delivery capacity and conductance-dependent gains, while measured responses bound the biological interpretation.

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Exact gradients separate local sensitivity from downstream credit}

For a point neuron $y_u=f_u(\sum_iw_ix_i+b_u)$, the chain rule gives $\partial\mathcal L/\partial w_i=x_if'_u\delta_u$, where $x_i$ and $w_i$ are synaptic input and weight, $b_u$ is bias, and $\delta_u=\partial\mathcal L/\partial y_u$ is the neuronal error. The derivative $f'_u$ is evaluated at the current input sum. Local eligibility $x_if'_u$ and task-derived credit therefore supply different information (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}A).

The rooted-tree model has soma \(n=0\), compartment voltage \(V_n\), and nonnegative synaptic conductances \(g_i\). A contact with reversal potential \(E_i^{\mathrm{rev}}\) and nonnegative activity \(x_i\) contributes current \(g_ix_i(E_i^{\mathrm{rev}}-V_n)\). In the directed artificial trees, child outputs drive their parents and a terminal-to-soma pass computes the steady state. Total input resistance \(R_n^{\mathrm{tot}}\) is the reciprocal of the summed leak, excitatory, inhibitory and child-coupling conductances (Methods). Differentiating the current balance yields
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial g_i}
=\underbrace{x_iR_n^{\mathrm{tot}}(E_i^{\mathrm{rev}}-V_n)}_{\mathrm{local\ eligibility}}
\underbrace{\delta^V_{n,u}}_{\partial\mathcal L/\partial V_n}.
\label{eq:factorization}
\end{equation}
Excitatory (E) and inhibitory (I) contacts share this factorization (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}A, filled blue and filled carmine contacts on the arbor). Eligibility tells how conductance changes local voltage; compartment credit tells how that voltage changes task loss.

Voltage error denotes a loss derivative. Each compartment has one directed path to the soma, giving
\begin{equation}
\delta^V_{n,u}=\delta^V_{0,u}\widetilde\alpha_n,
\qquad
\widetilde\alpha_n=
\prod_{(i\to k)\in\operatorname{path}(n\to0)}
f_i'(V_i)R_k^{\mathrm{tot}}g_{i\to k}^{\mathrm{den}}.
\label{eq:pathgain}
\end{equation}
Here \(f'_i(V_i)\) is the child compartment's output derivative, \(g_{i\to k}^{\mathrm{den}}\) is its coupling conductance to parent \(k\), and \(\delta^V_{0,u}=f'_0(V_0)\delta_u\) is the somatic voltage error. The path multiplier \(\widetilde\alpha_n\) converts one somatic error into a spatial field. The conductance-only component of this product is attenuated by additional conductance on a path's ancestors; branch nonlinearities and forward voltages can change as well (Supplementary Section~S1 and Fig.~S1).

Reciprocal cables instead require a joint adjoint calculation of how local currents affect task loss (Methods). These mathematical sensitivities do not establish a biological error-transmission mechanism.

Local credit assignment (LocalCA) retains eligibility and supplies an approximate compartment signal, denoted by a hat (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}B shows a strict scalar, one signal per neuron and the exact path, each on a generic tree):
\begin{align}
\widehat\nabla_{g_i}\mathcal L
=x_iR_n^{\mathrm{tot}}(E_i^{\mathrm{rev}}-V_n)\widehat\delta^V_{n,u},
\label{eq:dendriticlocalrule}
\\
\widehat{\bm\delta}^{V}_u=A_u\bm c_u.
\label{eq:routedfield}
\end{align}
Columns of \(A_u\in\mathbb R^{N_u\times K}\) are fixed spatial delivery profiles over \(N_u\) routed nonsomatic compartments; \(\bm c_u\) contains their example-dependent coefficients. To distinguish coefficient generation from delivery gain, we can write \(\bm c_u=\Gamma_u\bm c_u^{\rm source}\), where diagonal \(\Gamma_u\) scales each profile and \(\bm c_u^{\rm source}\) is the supplied signal (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}A, gain dial). Fixed nonzero gains leave the dictionary span and its oracle capture unchanged while altering conditioning and reliability-weighted learning (Supplementary Figs.~S4--S5). Broadcast, subtree and compartment profiles give increasingly detailed spatial addresses (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}C). This is a delivery model, distinct from the forward cable equations and the mechanism estimating coefficients.


At \(K=1\), the all-ones column \(A_u=\bm1\) broadcasts one coefficient through the arbor; at \(K=N_u\), the identity matrix \(A_u=I\) can reproduce the full nonsomatic field. Nested subtrees provide intermediate dictionaries. Profile count, rank (the number of independent profiles) and coefficient information are distinct resources. Exact path transport combines one somatic scalar with state-dependent local derivatives; it does not require an independent external error for every compartment.

Capture is the fraction of squared learning-signal magnitude that a dictionary can reproduce. For a nonzero field \(\bm\delta\),
\[
\mathcal C(A;\bm\delta)=\frac{\|P_A\bm\delta\|^2}{\|\bm\delta\|^2},
\qquad P_A=AA^\dagger.
\]
Here \(P_A\) gives the least-squares reconstruction, \(A^\dagger\) is the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse and \(\|\cdot\|\) the Euclidean norm. Capture measures field energy rather than decision accuracy. Averaging these per-field ratios weights fields equally; \(\tr(P_AC)/\tr(C)\), with \(C=\mathbb E[\bm\delta\bm\delta^{\mathsf T}]\), is the fraction of total ensemble energy captured, weighting fields by their energy. The uncentered second-moment matrix \(C\) has trace \(\tr(C)\), the sum of its diagonal entries. These capture measures can differ, and both assume optimal projection coefficients rather than an implemented encoder.

The field coordinate also matters. In nonlinear image models, we route errors of compartment outputs and retain the local output derivative in eligibility; voltage-error capture is reported separately. Identity-output conductance trees use voltage errors directly. Supplementary Table~S2 compares the coordinates, coefficient sources and state dependence. Learning experiments test whether this available capacity improves performance.

Projection can also filter update noise outside a dictionary's span. Retaining most of the useful gradient while admitting less noise can improve a one-step guarantee (Supplementary Fig.~S3G). If the span contains the entire gradient, its mean is preserved. A one-step smoothness bound formalizes this balance between retained descent and update variance (Methods; Supplementary Fig.~S3G). Its initialization utility did not select the best final morphology: in a separate 12,800-fit prospective experiment, it lost to a fixed maximum-budget tree in all twenty seed averages in both selection arms (Supplementary Fig.~S34). We therefore use the bound to interpret local filtering, not to select a final tree.

\subsection*{One signal per neuron captures most of the image-task learning benefit}

Image classification first tests whether within-arbor resolution helps when neuronal errors are available. All image networks used one dendritic layer and a trainable readout, which supplied exact neuronal output errors before the primary feedback interventions. The MNIST comparison used 128 directed \([3,3]\) trees, each with three proximal and nine terminal compartments, receiving flattened images. Shunting and raw-additive architectures shared the graph, initial contact construction, nonlinearities and training protocol, with architecture-specific initialization. The additive control removed reversal-potential driving forces and the conductance denominator, retaining signed current summation without shunting.

Six rules separated neuronal identity, spatial resolution and learning in the dendritic core (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}B--D): a strict scalar, per-neuron feedback (neuron-specific), a projected $K=1$ rule, projected $K=3$ subtree profiles, the exact path and a decoder-only reference. The strict scalar shared one signal across neurons; per-neuron feedback broadcast each neuron's own output error through its arbor. The projected $K=1$ rule averaged the exact activation errors over all twelve nonsomatic compartments, while the projected $K=3$ profiles averaged them within each proximal compartment and its three children. Per-neuron feedback uses the same all-ones profile as the projected $K=1$ rule and differs only in the coefficient source (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}C). Both used the same exact field and preserved its mean at identical parameters, while allowing different norms. The exact path retained every compartment error. A decoder-only reference trained the readout while freezing the core. Three development seeds selected each rule's rate from three common multipliers; ten fresh paired seeds per architecture evaluated selected and original common rates over 180 epochs (Methods; Supplementary Fig.~S7A,B).

At selected rates, per-neuron feedback exceeded the strict scalar by 9.23 percentage points in shunting trees and 7.46 points in additive trees, reaching 97.12\% and 97.11\% accuracy (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}D,E). Decoder-only learning reached 80.96\% and 89.57\%, respectively (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}D). Increasing the projected dictionary from one to three profiles changed accuracy by only 0.049 points in shunting trees (95\% paired interval, $-0.048$ to 0.141) and 0.036 points in additive trees ($-0.056$ to 0.122; point estimates in Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}D, note; intervals in Source Data). The common-rate comparison gave similarly small differences (Supplementary Fig.~S7C). The exact path added 0.010 points over per-neuron feedback in shunting trees (5 of 10 seeds positive) and 0.182 points in additive trees (0.114--0.252; positive in all ten seeds; Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}F); against the projected $K=3$ rule it was 0.09 and 0.04 points lower (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}D, note). Both the exact path and one projected profile reached about 97.3\% in additive trees. Most learning benefit therefore came from preserving neuronal coordinates; the smaller additive improvement was attainable without separating subtrees.

The richer dictionary nevertheless captured more of the trained activation-error field in the same cohort. Moving from one to three profiles raised mean capture from 0.622 to 0.655 in shunting trees and from 0.764 to 0.859 in additive trees, and the twelve-profile identity dictionary reproduces the field exactly by construction (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}G). Additive capture nevertheless fell over training at both budgets (0.851 to 0.764 at $K=1$; 0.893 to 0.859 at $K=3$), and the two shunting values at initialization were indistinguishable (0.5322 and 0.5323; open markers in Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}G). These ratios average over example--neuron fields and use the coordinates delivered during learning. Thus improved reconstruction did not yield a comparable accuracy benefit. Earlier voltage-credit diagnostics and recipient-permutation controls support spatial field structure and the importance of neuronal identity, respectively (Supplementary Section~S2 and Figs.~S8A--C).

With fixed random soma-level feedback (DFA), per-neuron feedback improved over the strict scalar by 11.03 points in shunting trees and 10.80 points in additive trees (fifteen paired seeds; Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}E; Supplementary Fig.~S6D), while exact paths added at most 0.09 points (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}F; Supplementary Fig.~S6E). This control changes weight transport but retains the single dendritic layer and the same output-error dimensionality. Fashion-MNIST also favored per-neuron feedback over the matched-width scalar fallback, by 4.46 and 3.78 points, with exact paths within 0.14 points of it (ten paired seeds; Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}E,F; Supplementary Fig.~S6C). On flattened CIFAR-10, exact paths reduced accuracy relative to per-neuron feedback by 0.86 points (0.55--1.17), while remaining equivalent to matched backpropagation within the prespecified one-point margin ($-0.115$ points, $-0.457$ to 0.226; two one-sided tests $P=1.6\times10^{-5}$; twenty paired seeds; Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}F, diamond in the band; Supplementary Fig.~S6A,B). These four cohorts differ in feedback source, scalar construction and tree (Methods).

Thus finer delivery need not improve learning even when the error field has spatial structure. Further depth and task-generator controls are in Supplementary Figs.~S6 and S9A--D, which cover input coverage, depth and the historical noise generators; the serial-depth half is Supplementary Fig.~S9E,F. We next introduce tasks that make specific spatial distinctions useful.

\subsection*{Branch-selective information prevents updates from inactive distractors}

Branch selection is useful when otherwise valid local activity comes from irrelevant inputs. We tested this with one logistic output and $B\in\{2,4,8\}$ parallel branch weight vectors and no internal forward compartments or conductance nonlinearity (Fig.~\ref{fig:branchconflict}A). Context selected the branch determining the output, while every branch received an image and formed an ungated local eligibility.

Inputs were average-pooled, standardized Fashion-MNIST images from classes 0 and 6. The selected image determined the binary target. At conflict dose \(\chi=0\), all branches received that image; increasing \(\chi\) independently replaced nonselected inputs with opposite-class images. For example \(i\), let \(b_i^\star\) be the selected branch, \(\bm x_{i,b}\) its input vector and \(y_i\in\{0,1\}\) the target. The branch weights \(\bm w_b\) give the pre-probability score (logit) and output error
\begin{equation}
z_i=\bm w_{b_i^\star}^{\mathsf T}\bm x_{i,b_i^\star},
\qquad \delta_i=\sigma(z_i)-y_i.
\label{eq:branchconflictforward}
\end{equation}
Here \(\sigma(z)=1/(1+e^{-z})\) is the logistic function. Unselected branches have zero exact derivative despite nonzero eligibility. Conflict therefore challenges a rule that ignores selection by exposing it to irrelevant, opposite-class eligibilities.

For \(N\) examples, the branch-specific and neuron-shared gradient estimates were
\begin{equation}
\bm d_b^{\rm selected}=N^{-1}\sum_i\delta_i\mathbf{1}[b_i^\star=b]\bm x_{i,b},
\qquad
\bm d_b^{\rm shared}=N^{-1}\sum_i\delta_i B^{-1}\bm x_{i,b}.
\label{eq:branchconflictupdates}
\end{equation}
The indicator $\mathbf{1}[\cdot]$ is one when its condition holds, zero otherwise; $1/B$ matches scale without supplying branch information. A feedback selector and a branch-local eligibility gate give the same update (Fig.~\ref{fig:branchconflict}B), testing access to selection rather than a need for independently computed external errors. We call these the branch-specific and neuron-shared rules. A deranged route instead delivers the same signal to the neighbouring branch at matched rank and sparsity (Fig.~\ref{fig:branchconflict}B, bottom row).

With centered, symmetric class evidence, the shared-mode prediction crosses zero at \(\chi_c=B/[2(B-1)]\): 1, \(2/3\) and \(4/7\) for two, four and eight branches (Fig.~\ref{fig:branchconflict}C; Methods). The measured initial signed utility of the neuron-shared update tracked this line to within 0.018 at all twenty-four sampled cells, although its bootstrap interval excluded the prediction in eleven of them (Fig.~\ref{fig:branchconflict}C). This is a statement about initialization: the trained chance crossing is a separate endpoint, displaced from the boundary because update alignment is lost during learning (Methods; Supplementary Fig.~S10B--D). It nonetheless followed the predicted order \(B=8<4<2\) in 20 of 20 seeds, and five two-branch seeds never reached chance within the sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:branchconflict}D). Selection also protects what has already been learned: in a separate two-stream interference task, neuron-shared credit erased a previously acquired context, losing 55.7 accuracy points \([53.8, 57.6]\), while branch-specific subtree routes lost none in all ten seeds (Fig.~\ref{fig:branchconflict}E; Supplementary Fig.~S10A).

At zero conflict, the branch-specific and neuron-shared rules achieved similar accuracy. Their difference increased with conflict, reaching 34.9--58.2 percentage points at full conflict across the tested branch counts (20 paired seeds; Fig.~\ref{fig:branchconflict}F--H). In every seed, the advantage of correct routing increased with conflict. A deranged route sent every signal to the wrong branch at matched rank and sparsity. It was harmful even at zero conflict, reducing accuracy by 12--25 points relative to branch-specific selection, whereas the neuron-shared rule was indistinguishable from branch-specific selection there (Fig.~\ref{fig:branchconflict}F--H, \(\chi=0\)); at high conflict the deranged rule settled below chance, near 22\% correct at every branch count, as did the neuron-shared rule at $B=4$ and $B=8$, while the two-branch neuron-shared rule reached 45.1\%. BP and the gated point matched branch-specific selection exactly in every seed, as required by their algebraic equivalence (Fig.~\ref{fig:branchconflict}B; Source Data; Supplementary Section~S3). Branch-count comparisons do not hold capacity fixed.

The neuron-shared rule reached chance at lower conflict as branch count increased, consistent with the predicted shift (Fig.~\ref{fig:branchconflict}D). Credit reaching inactive branches can therefore harm learning, and credit reaching the wrong branch harms it at every dose.

\subsection*{Ancestry provides a modest advantage at an intermediate feedback budget}

Between shared and branch-resolved feedback, nested subtree routes offer an intermediate dictionary. The task contained eight independent, eight-dimensional terminal weight blocks at the leaves of a balanced binary feedback tree. Only the context-selected block determined the logit. Internal tree nodes defined feedback supports and did not perform serial forward computation (Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}A).

Distractors opposed the selected stream's class signal increasingly with distance through the feedback hierarchy, deliberately favoring grouping that matches the task-to-route assignment. At each budget \(K\in\{1,2,4,8\}\), the routing field had \(K\) independent patterns and unit-length rows. Ancestry routes grouped the terminals into the whole tree, halves, sibling pairs or individual terminals (Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}B). Permuting task streams across leaves changed the task-to-route assignment without changing the streams or forward parameter budget. Here bandwidth counts independent feedback patterns, not physical stages or independent scalar losses; other single-pattern controls need not be uniform. The complete generator and update are given in Methods.

The generator predicts the budget at which ancestry can help. The selected stream has class coefficient one; its sibling, same-half nonsiblings and other-half streams have coefficients $-0.15$, $-0.45$ and $-0.75$. The unnormalized sum of the class signal within the selected ancestry group gives $-3.05$, $-0.05$, $0.85$ and $1$ at $K=1,2,4,8$, respectively. Coarse pooling reverses or nearly cancels the signal; sibling pairs first preserve a positive intermediate-group signal, and full resolution removes grouping differences. The implemented unit-row normalization divides these sums by the square root of group size, preserving their signs. This budget follows from the design, not an independently discovered optimum (Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}B, card footers).

Controls preserved rank while changing recipient assignment, support geometry or sparsity: cyclic derangement, depth-interleaving, random-sparse and dense random fields, whose delivered support of the cued stream's credit is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}B.

Increasing bandwidth improved correct routing, while derangement caused large losses (Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}C). At $K=1$ and $K=2$ both fell below chance, as the design predicts: pooling reverses the class signal within the cued group (Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}B). Matched-bandwidth partitions had coincident field capture, so capture does not explain the ordering (Supplementary Fig.~S11A). Degree- and depth-preserving rewiring removed 23.15 and 5.02 points at $K=2$ and $K=4$ but left fully shared and fully resolved ancestry routes exactly tied (Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}D).

The ancestry-specific effect was much smaller. Ancestry performed worse than the best matched control at low budgets, exceeded it by 1.27 percentage points at \(K=4\), and tied at full resolution. The paired \(K=4\) interval was 0.59--1.95 points, with 15/20 positive differences; a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test gave Holm-adjusted \(P=0.0101\) across four budgets. Ancestry also exceeded the dense rank-4, random-sparse and depth-interleaved controls by 2.31, 3.77 and 7.11 points, and the deranged control by 61.06 points, so the small best-of-four contrast reflects a maximum taken over controls rather than a weak effect (Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}E).

A separate twenty-seed cohort tested how supplied context cues could set the coefficients (Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}F). An encoder trained on 256 calibration cues with noise SD 0.5 and their four-route activation targets improved task accuracy over a frozen profile by 7.16 points, but remained 54.47 points below oracle routing. Exploratory delivery of only its most probable route reduced this noisy-cue gap to 16.26 points. With noise-free cues the correct route was identified at every calibration size, and the leakage cost then depended on calibration alone: soft coefficients lost 60.90 points with sixteen cues but only 0.31 points with 256, whereas hard selection matched the oracle in all twenty seeds. Leakage into incompatible routes therefore mattered when calibration was small, not whenever the readout was soft. Hard selection retained a deficit under noisy cues, and delaying either readout across independent contexts removed its benefit (Supplementary Fig.~S11B,C and Section~S3).

\subsection*{Cross-branch interactions change the spatial credit that learning uses}

Branch interactions can change credit's sign and magnitude between examples. We isolated this effect using targets with identical input-sensitivity spectra on the same compatible tree.

Eight independent inputs $x_i$ were equally likely to be $-1$ or $+1$. Each branch computed $u=a+bu_L+cu_R+du_Lu_R$ with four trainable coefficients. This multi-affine unit is affine in either child while the other is fixed. Pairwise targets summed four disjoint products, $f_M=\frac12\sum_{(i,j)\in M}s_{ij}x_ix_j$; quartic targets combined complementary quartets, $f_A=\frac12(s_A\prod_{i\in A}x_i+s_{\bar A}\prod_{i\notin A}x_i)$. Here $M$ partitions inputs into pairs, $A$ is one quartet, $\bar A$ its complement, and signs $s$ were independently sampled. Within each seed, targets shared a compatible balanced tree, input assignment, initial weights and examples (Fig.~\ref{fig:prospective}A). Both have input-sensitivity second moment $\E[\nabla_xf\nabla_xf^{\mathsf T}]=I_8/4$, giving identical strengths of independent sensitivity directions.

A scalar branch output cannot transmit several independently required input combinations to its parent. This communication constraint gives an exact compatibility criterion: at each subtree boundary, the matrix of interaction coefficients between inside and outside input products must have rank at most one after removing the constant inside row. The condition is necessary and sufficient across all boundaries; discarded singular values bound representation error (Methods; Supplementary Section~S4).

Logical rules make this forward constraint concrete. All fifteen binary trees over four inputs can represent AND, OR and parity exactly, but mixed rules can require particular input groupings. The rule $(a\,\mathrm{AND}\,b)\,\mathrm{OR}\,(c\,\mathrm{AND}\,d)$ needs only two serial stages, whereas $a\,\mathrm{AND}\,[b\,\mathrm{OR}\,(c\,\mathrm{AND}\,d)]$ needs three in this scalar model, despite containing the same two AND gates and one OR gate. These representability statements are independent of training (Supplementary Figs.~S14--S15). They identify possible morphologies before testing credit-dependent learning.

All credit rules used the same exact local eligibilities and scalar output error. We compared exact paths with unit broadcast and calibrated broadcast, whose six-site profile is calibrated from each student's mean path derivatives over 256 unlabeled initialization examples (Fig.~\ref{fig:prospective}B). Three development seeds selected learning rates with equal budgets; twenty fresh paired seeds tested the frozen choices. A separate nested target summed products over prefixes of two, four, six and eight inputs on its own compatible tree, which requires depth four and does not share the $I_8/4$ input spectrum (Supplementary Fig.~S12F).

Calibrated broadcast learned pairwise targets but remained inaccurate on quartic and nested targets (Fig.~\ref{fig:prospective}C--E). At the primary checkpoint (1,024 updates), mean normalized mean squared errors (NMSE) for exact path and calibrated broadcast were 0.0317 and 0.0236 on pairwise targets, 0.1516 and 0.9813 on quartic targets, and 0.0230 and 0.7690 on nested targets. NMSE divides squared prediction error by clean target variance. The predefined quartic-minus-pairwise difference in calibrated-broadcast-minus-exact gaps was 0.838 (95\% paired interval, 0.719--0.931), positive in all twenty seeds; a common learning rate gave 0.843 (0.727--0.935). Exact quartic learning was heterogeneous at this checkpoint: seventeen fits were near the noise floor and three remained stalled, and those three resolve one at a time between 1,024 and 4,096 updates (Fig.~\ref{fig:prospective}D). The lower pairwise mean for calibrated broadcast likewise reflected one slow exact fit; exact path performed better in fifteen of twenty seeds (Supplementary Figs.~S16--S17).

A balanced continuation of every algebraic trajectory to 16,384 updates tested whether these gaps persisted without retuning. All twenty selected-rate Adam exact quartic fits reached the noise floor by 4,096 updates, and the pairwise mean lag disappeared. At 16,384 updates, the quartic-minus-pairwise calibrated-broadcast deficit was 0.952 (95\% paired interval, 0.883--1.012) at endpoint states and 0.790 (0.691--0.880) at validation-selected states, positive in all twenty seeds at all four budgets (Fig.~\ref{fig:prospective}F). Calibrated-broadcast arms contacted coefficient bounds; exact-path arms did not. These are the same previously observed seeds and bounded dynamics, with common-rate, per-seed and optimizer controls (selected-rate Adam trajectories in Supplementary Fig.~S16A,C; Supplementary Figs.~S16--S17 and Table~S8).

The trained fields reveal a distinction missed by the input spectrum. Let $\bm q(x)$ contain the six nonsomatic derivatives of root output with respect to branch output; exact branch credit is scalar output error times $\bm q$. At the 1,024-step checkpoint, allowing an oracle amplitude per example, the best rank-one profile captured 99.7\% of exact-trained path-field energy for pairwise targets, but 46.1\% for quartic and 44.8\% for nested targets. The separation persisted at 16,384 updates: capture was approximately 100\%, 39.6\% and 40.5\%, respectively (Source Data). Reaching 95\% of the field energy took one fitted direction for pairwise targets and about five for quartic and nested targets. Loss-error weighting, eligibility weighting, site-normalized spectra and replay of 600 earlier fits preserved the original separation (Supplementary Section~S4). Uniform-profile capture did not (0.122, 0.169 and 0.176; Fig.~\ref{fig:prospective}G, leftmost category): a nearly one-dimensional field can have a signed, nonuniform direction. Positive fixed-profile scaling largely cancels in Adam, explaining calibrated/sign-only agreement; it does not explain the slower pairwise learning with changing exact paths.

The learned credit spectra help explain these input-matched targets' different tolerance of calibrated broadcast. The results favor example-dependent spatial credit without establishing that all six exact channels are necessary.

Additional controls delimit the result. Morphology also matters within a fixed rule: shuffling which input enters which leaf, at fixed tree shape and parameter count, raised exact-path NMSE by 0.575 (95\% paired interval, 0.557--0.592; positive in all twenty seeds) pooled over the three task families (Fig.~\ref{fig:prospective}H and Supplementary Fig.~S13C). Calibrated-broadcast Adam learned a compatible positive-conductance teacher accurately; broadcast learned aligned XOR-of-AND, whereas parity favored exact credit under the tested optimizer (Supplementary Figs.~S14--S15 and S18), and the 3,600-fit family-by-optimizer-by-profile screen behind these comparisons is in Supplementary Fig.~S13. Noisy-query interaction estimation also yielded useful trees, retained as supporting structure-design evidence in Supplementary Fig.~S36. Interaction structure therefore motivates specific credit comparisons rather than a universal monotonic resolution law.

\subsection*{Context-dependent branch tuning makes spatial credit useful in conductance trees}

Conductance trees provide a biophysical model for testing this distinction. All rules learned a seven-compartment teacher with monotonic branch responses: extended Adam training kept broadcast NMSE below 0.0006 despite a varying path field (Supplementary Fig.~S19). A separate construction tested context-selected branches with aligned or opposing feature tuning (Fig.~\ref{fig:conductancecredit}A).

Four terminal compartments fed two proximal compartments and a soma, using the directed E/I conductance balance (Methods). Both subtrees received the same positive feature pairs, $\exp(z_j)$ and $\exp(-z_j)$, for two independent latent inputs $z_j$. A binary context increased inhibitory conductance in one proximal compartment. Teacher excitation and inhibition produced increasing feature responses in both subtrees for the aligned task, but increasing responses in one and decreasing responses in the other for the opposed task. Teacher and student shared the circuit, ensuring representability. Students shared the teacher's nominal proximal and coupling gains, with independent Gaussian perturbations of SD 0.4 in log conductance; terminal gains started near one. All 24 conductances were plastic, and paired conditions shared initial weights and example streams.

At fixed weights, binary context generates at most two exact path profiles. Their gains are positive: context changes the relative weighting of the two distal subtrees, while proximal credit is context invariant. The nominal parent's conductance denominator changes by approximately eightfold. Thus branch selection is the relevant distinction, connecting this task to Fig.~\ref{fig:branchconflict}A,B.

To supply selection without oracle coefficients, a local rule delivered the somatic error to terminal parameters only when their parent was uninhibited, while retaining ungated error at proximal compartments and the soma. It used the supplied inhibitory context and exact local eligibilities, without evaluating exact paths or projecting an exact field. Two fixed distal profiles with oracle amplitudes and unit proximal credit supplied a separate capacity control; the four deliveries of the somatic error are drawn together in Fig.~\ref{fig:conductancecredit}B. An additional proximal profile in the earlier three-profile control therefore addresses proximal updates, rather than another context distinction.

Twenty new paired seeds tested the rule under a protocol frozen after exploratory results. All nine rules ran at three common rates through 16,384 updates; the primary rate and 4,096-update window were fixed before these outcomes. On opposed targets, mean held-out NMSE at validation-selected states within the primary window was $2.18\times10^{-5}$ for exact credit, $2.33\times10^{-5}$ for the local distal gate, $2.28\times10^{-5}$ for the two-profile oracle with unit proximal credit, and 0.487 for broadcast; the fixed-checkpoint broadcast trajectory reaches 0.522 at the 4,096-update rule of Fig.~\ref{fig:conductancecredit}D. On aligned targets, the corresponding exact, local-gate and broadcast errors were $7.53\times10^{-6}$, $1.06\times10^{-5}$ and $5.53\times10^{-4}$. The opposed-minus-aligned difference in broadcast-minus-gate error was 0.487 (95\% paired interval, 0.360--0.615), positive in all twenty seeds. It remained 0.468 (0.332--0.604) within the longer window (Fig.~\ref{fig:conductancecredit}E).

The controls identify what the gate must preserve. Reversing its distal selection gave opposed-task NMSE 0.949, with four of twenty fits never improving on their initialization. Applying it at proximal compartments as well gave 0.123 and prevented both proximal inhibitory gains from learning in every seed: their eligibility is nonzero only during inhibition, precisely when that gate closes, and the validation-selected state came within 256 updates, its fixed checkpoint reaching 0.553 (Fig.~\ref{fig:conductancecredit}F). The proper local rule changed all 24 parameters and succeeded across the three tested rates. The continuous, shunt-proportional shape of the same local distal gate reached NMSE $2.32\times10^{-5}$ under the same primary rate and window, within $1.4\times10^{-6}$ ($-1.7$ to $4.8\times10^{-6}$) of the exact path (Fig.~\ref{fig:conductancecredit}F). Both gates were tested with binary inhibitory contexts; continuously varying or noisy context remains untested.

The gate coarsens the exact rule's strong context-dependent gain ratio. At matched weights, broadcast mixes opposing context-conditioned updates despite a nonnegative per-example inner product with the exact gradient in every seed: in an earlier twenty-seed cohort the fraction of the distal gradient surviving context averaging was 0.64 for exact credit at both initial and broadcast-trained weights, but fell from 0.33 to 0.17 for the calibrated broadcast (Fig.~\ref{fig:conductancecredit}G). This supplies a local implementation given inhibitory context and somatic error, while leaving their biological origin open. The comparisons remain window- and parameter-bounded. Exact-path and local-distal-gate fits reached no conductance bound, whereas eighteen of twenty opposed broadcast fits had done so by 4,096 updates and all twenty by 16,384; the earlier wide-bound and rate controls remain in Supplementary Fig.~S20.

\subsection*{Task organization sets the benefit; budget sets the ranking}

The preceding comparisons hold a compatible tree fixed. Varying serial structure instead tests whether successive compartments help remove unwanted multiplicative changes in input strength. Physical depth $D_{\rm p}$ counts forward stages, whereas feedback resolution counts distinctions in the backward field.

Each network contained 64 somata and eight nonsomatic compartments per soma. D1 $[8]$, D2 $[2,3]$ and D3 $[2,1,2]$ distributed those compartments across one to three stages in the three- and four-tier comparisons while preserving contacts and trainable resources. The two-tier D2 tree used $[4,1]$, also with eight compartments. Branching vectors are ordered from soma to tips. A resource-identical grouped-point control connected all eight modules directly to the soma; raw-additive trees removed divisive conductance normalization. Flexible point networks provided additional, approximately parameter-matched comparisons (the ceiling of Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepth}D).

Distal excitation carried class information multiplied by nuisance gains. Inhibitory sensors reported those gains with sensor fidelity $\alpha$: zero retained the sensor distribution but removed trial-specific information, and one reported the factor exactly. With inhibitory reversal zero, the sensors entered the conductance denominator and could support successive divisions \citep{holt1997shunting,carandini2012normalization}. Nested factors acted over fine, coarse and global input groups; flat factors had equal-resolution supports; locally cancellable factors placed the needed ratio within each module (Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepth}B). Both nested and flat targets used commutative products; they differed in where factor information was available.

Task-family controls showed what serial organization contributed: at full sensor fidelity, exact-BP serial trees exceeded the resource-identical grouped-point control by 30.32 percentage points for nested gains (30.02--30.62) and 22.20 points for flat gains (21.73--22.65), and by exactly zero for locally available ratios, where all ten pairs were numerically identical. Removing trial-specific sensor information eliminated these benefits (Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepth}C). Within the nested three-tier task, aligned exact-BP accuracy rose from 61.0\% at D1 to 67.5\% at D2 and 91.8\% at D3, whereas the resource-identical grouped-point control stayed at 61.0\% at every depth, reversed sensor placement stayed between 60.6 and 61.1\%, and raw-additive trees fell to 51.9\%; a parameter-matched point network reached 99.0\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepth}D). Adding a fourth stage did not help: in the four-tier task D4 minus D3 was $-1.46$ points ($-1.74$ to $-1.16$), negative in all ten seeds, and the two-tier task was tested only at D1 and D2 (Supplementary Figs.~S22B,C and S24B). Serial structure was useful when the task required integrating factor information distributed across modules.

Credit comparisons within this useful forward architecture depended on training duration. Ten paired seeds compared D1 exact BP with five D3 credit/optimization conditions for up to 600 epochs, retaining the original rates, parameter groups and thirty-epoch validation patience. Because optimizer and random states were not archived, these are restarts of the original configurations and seeds rather than checkpoint continuations, which is why the 180-epoch D3-minus-D1 gain reads 30.82 points here and 30.86 in the original-budget fits (Supplementary Figs.~S22--S24 and Table~S9). At validation-selected states from the same trajectories, exact-BP D3 exceeded D1 by 30.82 points at 180 epochs and 38.17 at 600 (Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepth}E). Eight of ten D1 references stopped early, whereas all fifty D3 fits reached the cap with validation loss still falling (Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepth}F). The forward benefit is therefore budget-specific, with unequal optimization maturity across depths.

Within D3, the LocalCA exact-path-minus-shared-soma accuracy gap changed from $+10.86$ points at 180 epochs to $-1.52$ at 600 (95\% paired interval, $-1.83$ to $-1.23$), with this budget-indexed sign change in all ten seeds. The mean gap remained negative from epoch 315, reached $-2.94$ points at epoch 486, and moved back towards zero by 600; the pointwise interval straddles zero intermittently between epochs 300 and 325, so the crossing epoch is not resolved (Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepth}G). Exact-path LocalCA nevertheless retained lower mean cross-entropy over epochs 180--600, its advantage decaying from $-0.073$ to $-0.021$ nats and ending at 0.254 versus 0.276, negative in nine of ten seeds at 600 (Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepth}H). Accuracy counts threshold crossings, whereas cross-entropy also reflects predicted probabilities. Under the BP recipe, the 600-epoch exact-minus-broadcast accuracy gap was 0.48 points (0.40--0.55), positive in all ten seeds (Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepth}E). Thus serial organization and spatial credit answer distinct questions: distributed factor information supports the forward architecture, while the eventual ranking of the tested credit rules remains unresolved (Supplementary Figs.~S22--S24 and Table~S9).

\subsection*{Reconstructed arbors provide spatial routes beyond a shared broadcast}

The learning tasks motivate asking what delivery patterns real arbors supply. MICrONS links reconstructed anatomy and connectivity to visual responses \citep{microns2025}. Observed skeletons, soma positions and mapped contact locations and areas define the geometry; conductances, voltages and gradient fields are modeled.

Each inhibitory-bearing segment supplied a candidate route over its input-bearing descendants (Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}A). These supports form dictionary columns, supplemented by a constant column for common broadcast; in the median-sized initial cell the seven selected routes nest and cover 32 of its 70 input-bearing sites (Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}B). For a site-by-route matrix $A_K$, diagonal excitatory-area weights $W$ and weighted least-squares projector $P_{A_K,W}$, capture of a field $\bm t$ was
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C(A_K;\bm t)=\frac{\|P_{A_K,W}\bm t\|_W^2}{\|\bm t\|_W^2},
\qquad \|\bm v\|_W^2=\bm v^{\mathsf T}W\bm v.
\label{eq:reconstructioncapture}
\end{equation}
For an ensemble of perturbations, numerator and denominator sum over field columns. The field is the modeled gradient change after one small focal shunt, and capture is the fraction of its weighted energy inside the dictionary span (Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}C). A common-constrained SVD dictionary provided a ceiling at the same column budget. Controls used random anatomical routes, cable-distance bins, shuffled routes preserving nonzero counts, and surrogate trees preserving degree and depth while changing ancestry.

Small focal shunts in reciprocal cables generated gradient-change fields without inspecting the selected dictionary. These fields nevertheless favor ancestry by construction under a somatic loss, as derived below. The test asks how a small dictionary compresses the tree's own passive responses. In the initial eight-cell cohort, broadcast captured 31.4\% of field energy, near the 32.6\% rank-one ceiling (Source Data). Every dictionary therefore contained one common broadcast and $K-1$ spatial profiles, with the protocol fixed before evaluation in 47 disjoint cells from the same mouse and a reconstructed second mouse.

Varying $K$ separates the common signal from the additional spatial information each dictionary can capture (Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}D). At eight columns in the disjoint 47-cell cohort a shared broadcast captured 0.203 of total energy and broadcast-plus-ancestry 0.619, against 0.578 for degree--depth matched surrogate trees, 0.511 for depth bins and 0.436 for shuffled routes (Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}D); the spatial share beyond that broadcast is 0.416 for ancestry against 0.376, 0.309 and 0.233 for the three controls (Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}E). Cell by cell, ancestry exceeded its own surrogate mean in 38 of 47 cells, but in 11 cells at least half of the 200 surrogate trees matched or exceeded the actual tree (Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}F). The paired ancestry-minus-surrogate advantage was 0.041 of total energy (95\% cell-bootstrap interval, 0.020--0.062) and 5.08 percentage points of post-broadcast energy (2.44--7.68); against depth bins it was 0.108 (0.077--0.135) and 13.51 points (9.86--16.84), positive in 45 cells, with shuffled and random routes at 23.06 and 27.17 points (Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}G). Actual ancestry improves average compression within this passive response family, while coarse geometry explains much of the capacity.

Capture and delivery cost are separate constraints (Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}G, right columns and footer). The eight-column ancestry dictionary used 18.75\% of the entries of a fully dense dictionary on average---12.5\% for the always-dense broadcast column and 6.25\% for the seven route columns---and its shuffled-routes control preserved exactly the same nonzero count at much lower capture. Equal column count does not ensure equal rank, and nonzero entries are a delivery-cost proxy rather than feedback synapses or metabolic expenditure.

The ancestry-over-surrogate-over-depth ordering held in the initial eight-cell cohort and in the Pinky second-mouse cohort, where eight of ten qualifying cells supported the eight-column comparison (Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}H); the rank of the random-route control, by contrast, exchanged with depth bins between cohorts. Pinky's 9--13 input-bearing sites put ancestry near the residual-capture ceiling, so lower-budget comparisons and exclusions remain separate from the larger arbors. The initial and disjoint cohorts come from one MICrONS mouse; Pinky is a second animal. Geometry, labeling and compression sensitivities, including direct-label coverage and direct-versus-proxy agreement, accompany the cohort controls in Supplementary Figs.~S25--S28, with the wiring-density reconciliation in Supplementary Fig.~S25A, the coarse-energy controls in Supplementary Fig.~S27B,C and the preprocessing audit in Supplementary Fig.~S28A; there, no inhibitory-targeting endpoint persists after joint three-dimensional matching (Supplementary Fig.~S26B--D), and the initial cohort's anatomy-specific coarse-energy excess does not replicate in the disjoint 47-cell cohort (Supplementary Fig.~S27D).

\subsection*{Focal shunting sets ancestry-defined route gains with selectivity set by electrotonic state}

A focal shunt provides an exact link between anatomical routes and conductance-dependent gains. We compared a focal inhibitory shunt with a current injection matching its baseline focal current in reconstructed reciprocal cables. A separately solved somatic current restored baseline somatic voltage after either intervention (Fig.~\ref{fig:focal}A). The endpoint was the change in the modeled excitatory-conductance gradient at open excitatory contacts.

Let \(G\) be the passive conductance matrix and \(\bm b\) the current-source vector, so that baseline voltages satisfy \(G\bm V=\bm b\). With \(\bm e_k\) the unit vector at compartment \(k\), adding inhibitory conductance \(\kappa_k\) at compartment \(k\) changes both \(G\) and its source: \(G'=G+\kappa_k\bm e_k\bm e_k^{\mathsf T}\), \(\bm b'=\bm b+\kappa_kE_{\rm I}\bm e_k\). The matched injection supplies baseline current \(\kappa_k(E_{\rm I}-V_k)\) without changing \(G\). The adjoint $\bm q$ gives loss sensitivity to current injected at each compartment. For a fixed somatic loss derivative, it changes by
\begin{equation}
\bm q'=\bm q-\frac{\kappa_kq_k}{1+\kappa_k(G^{-1})_{kk}}G^{-1}\bm e_k.
\label{eq:sherman}
\end{equation}
The column \(G^{-1}\bm e_k\) gives the voltage response to unit current at \(k\). On a passive tree with a somatic loss, the resulting adjoint change has an exact ancestry structure: all descendants of the shunt share the attenuation factor \([1+\kappa_k(G^{-1})_{kk}]^{-1}\), while each sister block along the path to the soma shares its own, and the soma-side remainder a fifth factor (Fig.~\ref{fig:focal}B; Supplementary Section~S8). These disjoint blocks form an ancestry partition. Weighting their indicators by the baseline soma-to-site transfer gives a dictionary on which the shunt acts through diagonal gains, providing a physical instance of \(\Gamma_u\), drawn as the block indicator matrix and its gain column in Fig.~\ref{fig:focal}B. The diagonal form applies in this partition basis; it need not hold for an arbitrary selected set of overlapping routes.

In the small-perturbation fields used for Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}, the driving-force term was substantial (0.99--3.50 times the full field's weighted squared energy across the initial eight-cell cohort) and opposed the adjoint term (weighted correlations $-0.979$ to $-0.913$), yet 97.1--99.9\% of full-field energy remained within each focal ancestry partition (Supplementary Section~S8).

We compared descendants \(D(k)\) with depth-matched off-route sites \(C(k)\); Supplementary Section~S8 gives a kernel criterion for stronger absolute adjoint changes below a shunt, with the within-cell control in Supplementary Fig.~S29B and the contact-label and reversal-potential controls in Supplementary Fig.~S29E,F.
The analyzed gradient was sensitivity to open excitatory conductance, \(\gamma_i=q_i(E_{\rm E}-V_i)\). The local driving force \(E_{\rm E}-V_i\) can change differently within each block, so the complete synaptic gradient need not share a single block gain. With primes denoting post-intervention values and \(\epsilon_\gamma\) a numerical floor defined in Methods, we measured absolute log change, \(m_i=|\log[(|\gamma_i'|+\epsilon_\gamma)/(|\gamma_i|+\epsilon_\gamma)]|\), and localization \(\Lambda_k=\operatorname{median}_{D(k)}m_i-\operatorname{median}_{C(k)}m_i\). Positive localization means a larger gradient change below the shunt.

In the permissive normalized model, shunts changed descendant gradients more strongly than depth-matched off-route gradients, with increasing localization at higher dose (Fig.~\ref{fig:focal}C,D). Current injection changed descendant gradients roughly threefold less. Its unrelated-site changes were zero by construction under the soma-restoring control, and its depth-matched off-route changes averaged \(2.7\times10^{-4}\) at unit dose (cells to \(8.8\times10^{-4}\)); this is not a general claim that current injection cannot change a gradient. Holding the post-shunt voltage but replacing its adjoint with the baseline adjoint removed most localization, supporting a transport contribution beyond the voltage change alone (Fig.~\ref{fig:focal}E). Adding the post-shunt adjoint on top of the substituted driving force (\(q'd'-d'\)) added 0.079 log units of localization, positive in all eight cells, while substituting the driving force alone left 0.026 against 0.104 for the full shunt.

Physical calibration constrained the spatial selectivity of the gradient change. At axial resistivity \(R_a=150\,\Omega\,\mathrm{cm}\) and specific membrane resistance \(R_m=15{,}000\,\Omega\,\mathrm{cm}^2\), mean shunt-minus-current localization was small and opposite-signed in the initial eight cells and a disjoint 45-cell cohort from the same mouse: \(-0.0019\) and \(+0.0024\), respectively. Signed changes showed attenuation of both descendant and off-route gradients at high resistance; lowering resistance primarily increased descendant attenuation (Fig.~\ref{fig:focal}F), whereas the matched injection enhanced them (\(+0.0099\) at \(R_m=15{,}000\), \(+0.043\) at \(R_m=300\), initial eight cells). This increased the localization contrast by more than an order of magnitude (Fig.~\ref{fig:focal}G). Distributed background conductance produced the same qualitative recovery at fixed high membrane resistance (Fig.~\ref{fig:focal}H; \(-0.0004\), \(+0.121\) and \(+0.259\) at background leak zero, one and four times baseline, positive in eight of eight cells at one and four), consistent with a dependence on electrotonic state \citep{chance2002gain,destexhe2003highconductance}.

Adding weak voltage-dependent channel currents produced a dose response close to the passive response at $R_m=1,000\,\Omega\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ with one baseline-leak unit of distributed background conductance (Supplementary Section~S8; Fig.~S30D,E). This checks the local linearization around solved steady states. The baseline Jacobian included gate derivatives but remained fixed across doses, so the check does not establish robustness to strongly active dendrites or channel kinetics.

The partition identity and electrical calibration connect anatomical dictionaries to conductance-dependent route gains \citep{gidon2012inhibition}. Their selectivity depends on cable state. These imposed perturbations establish modeled transport, not endogenous error production or plasticity.

\subsection*{Measured responses bound the anatomical alignment proposal}

Measured responses test whether functional relationships follow the ancestry structure suggested by modeled capacity and transport. Seven MICrONS targets had complete arbors and imaged partners: 69 partners in the selected-scan dataset and thirteen eligible scans in the scan-complete analysis. These recordings sampled stimulus responses rather than learning, so no credit was delivered (Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}A).

Partners with more shared dendritic ancestry did not have reliably more similar condition-mean responses after controlling Euclidean separation and soma-to-contact path-length differences (shared-path comparison, Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}A; estimate, Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}B). Mean partial rank correlations were $-0.069$ for selected scans and $-0.048$ for all eligible scans, the same seven targets re-analyzed, and both target-bootstrap intervals included zero (Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}B,C). The null did not depend on the measure: raw shared-path ($-0.025$), tree-distance ($-0.017$) and same-major-branch ($-0.003$) associations were also centered on zero (Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}C; Supplementary Fig.~S31). This provides no evidence of preferential ancestry alignment in the sampled partners, at a different scale from population-level like-to-like wiring \citep{ding2025wiring}.

To establish the sensitivity of this small sample, response-level simulations preserved observed partners, shared inputs, repeated stimuli and seven-target inference, with noise calibrated to measured repeat reliabilities. Under the specified Gaussian ancestry-signal model, 80\% detection required a mean simulated target-level partial rank correlation of approximately 0.25 (0.249 by interpolation, or $\lambda=0.65$ once the Monte Carlo interval is respected), and the observed estimate and its whole interval lie below that point (Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}D). With perfect reliability the same threshold falls only to 0.248, so the limit is the seven-target sample rather than measurement noise. The observed repeat reliabilities used for attenuation vary across recordings (Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}D, inset). This conditional threshold leaves weaker effects, different signal geometries and unobserved partners unresolved (Methods; Supplementary Section~S9).

A separate passive-tree model predicted each target's responses from its recorded partners. Its selected ancestry routes reached only 48.0\% of mapped inputs, and in six of the thirteen scans every route reached a single input (Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}E,F), so restricting the transfer profile to these supports tests transfer geometry more than example-dependent credit, and no measured positive control exists for this comparison. Held-out prediction, input-removal and reconstruction controls are grouped in SI (Supplementary Figs.~S31--S32), where the ancestry-minus-ridge difference (0.029; 95\% target-bootstrap interval, $-0.013$ to 0.074) is matched by random anatomical routes (0.031; $-0.009$ to 0.068). This model diagnostic and the measured ancestry-similarity test constrain different parts of the anatomical proposal.

\section*{Discussion}

Learning benefits from spatial selection when a common signal mixes incompatible synaptic changes. Dendritic morphology supplies candidate delivery patterns, teaching mechanisms supply their coefficients, and conductance regulates their gains. In the single-layer image classifiers, preserving neuronal identity supplies most of the benefit, including with random between-neuron feedback. Contextual conflict favors branch selection, supplied through feedback or local eligibility gating. Hierarchical distractors favor ancestry at a bandwidth predicted by the generator's coefficients. These results distinguish useful information from its physical carrier.

The interaction comparison explains why input rank alone is insufficient: pairwise and quartic targets share that spectrum and a compatible tree, yet learn different credit fields with different tolerance of fixed profiles. Spatial direction matters alongside dimension: even a nearly one-dimensional field can be poorly captured by uniform broadcast. These learned-state properties do not rescue the failed initialization selector. Intermediate dictionaries, adaptive profiles and alternative dynamics remain plausible solutions.

Conductance trees connect this distinction to computation: simple credit learns compatible monotonic teachers, whereas opposed tuning under contextual inhibition benefits from selection. A local inhibitory gate supplies that selection without oracle path coefficients, provided proximal credit remains ungated. The task supplies context and somatic error. Forward compatibility is a separate constraint: the scalar multi-affine cut criterion connects input grouping to hierarchical tensor descriptions and supports structure estimation from noisy examples \citep{hackbusch2009tensor,grasedyck2010hierarchical,cohen2016tensor}. Serial composition likewise helps remove distributed nuisance factors, but the LocalCA accuracy advantage of exact credit changes sign between the 180- and 600-epoch budgets while exact credit retains lower cross-entropy. The gap is still moving at the cap; forward depth and backward resolution need separate interpretation.

Obtaining coefficients remains a limitation beyond this explicit context gate. The separate encoder experiment exposes two obstacles: identifying a route from noisy cues and, when calibration is small, preventing coefficient leakage into incompatible routes even after identification is correct (Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}F). With 256 noise-free cues the soft readout came within 0.31 points of the oracle, so leakage is a calibration cost rather than an intrinsic property of a soft readout. Exploratory hard selection removes it at every calibration size, but leaves a substantial noisy-cue deficit. Exact paths and projected coefficients elsewhere remain information-access controls. An adequate span alone does not ensure useful delivery.

Reconstructed arbors compress their modeled passive response fields beyond a common broadcast. Degree--depth surrogates explain much of this capacity, and the target fields themselves favor ancestry through passive transport under a somatic loss. Testing alignment beyond that construction requires measured fields or a model with different error sources or active dynamics; changing the passive perturbation family alone would not suffice. Focal shunting supplies a biophysical link by reweighting the baseline-weighted ancestry partition. Driving-force changes partly cancel adjoint changes, while cable state sets the spatial selectivity of the resulting gradient. This concerns sensitivity transport, distinct from inhibition that computes errors or regulates forward gain and load \citep{rossbroich2023disinhibitory,galloni2026cellular,greedy2026celltype,safaai2026gainload}.

The small recorded cohort provided no evidence of preferential ancestry alignment; the offline learning diagnostic chiefly tests an almost fixed transfer profile. A direct experiment would compare plasticity below a focal inhibitory site with electrotonically matched off-route sites while matching somatic voltage, first-order current and outcome signal. Changing conductance state would test the predicted separation between shared and ancestry-selective effects \citep{francioni2026vectorized,cichon2015branch,bloss2016structured}.

The framework complements somatic prediction, segregated teaching, interneuron-mediated errors and burst plasticity \citep{urbanczik2014dendritic,guerguiev2017segregated,sacramento2018dendritic,payeur2021burst}, alongside cable-learning and engineered gating models \citep{bicknell2021synaptic,chavlis2025dendrites,sezener2021gated,iyer2022activedendrites,lv2025dendritic}. Spikes, temporal eligibility, recurrence and channel kinetics may change the available profiles and coefficient estimation \citep{bittner2017btsp,magee2020plasticity}. The organizing question remains which task-relevant distinctions the arbor and learning mechanism can jointly preserve.

\section*{Methods}

Resolved configurations, seeds, checkpoints, exclusions and source hashes accompany the numerical tables. Diagnostics reusing a cohort retain its original independent units.

\subsection*{Conductance models and delivery coordinates}

In a directed tree, $[b_1,\ldots,b_D]$ lists branching factors from soma to tips, giving $\sum_{d=1}^D\prod_{r=1}^db_r$ nonsomatic compartments. A child $c$ supplies output $a_c=f_c(V_c)$. With leak $g_n^{\rm L}$, aggregate open excitatory/inhibitory conductances $G_n^X=\sum_{i\in\mathcal S_n^X}g_i^Xx_i^X$ for contact class $X$, and child couplings $g_{c\to n}^{\rm den}$, the steady voltage is
\begin{equation}
V_n=R_n^{\rm tot}\left(g_n^{\rm L}E_{\rm L}+G_n^{\rm E}E_{\rm E}+G_n^{\rm I}E_{\rm I}+\sum_cg_{c\to n}^{\rm den}a_c\right),
\quad (R_n^{\rm tot})^{-1}=g_n^{\rm L}+G_n^{\rm E}+G_n^{\rm I}+\sum_cg_{c\to n}^{\rm den}.
\label{eq:directedvoltage}
\end{equation}
Here $x_i\geq0$ is contact activity, $g_i\geq0$ its conductance, and $E_X$ its reversal potential. A terminal-to-soma pass evaluates the directed steady state. Artificial conductance trees use dimensionless leak one and reversals $E_{\rm L}=E_{\rm I}=0$, $E_{\rm E}=1$. Monotone parameter transforms enforce nonnegative conductances. Branch-output nonlinearities, termed reactivations in the implementation, are identity functions when disabled. Both excitatory and inhibitory contacts learn in the regular-tree experiments.

For a reciprocal cable residual $\bm F(\bm V,\bm g,\bm x)=0$, the nonsingular voltage Jacobian $J_V$ gives the adjoint
\begin{equation}
J_V^{\mathsf T}\bm q=\nabla_{\bm V}\mathcal L,
\qquad \partial\mathcal L/\partial g_i=-\bm q^{\mathsf T}\partial\bm F/\partial g_i.
\label{eq:generaladjoint}
\end{equation}
With passive current balance, the synaptic gradient is $x_i(E_i^{\rm rev}-V_n)q_n$. This established sensitivity calculation supplies the reference for restricted credit \citep{almeida1987asynchronous,pineda1987recurrent,schiess2016somatodendritic,bicknell2021synaptic}. For output $a_n=f_n(V_n)$, activation error $\delta^a_n=\partial\mathcal L/\partial a_n$ gives voltage error $\delta^V_n=f_n'(V_n)\delta^a_n$. Image LocalCA replaces $\delta^a_n$ while retaining $f_n'$ and exact local gate derivatives in eligibility. Fixed activation-space profiles can therefore vary in voltage space; exact supplied fields recover exact selected gradients.

The raw-additive control retains the graph but sums signed inputs without the conductance denominator or reversal-potential driving force. Historical normalized-additive controls additionally standardize voltage across stage coordinates within each example; Supplementary Section~S2 defines those variants. The optional four- and five-factor rules add stage-level preconditioners and are described in Supplementary Section~S1.

In $A\Gamma$, nonzero diagonal gains preserve the dictionary span and oracle capture while changing coefficients and conditioning (Supplementary Fig.~S5B--D). This describes a passive shunt in its baseline-weighted ancestry partition, not arbitrary overlapping dictionaries. Stage count, feedback rank, external error dimension and state-dependent path resolution remain distinct (Supplementary Tables~S1--S2).

\subsection*{Image feedback ladders and checkpoint diagnostics}

The trained decoder supplies somatic activation errors $\delta_{i,u}=\partial\mathcal L/\partial a_{i,0,u}$ for example $i$ and neuron $u$. Strict scalar feedback uses their mean, or their largest-magnitude signed coordinate when the mean's magnitude is at most $10^{-6}$, throughout the core including somatic gates. Per-neuron feedback preserves each coordinate; the exact path additionally applies local path derivatives. The historical matched-width fallback retained neuronal identity at the 128-wide somatic stage, affecting 640 of 2,413,312 core parameters. A separately trained strict-scalar control tests that exception (Supplementary Fig.~S2, Supplementary Section~S2 and Source Data indexed in Table~S12).

The new MNIST ladder uses 128-soma $[3,3]$ shunting and raw-additive networks, with 40 excitatory and 20 inhibitory contacts per nonsomatic compartment. Within each architecture, rules share initial contact construction, initialization policy, decoder and data streams. Pixels are divided by 255 and flattened without further normalization or augmentation. Each seed divides the 60,000 training images into 48,000 training and 12,000 validation examples; all 10,000 official test images remain held out. Training uses 180 epochs, batches of 256 and the validation-selected state (minimum validation cross-entropy), without early stopping. \subsection*{Contextual conflict and ancestry routing}

The branch-conflict task uses Eqs.~\ref{eq:branchconflictforward}--\ref{eq:branchconflictupdates}, with independent opposite-class replacements of nonselected Fashion-MNIST inputs at probability $\chi$. Twenty paired seeds cross $B=2,4,8$ with eight doses. Gated-point and BP calculations receive equivalent updates; A deranged route changes recipients at fixed rank and support. A separate two-stream interference cohort (ten paired seeds 4100--4109, $K=2$ subtree routes, two contexts) trains on context 0, switches to context 1 for twelve epochs and re-tests context 0 on 1,024 held-out examples; the retained loss is reported as context-switch forgetting (\texttt{source\_data/trained\_subtree\_address/}).

Under centered, symmetric class evidence, shared-mode compatibility is $s(\chi)=1-2\chi(B-1)/B$, with zero at $\chi_c=B/[2(B-1)]$. Trained crossings are evaluated separately by mean-curve interpolation and each seed's first sampled at-or-below-chance dose, retaining non-crossings. Checkpoint re-execution reproduces endpoints and compares exact/shared fields at both common and own trained states (Supplementary Fig.~S10B--D). The measured counterpart of $s(\chi)$, reported as the initial signed utility, is the Source Data column \texttt{mean\_initial\_signed\_utility}: the initialization-state inner product of the neuron-shared update with the exact update, signed and normalized so that the analytic $s(\chi)$ is its prediction. It is distinct from the nonnegative capture $U$ reported elsewhere.

The ancestry factorial contains eight eight-dimensional blocks. Context $c_i$ and binary label sign $s_i$ are balanced, and inputs are $\bm x_{i,b}=a(b,c_i)s_i\bm t_b+\bm\epsilon_{i,b}$, with seed-specific unit teachers $\bm t_b$ and Gaussian noise $\bm\epsilon_{i,b}\sim\mathcal N(0,1.1^2I_8)$. The selected coefficient is one; sibling, same-half nonsibling and opposite-half coefficients are $-0.15,-0.45,-0.75$. A leaf permutation changes the task-to-route assignment without changing the streams. For budget $K$, ancestry feedback has context-by-block entries $\Phi^{(K)}_{cb}=\sqrt{K/8}\,\mathbf1[g_K(\pi(c))=g_K(b)]$, where $g_K$ indexes contiguous groups and $\pi$ maps streams to leaves. Thus every row has unit Euclidean norm.

The update is $\Delta\bm w_b=-(8\eta/N)\sum_i\delta_i\Phi^{(K)}_{c_ib}\bm x_{i,\pi^{-1}(b)}$, with $N$ training examples and rate $\eta$. Each seed supplies 2,048 training, 2,048 test and 1,024 switch examples. Full-batch descent uses rate 0.3, eighty training epochs and twenty switch epochs. Controls include deranged ancestry, depth-interleaved and random-sparse supports, dense rank-$K$ fields and a training-informed PCA reference. The best matched control is the per-seed post-training maximum over the four non-PCA controls. Integer test counts preserve ties in the named Wilcoxon comparison; Holm correction covers four budgets. Alternative mean-sign-flip and historical BH analyses remain explicitly labeled in the Source Data indexed in Supplementary Table~S12. The separate noisy-context encoder and cue-delay experiments are fully specified in Supplementary Section~S3.

\subsection*{Update utility and prospective selection}

Let the exact gradient be $\bm\mu$ and the delivered update estimator have mean $\bm m$ and covariance $\Sigma_{\rm route}$ at the current weights. For an $L$-smooth loss and a nonnegative step $\eta$, expected loss after the update is bounded above by $\mathcal L-\eta\bm\mu^{\mathsf T}\bm m+(L\eta^2/2)(\|\bm m\|^2+\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{\rm route})$. Optimizing this expression gives utility $U=[\bm\mu^{\mathsf T}\bm m]_+^2/[2L(\|\bm m\|^2+\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{\rm route})]$, with zero for a zero denominator. Utility certifies one-step progress under the stated moments; it is not a long-horizon ordering.

Synthetic quadratic screens use sixteen Haar coordinates and a designed signal/noise crossover, without a forward neuron (Supplementary Fig.~S3). Retrospective factorial utility uses actual full-batch updates and a global logistic-curvature bound; maximum bandwidth matches its selection success. Supplementary Figs.~S34--S35 retain the failed 12,800-fit prospective selector, its loss to fixed maximum-budget and rank-only baselines in every seed, and the 25,600-fit dynamics extension with evolving noise, observed-context-count controls, sealed choices; the computational-cost accounting is indexed in Supplementary Table~S12.

\subsection*{Interaction compatibility, fixed profiles and learned spectra}

For a subtree leaf set $S$, $C_S$ arranges the target's Fourier interaction coefficients by products inside and outside $S$; $C_S^\circ$ removes the constant inside row. Scalar multi-affine trees represent the target exactly if and only if every centered cut has rank at most one. If $\mathcal S(T)$ contains all proper cuts of tree $T$ and singular values are ordered from largest to smallest,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{MSE}(f,\widehat f_T)\geq B(T;f):=\max_{S\in\mathcal S(T)}\sum_{j\geq2}\sigma_j(C_S^\circ)^2.
\label{eq:morphologyselection}
\end{equation}
Overlapping cut tails cannot be added as a lower bound. Supplementary Section~S4 gives the constructive proof, minimum-depth algorithm and its relation to hierarchical tensor representations. The twelve-tree census with its candidate-selection outcome, the noisy-query estimator, end-to-end learning pipeline, conductance grouping controls and Boolean truth tables remain in Supplementary Figs.~S12 (selection in panel~C), S14--S15, S18 and S36. The theorem's assumptions do not extend automatically to general conductance ancestors.

Three development seeds select rates with equal budgets; twenty fresh seed blocks pair matching and quartic conditions across trees, assignments, initialization, inputs, additive noise and minibatch order. Exact, unit, calibrated and sign-only profiles share local eligibilities; calibration averages path derivatives over 256 unlabeled initialization examples. Selected/common rates under SGD and Adam yield 432 development and 1,200 fresh fits. Coefficients stay within $[-2,2]$, with bound contacts recorded. A balanced extension replays all seven original checkpoints through 1,024 steps, then continues all 720 unique selected/common-rate algebraic trajectories to 16,384. Terminal and validation-selected extensions describe the same seeds (Supplementary Section~S4). Noisy held-out NMSE divides by clean target variance: one for matching and nested, one-half for quartic. All have noise variance 0.0225. The primary Adam contrast subtracts the pairwise calibrated-minus-exact gap from its quartic counterpart.

For evaluation-example-by-site path matrix $Q$, the best rank-one profile is the leading right singular vector. Its capture is $\sigma_1(Q)^2/\sum_j\sigma_j(Q)^2$, an oracle reconstruction with example-dependent amplitudes. Additional diagnostics evaluate uniform, calibrated and ancestry profiles, loss-residual weighting, eligibility-weighted updates and root-mean-square normalization of each site. Participation-ratio effective rank is $(\operatorname{tr}C)^2/\operatorname{tr}(C^2)$ for uncentered $C=Q^{\mathsf T}Q/N$. At fixed weights and inputs, changing labels leaves the path field unchanged; eighty relabeling controls verify this. Independent checkpoint analysis and faithful replay of 600 earlier fits provide numerical and trajectory checks. Complete definitions, pairing audits, optimizer effects and the positive-conductance boundary are in Supplementary Sections~S4--S5.

Students start from terminal gains one, proximal inhibitory gains 15, daughter couplings 20 and 0.2 per subtree, and soma couplings four. \subsection*{Serial depth, sensor fidelity and optimization}

Physical-depth networks contain 64 somata and eight nonsomatic modules each. D1--D4 reallocate these modules across branching vectors $[8]$, $[2,3]$, $[2,1,2]$ and $[1,1,2,2]$ in the three- and four-tier comparisons; the two-tier D2 tree uses $[4,1]$. \subsection*{Focal shunting and the electrotonic boundary}

Up to sixteen focal sites per cell span depth quartiles and require at least three excitatory descendants and three comparison sites. Normalized E/I scales are 0.35 with reversals one and $-0.2$. Shunt doses are 0.25, 0.5, one and two times local membrane-plus-synaptic conductance. A matched injection supplies first-order current $\kappa_k(E_{\rm I}-V_k)$ without changing the conductance matrix. A separately solved somatic current restores baseline voltage and is held fixed during differentiation. The squared-loss target is one normalized unit below baseline; finite differences check the adjoint gradients.

Localization subtracts depth-matched off-route from descendant medians of $|\log[(|\gamma_i'|+\epsilon)/(|\gamma_i|+\epsilon)]|$, with $\epsilon=10^{-15}\max(1,\max_i|\gamma_i|)$. Because this is unsigned, signed gradient changes are reported separately. Adjoint and driving-force substitutions in $\gamma_i=q_i(E_{\rm E}-V_i)$ distinguish transport from local voltage effects. Sites and relation-reassignment templates are nested within cells. The passive ancestry-partition identity and its dictionary qualification are derived in Supplementary Section~S8.

Physical calibration uses cylindrical axial conductance $\pi r^2/(R_a\ell)$ and leak $2\pi r\ell/R_m$, expressed in nS, with spherical soma area $4\pi r^2$. Here $r$ and $\ell$ are segment radius and length, $R_a$ axial resistivity and $R_m$ specific membrane resistance. Sweeps include $R_a=100,150,250\,\Omega\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $R_m=300$--$30,000\,\Omega\,\mathrm{cm}^2$, fixed and locally normalized doses, and background conductance. The disjoint calibration cohort contains 235 eligible sites in 45 cells. Geometric fallbacks and dose definitions are specified in Supplementary Section~S8.

The weak-channel check uses $R_a=150$, $R_m=1,000$ in those units and background conductance equal to leak. Median maximal Na, K, Ca and HCN conductances are 0.06, 0.10, 0.025 and 0.05 times leak; NMDA is 0.15 times excitatory conductance. Sixty-four draws per cell passed the predefined steady-state checks. Responses use rank-one updates of the fixed baseline Jacobian, including voltage-gate derivatives, followed by somatic state matching. They are local linearizations rather than newly solved nonlinear equilibria at each dose (Supplementary Fig.~S30D,E).
