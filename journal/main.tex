\begin{abstract}
Learning requires neurons to assign circuit-level errors to individual
synapses, yet whether dendrites contribute remains unclear. We derive exact
gradients for conductance-based trees and show that each synaptic update
separates into local eligibility and a transported compartment error. This
factorization yields a credit-operator theory that predicts when restricted
routing helps: retained task signal must exceed representation error, gain
mismatch and admitted noise. Simulations show that neuron identity provides
most useful feedback, whereas subtree addresses help only at intermediate
bandwidth. At fixed resources, serial dendritic stages improve learning only
when their order matches a task's multiplicative hierarchy; the benefit
saturates and can reverse when the relevant ratios are locally available.
Reconstructed arbors provide sparse ancestry addresses whose modeled capacity
shows the same direction in two MICrONS animals, although the deeply sampled
animal reveals mainly coarse addresses. Focal shunting regulates route gain
only in permissive electrotonic regimes, and measured visual responses show no
morphology-specific alignment with endogenous task credit. Dendritic structure
therefore does not generally solve credit assignment. Instead, it provides a
conditional routing resource whose value depends jointly on coordinate
precision, address resolution, conductance state and task--morphology
alignment.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}

A biological neuron must distribute credit for its output errors across
thousands of synapses arranged on a branching dendritic tree. Point units in
standard neural networks do not face this subcellular problem
\citep{koch1999biophysics,spruston2008pyramidal,richards2019dendritic}. Each
synapse can access presynaptic drive, local voltage, reversal potential and
input resistance. Inhibition can also increase local conductance, shunt
excitation and change the gain of every dendritic path that passes through the
affected compartment \citep{gidon2012inhibition,larkum2013cellular}.

Backpropagation applies the chain rule from an objective to every parameter
\citep{rumelhart1986learning}. It therefore provides the exact optimization
reference, but it is not itself a biological mechanism. A literal
implementation would require parameter-specific derivatives, coordinated
backward Jacobians and appropriate forward--feedback relationships. Synapses,
by contrast, have access to presynaptic activity, local postsynaptic state and
modulatory signals whose spatial precision is uncertain
\citep{lillicrap2020backprop,whittington2019theories}. Credit assignment within
a neuron therefore involves two linked spatial questions: which neuron should
change, and how should its teaching coordinate be distributed through the
arbor? We ask when a restricted signal can approximate the exact dendritic
error field.

Biologically motivated learning schemes relax different requirements of
backpropagation. Three-factor rules combine local eligibility with a
modulatory signal \citep{fremaux2016threefactor}. Feedback alignment replaces
transposed weights with alternative pathways and requires positive functional
correspondence rather than exact symmetry
\citep{lillicrap2016feedback,nokland2016dfa}. Predictive, equilibrium and
prospective schemes infer desired neural states
\citep{scellier2017equilibrium,whittington2017predictive,song2024prospective},
whereas eligibility-propagation methods separate online synaptic traces from
neuron-specific learning signals \citep{bellec2020eprop}. Dendritic-error and
burst models separate forward and teaching signals across compartments,
interneurons or temporal codes
\citep{schiess2016somatodendritic,guerguiev2017segregated,
sacramento2018dendritic,payeur2021burst,ellenberger2026gle}. Most of these
approaches deliver a neuronal coordinate or use a small number of named
compartments. What a full dendritic arbor contributes after credit reaches a
neuron remains unresolved.

We begin by deriving the exact gradient for a conductance-based dendritic
network. Every gradient factorizes into \emph{local eligibility $\times$
transported error}. Eligibility depends on presynaptic activity, driving force
and input resistance, while a neuronal teaching coordinate is conveyed through
path-specific gains. This factorization turns dendritic credit assignment into
a concrete approximation problem: which spatial error fields can a restricted
biological signal represent, and under what conditions do those fields improve
learning?

We then place neuronal coordinates, dendritic addresses and route gains within
a common credit-operator theory. Teaching signals provide signed neuronal
coordinates, dendritic ancestry provides nested candidate addresses, and
conductance determines the gain of each route. The theory predicts a
quantitative boundary: restricted routing helps stochastic learning only when
the useful gradient signal it retains offsets representation bias, coefficient
error and admitted noise. Here, ``phase'' denotes an operational regime in
alignment--bandwidth--noise space, rather than a temporal training phase or a
thermodynamic transition. The framework also separates three possible roles
of depth: adding address scales, composing nonlinear forward operations and
increasing the cost of transporting a signal along more variable paths.

The experiments follow this hierarchy. Controlled simulations isolate
coordinate bandwidth, neuron-to-tree assignment and task-dependent routing
between sibling subtrees. A hierarchy-depth experiment tests the predicted
signal--noise crossover, and a fixed-resource experiment asks when additional
physical dendritic stages improve forward learning. MICrONS reconstructions
then test route capacity, focal shunting and task alignment, while an external
six-animal dataset tests for signed neuron-specific coordinates. The evidence
supports a conditional account: neuron identity supplies most of the useful
information, dendrites provide sparse addresses, shunting regulates route gain
only in suitable electrotonic regimes, and anatomy helps only when its routes
correspond to task credit (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}).

\section*{Results}

We move from the error available to a point neuron to the structured field
required by synapses across a dendritic tree. A global scalar conveys only the
sign or magnitude of an error. A neuron-indexed coordinate identifies the
receiving cell, a within-tree address selects a subtree, and route gain
determines how strongly the signal reaches that path. The theory defines these
levels, controlled networks isolate them, and anatomical and functional data
test whether real arbors provide and use the predicted routes. Study
chronology, numerical gates and exclusions are reported in Methods.

We use exact transported compartment error only as an information ceiling, not
as a proposed biological signal. Likewise, every routed advantage reported
below is a resource claim rather than evidence for a computation unique to
dendrites. A point model supplied explicitly with the same routing fields and
gains matches the dendritic model by construction. The relevant question is
therefore which routing resources anatomy makes available and what wiring cost
they impose.

\subsection*{Exact dendritic gradients factor into eligibility and transported error}

For a point neuron $u$ with drive $s_u=\sum_i w_i x_i$, activity
$V_u=f(s_u)$ and error $\delta_u=\partial\mathcal L/\partial V_u$, the chain
rule gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial w_i}
=\underbrace{x_i f'(s_u)}_{\text{synapse-local eligibility}}
\underbrace{\delta_u}_{\text{one coordinate shared by neuron }u}.
\label{eq:pointneuron}
\end{equation}
Backpropagation computes $\delta_u$ from the downstream Jacobian, whereas
biological models approximate it. A point unit has no subcellular state: all
its synapses share this coordinate and differ only through eligibility. We ask
how a dendritic tree extends that credit.

The local factorization is not limited to a single feedforward tree. Let the
steady state of a compartmental neuron satisfy
\begin{equation}
\bm F(\bm V,\bm g,\bm x)=\bm 0,
\qquad
J_V=\frac{\partial\bm F}{\partial\bm V},
\qquad
J_V^{\mathsf T}\bm q=\nabla_{\bm V}\mathcal L .
\label{eq:generaladjoint}
\end{equation}
This fixed-point adjoint is the steady-state member of the
Almeida--Pineda recurrent-backpropagation lineage
\citep{almeida1987asynchronous,pineda1987recurrent}. Intuitively, $q_n$
measures the change in loss produced by a small current injection at
compartment $n$. When $J_V$ is nonsingular, the steady state varies
differentiably with its parameters, and implicit differentiation gives the
exact parameter gradient
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial g_i}
=-\bm q^{\mathsf T}\frac{\partial\bm F}{\partial g_i}.
\label{eq:implicitgradient}
\end{equation}
For a conductance synapse at compartment $n$, using the residual convention
$\bm F=G\bm V-\bm b$, this becomes
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial g_i}
=\underbrace{x_i(E_i-V_n)}_{\text{synapse-local factor}}\,
\underbrace{q_n}_{\text{compartment adjoint}}.
\label{eq:generalconductancegradient}
\end{equation}
The directed path product below is the triangular-tree corollary of
Eq.~\ref{eq:generaladjoint}, where the tree is acyclic and its Jacobian can be
ordered triangularly. In a reciprocal passive cable, $J_V=G$ is symmetric
positive definite and $G^{-\mathsf T}=G^{-1}$, which gives the
Green's-function adjoint used for reconstructed cells. The inverse transpose
remains essential for a general nonsymmetric or recurrent compartmental
system. In the triangular tree considered below,
$q_n=R_n^{\mathrm{tot}}\,\partial\mathcal L/\partial V_n$. Therefore,
Eq.~\ref{eq:generalconductancegradient} and the compartment factorization in
Eq.~\ref{eq:factorization} express the same identity, with the input-resistance
factor assigned to different terms. Dendritic credit thus always consists of
local eligibility multiplied by a compartment-specific adjoint. Only the
construction of that adjoint changes.

To extend the point unit, we use a directed, feedforward rooted tree
\citep{safaai2026localcredit}. Compartment $n$ receives synaptic conductances
$g_i$ with presynaptic activities $x_i$ and reversal potentials $E_i$. Let
$\mathcal S_n$ and $\mathcal C_n$ denote its synapses and child compartments,
respectively, and let $g_n^L$ and $E_L$ denote leak conductance and reversal
potential. At the conductance stage, the steady-state voltage is
\begin{equation}
V_n = R_n^{\mathrm{tot}}
\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal S_n}g_i x_iE_i
+\sum_{c\in\mathcal C_n}g_{c\to n}^{\mathrm{den}}a_c
+g_n^LE_L\right),
\qquad
R_n^{\mathrm{tot}}=(g_n^{\mathrm{tot}})^{-1},
\label{eq:voltage}
\end{equation}
where $a_c=f_c(V_c)$ is the activity transmitted by child $c$. The total
conductance
$g_n^{\mathrm{tot}}=g_n^L+\sum_{i\in\mathcal S_n}g_i x_i+
\sum_{c\in\mathcal C_n}g_{c\to n}^{\mathrm{den}}$ includes leak, active
synaptic conductance and child coupling. Differentiating with respect to a
synaptic conductance gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial g_i}
=\underbrace{x_iR_n^{\mathrm{tot}}(E_i-V_n)}_{\text{synapse-local eligibility}}
\underbrace{\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial V_n}}_{\text{compartment error}}.
\label{eq:factorization}
\end{equation}
Presynaptic activity, driving force and input resistance are local to the
synapse and its compartment. The remaining task dependence is contained in
$\partial\mathcal L/\partial V_n$.

Because a tree has one route from each compartment to the soma, its exact
compartment error is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial V_n}
=\delta_{0,u}\widetilde\alpha_n,
\qquad
\widetilde\alpha_n=
\prod_{(i\to k)\in\operatorname{path}(n\to0)}
f_i'(V_i)R_k^{\mathrm{tot}}g_{i\to k}^{\mathrm{den}},
\label{eq:pathgain}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_{0,u}$ is the somatic error for neuron $u$. Exact reconstruction
from Eqs.~\ref{eq:factorization}--\ref{eq:pathgain} matched automatic
differentiation across 100 diagnostic runs: median relative gradient error was
$7.43\times10^{-8}$ and the maximum was $1.88\times10^{-7}$
(Methods and Supplementary Section~S5).

Equation~\ref{eq:pathgain} applies to the directed artificial network used in
the regular-tree experiments. The reconstructed-cell analyses below instead
use a reciprocal passive cable matrix grounded in classical dendritic cable
theory \citep{rall1962dendrites}. In that model the compartment error is
an exact Green's-function adjoint rather than this simple product, but the
synaptic gradient retains the same separation between local sensitivity and a
soma-derived compartment error.

This identity specifies what a local learning rule must approximate. Our
three-factor rule preserves the exact eligibility in
Eq.~\ref{eq:factorization} and replaces the compartment error with an available
teaching field. Four- and five-factor variants add slowly estimated reliability
preconditioners but leave the fast local factorization unchanged. We use exact
path transport only as an information oracle.

\paragraph{From exact gradients to restricted credit operators.}

The factorization isolates the quantity that differs across local rules. Let
$\widehat{\bm g}=\bm g+\bm\xi$ be an unbiased stochastic gradient with
covariance $\Sigma$, and let a fixed credit operator $M$ encode the available
coordinate, route assignment and gain. The update is
$\bm w^+=\bm w-\eta M\widehat{\bm g}$. If the population loss has an
$L$-Lipschitz gradient, the descent lemma gives
\begin{equation}
\E\mathcal L(\bm w^+)\leq \mathcal L(\bm w)
-\eta\bm g^{\mathsf T}M\bm g
+\frac{L\eta^2}{2}\left(
\lVert M\bm g\rVert_2^2+\tr(M\Sigma M^{\mathsf T})\right).
\label{eq:creditoperatorbound}
\end{equation}
When $\bm g^{\mathsf T}M\bm g>0$, optimizing this bound over $\eta$ yields
the phase utility
\begin{equation}
U(M)=\frac{[\bm g^{\mathsf T}M\bm g]^2}
{2L\{\lVert M\bm g\rVert_2^2+\tr(M\Sigma M^{\mathsf T})\}}.
\label{eq:phaseutility}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:creditoperatorbound} is an upper guarantee for a general
$L$-smooth loss. It is exact for the corresponding quadratic when the
curvature equals $L$ along the update directions. Otherwise, comparisons of
$U(M)$ order the guarantees rather than the realized losses.

Throughout this paper, a \emph{phase} denotes a regime separated by a
quantitative crossover, such as whether rejected noise is larger or smaller
than discarded signal. It does not refer to a thermodynamic phase transition.
Additional address dimensions should therefore help only when their retained
task signal outweighs finite-step gain effects and admitted gradient noise.
Exact full-batch backpropagation has $M=I$ and $\Sigma=0$. At a matched
infinitesimal update norm, no alternative direction can provide greater
first-order descent. A projected stochastic gradient can nevertheless
outperform an unprojected stochastic gradient by rejecting noise. For an
orthogonal projector $P$ on an identity-curvature quadratic at the common step
$\eta=1/L$, the exact crossover is
$\tr[(I-P)\Sigma]>\lVert(I-P)\bm g\rVert_2^2$: rejected noise must exceed
discarded signal. At smaller common steps, the rejected-noise threshold rises
(Supplementary Information). Backpropagation followed by the same projection
is therefore the appropriate upper control for any projected local rule.

One-step geometry predicts within-family progress and large contrasts caused

\smallskip
\textbf{Capture vocabulary.}
\emph{Field capture} is the fraction of exact mean-field energy retained by a
dictionary projection; \emph{spectral capture} averages this fraction over a
credit covariance; \emph{checkpoint capture} applies it to a trained gradient;
and \emph{capture per unit wiring} divides field capture by feedback-connection
density. We use an explicit qualifier whenever the estimand is not field
capture.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

For a route dictionary $\Phi$, topology determines the span
$\operatorname{col}(\Phi)$, whereas learning must also estimate the route
coefficients. The total weighted field error separates exactly into an
irreducible projection residual and a within-span coefficient-estimation error
(Supplementary Information, ``Stochastic credit-operator phase theory'').
Multiplying fixed route columns by nonzero scalar shunting gains leaves their
span and spectral capture unchanged, although it can substantially change Gram
conditioning. State- or branch-dependent gain, in contrast, can act as a
reliability preconditioner. For disjoint branches with signal energy $S_b$,
noise energy $N_b$ and fixed step $\eta=c/L$,
Eq.~\ref{eq:creditoperatorbound} is optimized over attenuations
$0\leq a_b\leq1$ by
$a_b^*=\min\{1,S_b/[c(S_b+N_b)]\}$. The reliability factor
$S_b/(S_b+N_b)$ is the special case $c=1$, paralleling the forward reliability
weighting derived for conductance-based dendrites
\citep{jordan2024conductance}. A point model supplied with the same branch
gates must match. The biological question is whether dendritic conductance can
make such localized gain available.

\subsection*{Per-neuron bandwidth dominates restricted feedback}

A global scalar has low bandwidth but does not identify which neuron produced
an error. In our initial experiments, the matched-width/scalar-fallback rule
retains the somatic vector only at a dendritic stage of identical width;
otherwise, it averages that vector to one scalar per example. Neuron-indexed
feedback instead repeats the appropriate somatic coordinate across all
compartments in the corresponding neuron's tree. It therefore communicates one
value per neuron, not one value per compartment.

The contrast between scalar and neuron-indexed feedback reflects bandwidth,
not a new scaling principle. Global perturbation estimates are already known
to become noisy as network size increases
\citep{werfel2005learning,lillicrap2016feedback}. Our focus is the additional
value of neuron-to-tree ownership and within-tree addressing once neuronal
identity is available.

We compared these fields in regular $[3,3]$ trees containing 13 modeled
compartments per neuron. Across 15 paired MNIST seeds, neuron-indexed feedback
increased three-factor accuracy from $90.54\pm0.54$\% to
$97.16\pm0.11$\% in shunting networks and from $91.76\pm0.78$\% to
$97.14\pm0.11$\% in width- and connectivity-matched additive networks
(Fig.~\ref{fig:feedback}a). All 15 pairs improved in both architectures. At the
same neuron-indexed-trained checkpoints, the cosine between local and exact
dendritic gradients increased from $0.012\pm0.059$ to $0.708\pm0.027$ in
shunting trees and from $-0.001\pm0.047$ to $0.625\pm0.035$ in additive
trees (Fig.~\ref{fig:feedback}c). The increase was positive at all 15 matched
checkpoints in each architecture.

Supplying exact transported compartment errors tests the derived local
eligibility without introducing a feedback approximation. Across five paired
shunting seeds, three-factor learning with a local decoder reached
$97.18\pm0.12$\%, compared with $97.03\pm0.08$\% for the
same-architecture backpropagation reference. Neither the empirical five-factor
preconditioner nor a backpropagated decoder produced a material gain under
exact transport (Supplementary Fig.~S3c). The poor scalar-feedback result
therefore does not reflect a failure of the derived local eligibility. The
large scalar-to-neuron-indexed gap reflects lost neuronal identity, whereas
the smaller remaining gap to exact transport reflects missing path
information.

Shunting changes the conductance component of path gain. In a separate
five-seed diagnostic cohort with five inhibitory synapses per branch, the
unweighted path-gain coefficient of variation across compartments within an
example was $0.229$ for shunting trees and $1.161$ for additive trees. The
ordering was the same in all five paired seeds (Source Data). This statistic
describes the electrical operating point but does not establish a better
gradient direction. A 15-seed comparison that crossed architecture with
analytical or occupancy-calibrated branch-output-nonlinearity initialization
showed no general shunting advantage. Under analytical initialization, both
architectures reached a mean accuracy of $90.92$\%
($\pm0.42$ and $\pm0.51$ s.d.\ for shunting and additive; paired difference
$-0.00$, $P=0.982$), whereas occupancy calibration modestly favored the
additive model (Source Data). We therefore interpret conductance as a
route-gain mechanism rather than a universal optimizer.

Broader archived controls define the limits of this conclusion
(Supplementary Figs.~S1--S4). Under restricted feedback, shunting trees were
more stable than matched additive trees in depth and teaching-noise stress
tests. The advantage was task dependent, however, and did not isolate a
backward shunting mechanism. Increasing feedback structure consistently
improved learning, including in a flattened CIFAR-10 mechanism control, whereas
unmatched local errors and forward nonlinearities did not replace the required
teaching field. These experiments support a conditional robustness claim, not
a general accuracy advantage.

We prospectively trained 640 models spanning two tasks, two neuron models, one
to four dendritic stages, three feedback fields and matched backpropagation,
with ten paired seeds per condition. All local conditions used the same
factorization-derived eligibility and training protocol. A prespecified
input-validity rule, defined independently of the outcomes, excluded all 160
synthetic-noise shunting runs because their signed transfer output is not a
valid drive for a positive-conductance shunting denominator (Methods). The
remaining 480 runs included both models on MNIST and the additive model on the
noise task.

Relative to a global scalar broadcast, neuron-indexed feedback increased
held-out accuracy by 4.92--6.99 percentage points on MNIST and by
9.76--14.16 points in the additive noise model. All 120 retained paired seeds
improved (Fig.~\ref{fig:feedback}g and Supplementary Fig.~S19a,b). Across all
12 retained task--model--depth conditions, exact transport placed mean
three-factor accuracy within 0.14 points of matched backpropagation
(Supplementary Fig.~S19c).

A fresh Fashion-MNIST ladder tested whether the dominant identity effect was
specific to MNIST (Fig.~\ref{fig:feedback}b,e). Without retuning the
architecture or learning protocol, neuron-indexed feedback outperformed the
matched-width/scalar fallback by 4.46 points in shunting trees (95\%
paired-seed interval 4.09--4.88) and by 3.78 points in additive trees
(3.43--4.14). All ten paired seeds were positive in both architectures (exact
sign-flip $P=0.002$). Exact path transport provided no reliable additional
benefit beyond neuron identity: $-0.11$ points in shunting trees
($-0.21$ to $-0.01$; 2/10 positive; $P=0.096$) and 0.13 points in additive
trees ($-0.08$ to 0.35; 8/10 positive; $P=0.314$). This second dataset
strengthens the bandwidth conclusion. The separate question of within-tree
addressing is tested in the credit-reversal experiments below.

A bandwidth-matched control preserved the number and values of the coordinates
but deranged their mapping to trees. Across the six retained conditions,
correct assignment improved accuracy by 0.15--0.70 points and was favored in
58/60 pairs (Fig.~\ref{fig:feedback}e); five of the six contrasts survived
correction. Bandwidth therefore accounts for most of the
scalar-to-neuron-indexed gain, while correct coordinate ownership contributes a
smaller effect. This control does not assign distinct task-dependent
coordinates to different subtrees of the same neuron and is therefore not a
trained test of within-tree addressing.

The same input-validity rule excluded the 400-run inhibitory-dose family from
inference because its shunting-minus-additive endpoint depends on invalid
signed-shunting cells. We instead used an independent, clean-source 320-run
audit to test agreement between exact transport and backpropagation across ten
paired seeds, four depths, two tasks and two cores. In the synthetic shunting
cells, an explicit ReLU transfer was applied before positive conductances were
formed (Supplementary Fig.~S19e). All 320 runs satisfied the configuration,
log, metric and finite stage-complete checkpoint criteria.

After averaging depths within each seed, exact-minus-backpropagation accuracy
was $0.005$ percentage points on additive MNIST, $-0.013$ on shunting MNIST,
$-0.112$ on additive noise and $-0.098$ on shunting noise. All four 95\%
paired bootstrap intervals included zero (Source Data). Across the 160 raw
method pairs, the mean difference was $-0.055$ points and the largest absolute
pair difference was $5.90$ points. These results are empirical agreement
checks, not formal equivalence tests or claims of pointwise identity.

A retained 240-run spatial-map control improved accuracy by 0.36--2.57
points, including under backpropagation and in the additive randomly projected
noise model (Supplementary Fig.~S7). With 336 contacts, spatial branches
covered 336 unique inputs, compared with 276.2 for random branches. This
difference identifies a forward-connectivity effect rather than a
task-specific credit-routing effect.

Among 320 fixed-budget runs, the 160 additive cells provide the valid
synthetic comparison at 16 terminal branches and 960--968 contacts per soma.
Depth four underperformed depth one in all 40 paired-seed contrasts:
$-7.92$ points under scalar feedback, $-1.06$ under neuron-indexed feedback,
and $-1.37$ under both exact transport and backpropagation
(Supplementary Fig.~S19f; Supplementary Fig.~S8). Although deeper trees retain
more trainable parameters, scalar feedback markedly amplified the stress
associated with depth.

Across 120 retained backpropagation checkpoints, mean gradient cosine was
0.027 for scalar feedback and 0.586 for neuron-indexed feedback. Norm-matched
updates decreased the loss at 75/120 and 120/120 checkpoints, respectively.
Cosine also predicted retained one-step progress
($\rho=0.893$, 95\% checkpoint-bootstrap interval 0.860--0.923;
Supplementary Fig.~S9). This analysis links field geometry to loss change
without replacing the trained-learning factorial.

A trained test of within-tree addressing requires task credit to differ across
sibling subtrees. We therefore froze a two-stream credit-reversal task before
inspecting its outcomes. Both sibling streams were active on every example.
Context selected the stream that controlled the somatic output, while the other
stream carried an anticorrelated distractor. Because context and label were
exactly balanced, context alone could not predict the target. The same scalar
somatic error consequently required different branch updates in the two
contexts. At initialization, the one-neuron field captured 0.509 of the
eligibility-weighted exact-gradient energy, below the prespecified 0.90
rejection threshold, whereas two correct subtree coordinates captured 1.000.

Across ten paired seeds, correct two-subtree routing reached 80.63\%
held-out accuracy. The corresponding value was 76.24\% for one shared
neuronal coordinate, a difference of 4.39 percentage points
(95\% paired bootstrap interval 3.33--5.48; 10/10 seeds), and 19.63\% after
a fixed within-neuron derangement that assigned every route to the wrong
sibling, a difference of 61.00 points (59.93--62.09; 10/10 seeds;
Fig.~\ref{fig:feedback}f). A dense random rank-two map reached 58.47\% and
performed below correct routing in all ten seeds. Gradient capture tracked
norm-matched one-step progress (Supplementary Fig.~S19h). After a
single-context adaptation block, the shared rule lost 55.66 points on the
previous context, whereas correct routing left the inactive sibling unchanged.
The difference in forgetting was 55.66 points
(53.84--57.67; Supplementary Fig.~S19i).

Correct subtree routing, exact transport and analytic backpropagation were
identical in this shallow linear branch model. A point-neuron implementation
with the same two context-gated parameter groups also matched them exactly.
The experiment therefore demonstrates the value of the address rather than a
computational advantage unique to dendrites. A point model can obtain the same
solution through explicit feature gating. This experiment provides the
decisive $K=2$ support contrast on one forward architecture; the next
factorial broadens the feedback bandwidth and representation conditions.

\subsection*{Structured subtree addresses help at an intermediate feedback budget}

Correct ancestry routing outperformed every matched non-anatomical control
only at an intermediate feedback budget. To establish this, we froze an
eight-context hierarchical credit-reversal task and crossed five
parameter-matched representations, feedback budgets
$K\in\{1,2,4,8\}$, six route families and three references. Twenty paired
independent seeds produced all 2,700 planned fits. Context and label were
exactly balanced, every route field had unit norm per example, and initial
weights, data and optimization were paired. The dendritic, explicitly gated
point, flat-compartment and grouped-point implementations each had 64 forward
parameters, 64 active input contacts and one nonlinearity, and all received
identical routed coefficient fields. A degree- and depth-matched rewired tree
preserved these resources while disrupting task--topology correspondence.

The benefit of correct ancestry routing was non-monotonic. Mean held-out
accuracy increased from 0.187 at $K=1$ to 0.449, 0.801 and 0.810 at
$K=2,4,8$, respectively (Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}b). Correct ancestry
strongly outperformed fixed within-neuron route derangement at $K=2$
(difference 0.264, 95\% paired bootstrap interval 0.243--0.285) and $K=4$
(0.611, 0.601--0.620). The comparison with the best matched non-anatomical
control, selected separately within each seed, was more selective. Correct
ancestry lost at $K=1$ and $K=2$, won at $K=4$ by 0.0127
(0.0059--0.0197; 15/20 seeds; two-sided Wilcoxon $P=0.0048$,
Benjamini--Hochberg-adjusted $P=0.0064$ across the four budgets), and tied at
full rank $K=8$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}c). Thus, an unrestricted
learned rank-$K$ oracle and random rank-$K$ routes used low-dimensional task
structure more efficiently at the smallest budgets, whereas nested anatomy
became advantageous only when four coordinates were available.

The correct matched tree exceeded the rewired tree by 0.231
(0.210--0.254) at $K=2$ and by 0.050 (0.042--0.060) at $K=4$, with all 20
seeds positive in both contrasts (Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}d). Rewiring
had no effect at $K=1$, where the route is shared, or at $K=8$, where every
leaf is addressed. Exact transport and analytic backpropagation were
identical. The four implementation-equivalent dendritic, gated-point, flat and
grouped-point controls also matched exactly in every deterministic and
paired-stochastic condition (maximum accuracy difference 0;
Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}e).

Initial exact-gradient capture alone did not determine final accuracy across
route families. Fields with similar capture but different route signs and task
alignment learned differently (Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}f). To quantify
this boundary, we deterministically reconstructed all 2,700 fits and evaluated
the shared-coefficient partition residual at initialization and at the trained
checkpoint (Supplementary Section~S5 and Supplementary Fig.~S23). In the
matched tree, trained ancestry routes captured 0.162, 0.315, 0.547 and 1.000 of
the weighted exact coefficient field at $K=1,2,4,8$, whereas ownership
derangement captured zero for $K>1$. Across the full route ladder, capture and
held-out accuracy were positively associated within every seed (mean
within-seed Spearman $\rho=0.824$, 95\% seed-bootstrap interval
0.810--0.838; 20/20 seeds). The association remained positive after excluding
the exact $K=8$ boundary ($\rho=0.639$, 0.610--0.665; 20/20).

However, correct ancestry, depth bins and random sparse groups had similar
capture at a given $K$ despite different trained outcomes. The residual
therefore explains the large bandwidth and ownership failures but not the
smaller support-specific advantage at $K=4$, which requires the signal--noise
and trajectory analyses below. Overall, this experiment establishes a
structured-address resource advantage at intermediate bandwidth, not an
intrinsic superiority of dendritic material. Point models reproduce the effect
when they receive the same parameter groups and routing fields explicitly.

\subsection*{A credit-operator phase theory predicts when restricted routes help}

Task--tree alignment, matched address depth and noise rejection each followed
the prediction of the credit-operator bound. We therefore froze a controlled
experiment derived directly from
Eqs.~\ref{eq:creditoperatorbound}--\ref{eq:phaseutility}. Sixteen coordinates
were represented in an orthonormal balanced-tree Haar basis. Across fifty
independent confirmatory seeds, the experiment generated 10,800
seed--condition rows spanning route rank, task--tree alignment, task hierarchy,
routed model depth, signal and noise retention, and branch-reliability
heterogeneity. The forward coordinate budget was fixed so that the experiment
isolated stochastic credit geometry. It was not a positive-conductance
forward-neuron simulation.

First, the benefit of ancestry routes varied continuously with spectral
task--tree alignment $\rho\in[0,1]$. Here, $\rho=0$ denotes the seed-specific
unaligned covariance, and $\rho=1$ denotes tree-Haar alignment. At full
alignment and $K=4$, ancestry captured 0.552 more task-gradient energy than a
random rank-matched route (95\% paired bootstrap interval 0.535--0.569; 50/50
seeds; two-sided Wilcoxon $P=1.78\times10^{-15}$;
Fig.~\ref{fig:creditphase}b). The advantage was near zero under random
alignment, increased with alignment, remained below the Ky--Fan dense
principal-subspace upper bound, and vanished at full rank. The Ky--Fan bound
is the maximum covariance capture available to any rank-$K$ orthogonal
projector.

At full alignment and $K=4$, ancestry attained the rank-matched upper bound to
numerical precision. This is the generic outcome for hierarchical credit
because the $K$-route ancestry span equals the span of the $K$ coarsest
tree-Haar modes. Anatomy therefore attains the rank-$K$ Ky--Fan bound whenever
the tree's wavelet basis diagonalizes a task covariance with a coarse-dominant
spectrum. More generally, it attains the bound exactly when the leading
rank-$K$ eigenspace lies inside the laminar span. For other covariances, its
shortfall is bounded by the leading-eigenspace energy outside that span
(Supplementary Section~S4).

A deterministic endpoint reanalysis used the affine covariance mixture to
calculate an exact ancestry-versus-random alignment threshold within each
seed. Nineteen of 50 random-route draws already favored ancestry at zero
alignment, and all 50 favored it at full alignment. Treating the former as
requiring zero alignment, the median $K=4$ threshold was 0.0173 and the mean
was 0.0523 (95\% seed-bootstrap interval 0.0336--0.0730). This threshold is
relative to the sampled random routes and is not a universal biological
constant.

Second, routed depth was optimal when it matched the task hierarchy. The
controlled generator imposed this correspondence: signal occupied levels
through $H$, while finer levels carried additional noise. The test therefore
validates the predicted crossover locations quantitatively; it does not imply
that $D_{\mathrm{r}}=H$ for arbitrary tasks. For task depths $H=1,2,3,4$, the
lowest mean final population loss occurred at route resolution
$D_{\mathrm{r}}=H$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:creditphase}d and Supplementary Fig.~S16).
Across the 200 seed--task pairs, matched depth reduced loss relative to the best
mismatched depth by 0.0957 (95\% interval 0.0782--0.1134 after averaging the
four task-depth contrasts within each independent seed; 48/50 seeds; two-sided
Wilcoxon $P=3.00\times10^{-13}$). Too little depth left target components
outside the route span, whereas excess depth admitted fine-scale stochastic
gradient energy. At $D_{\mathrm{r}}=4$, the projector is full rank and the
update is identical to the common stochastic-backpropagation reference. In
this experiment, depth helps by matching task address resolution, not simply
by increasing.

Third, independent variation of signal and noise retention recovered the
predicted boundary. Backpropagation followed by the same route projection
produced lower mean one-step population loss than unprojected stochastic
backpropagation in exactly those of the 25 condition means for which rejected
noise exceeded discarded signal (Fig.~\ref{fig:creditphase}e). The routed
local estimator carried an additional specified coefficient-noise cost and
never exceeded this projected-backpropagation control.

Branch-specific reliability gains improved progress only when their ordering
matched the branch signal-to-noise ratios. At maximum heterogeneity, the
reliability-aligned gain exceeded the best single global gain by 1.241 in
one-step loss reduction (95\% interval 1.116--1.373; 50/50 seeds;
$P=1.78\times10^{-15}$). Shuffling or reversing the gains removed or reversed
the benefit (Fig.~\ref{fig:creditphase}f). An explicit point gate receiving
the same gains matched exactly (maximum difference 0).

We then asked whether physical conductance could realize this oracle
attenuation in a trained branch model. Eight branches received strictly
nonnegative input rates and excitatory conductances. A nonnegative shunt with
reversal zero reduced branch input resistance, while an oracle compensating
current $\kappa_bV_b$ kept the forward voltage unchanged. This intervention
isolated the gain that multiplies local eligibility. Fifty fresh confirmatory
seeds crossed five levels of reliability heterogeneity and nine matched
controls (Supplementary Fig.~S13). At maximum heterogeneity,
reliability-aligned shunting increased one-step test-loss reduction relative to
the best global shunt by 0.001817 (95\% paired bootstrap interval
0.001618--0.002020; 49/50 seeds; two-sided Wilcoxon
$P=3.55\times10^{-15}$). Its mean one-step advantage over no shunt was
0.001339 (0.000594--0.002096; 33/50 pairs; $P=0.0030$).

After 40 updates, reliability-aligned shunting reduced test loss by 0.02040
relative to the best global shunt ($-0.02222$ to $-0.01871$ for matched minus
global; 50/50 seeds favored the aligned condition,
$P=1.78\times10^{-15}$). It also outperformed shuffled and anti-aligned
shunts. Relative to the unshunted noisy rule, however, its final-loss
difference was 0.000425 ($-0.002456$--0.003071; $P=0.154$), providing no
reliable advantage in either direction. Exact clean backpropagation remained
the lowest-loss reference.

A state-matched additive control was identical to the no-shunt condition. An
explicit point gate receiving the same sample-dependent factors was evaluated
through an independent update path and reproduced the conductance trajectory
to numerical precision. Positive conductance can therefore approximate the
predicted reliability ordering under a state clamp. The immediate benefit does
not establish autonomous shunt learning, a dendrite-specific computation, or
uniform superiority during training.

We next removed oracle access to branch signal and noise energies. At each
state, two independent noisy observations of the local branch gradient
estimated $S_b$ from their inner product and $N_b$ from half their squared
difference. Eight observation pairs initialized an exponential running
estimate that set nonnegative shunts throughout training
(Supplementary Section~S4 and Supplementary Fig.~S24). Fifty fresh paired
seeds completed all 1,050 planned outcomes.

At maximum heterogeneity, adaptive local shunting lowered final test loss by
0.01622 relative to an adaptive global shunt (95\% paired bootstrap interval
0.01450--0.01791; 50/50 seeds) and by 0.00553 relative to shuffled adaptive
placement (0.00377--0.00742; 41/50). However, adaptive local loss was 0.00425
higher than no shunt (0.00250--0.00598; 39/50) and 0.00345 higher than the
fixed initial oracle (0.00258--0.00430; 43/50). The final learned gain ordering
had a mean within-seed Spearman correlation of $\rho=0.756$ with the initial
oracle. An independently evaluated adaptive point gate again matched to
numerical precision. Local observations therefore recovered enough reliability
ordering to outperform global or misplaced attenuation, but not enough
calibration to improve on unattenuated noisy learning.

Finally, static nonzero gain rescaling changed numerical conditioning without
changing route span. Nested indicators, their tree-Haar basis and a gain-scaled
version had identical capture to $1.33\times10^{-15}$, while the condition
number ranged from 1 for the Haar basis to 583 for the illustrated scaled
parameterization (Supplementary Fig.~S14). We then trained noisy route
coefficients prospectively in a separate 50-seed experiment with exactly the
same span (Supplementary Fig.~S14). The three rank-eight coordinate systems had
condition numbers of 1, 15 and 388.5, with zero target address residual.

The preregistered prediction of an unconditional Haar advantage was falsified
at effective sample size four. Raw nested and scaled nested coordinates reduced
final loss by 0.277 and 0.191 relative to Haar because their slow modes filtered
update noise. At effective sample size 256, the ordering reversed: Haar reduced
loss by 0.0256 and 0.1709, respectively, in 50/50 paired seeds. An exact
finite-time bias--variance calculation predicted median crossovers at effective
sample sizes 38.5 and 8.25. An oracle Gram-preconditioned control made every
same-span field trajectory identical to $1.16\times10^{-15}$. Conditioning is
therefore neither pure defect nor additional capacity. It trades finite-time
bias against estimator variance.

The phase utility also proved diagnostic in the completed 2,700-fit factorial.
Reconstructing 64 initialization minibatches for each of 20 seeds produced 540
dendritic-route conditions. $U(M)$ predicted norm-matched one-step progress
with Spearman $\rho=0.937$ (seed-block 95\% interval 0.9336--0.9412) and final
held-out accuracy with $\rho=0.916$ (0.905--0.929). Spectral capture alone
predicted one-step progress less accurately ($\rho=0.877$), with a positive
seed-block difference of 0.060 (0.050--0.070;
Fig.~\ref{fig:creditphase}c). This secondary reanalysis explains the immediate
geometry at a fixed initialization; it is not an independent trained
experiment.

The bound also locates optima, although with an informative limitation
(Supplementary Fig.~S16). Because every tested field is a projector on the
same nested ladder, utility increases with feedback bandwidth only while each
additional route scale contributes proportionally more signal than noise. An
interior optimum is therefore expected whenever the signal spectrum decays
faster than the noise spectrum (Supplementary Section~S4).

In the depth experiment, the per-seed maximum of the initialization utility
coincided with the trained loss minimum in 135 of 200 seed--task pairs. The
group-mean maximum matched exactly at $H=1,2,3$. At the full-rank boundary
$H=4$, however, the one-step bound was conservative and favored
$D_{\mathrm{r}}=2$. In the factorial, the same utility reproduced the exact
ties at $K=8$, where all spans coincide, but predicted the trained $K=4$
ancestry advantage over the best control in only 6 of 20 seeds. That advantage
accumulates along the training trajectory and is not visible in one-step
initialization geometry, consistent with the finding that capture alone does
not determine learning (Fig.~\ref{fig:subtreefactorial}f).

\subsection*{Task-aligned physical depth improves learning at fixed budgets}

The fixed-state phase experiment addresses route resolution but does not test
whether serial dendritic dynamics contribute to forward computation. We
therefore constructed a separate positive-rate task in DendriNet, the
conductance-based population-network implementation used throughout (Methods).
All inputs were nonnegative rates that drove positive conductances. A distal
class signal was modulated by nested fine, coarse and global gains, and
separate nonnegative inhibitory streams measured those factors. This
mechanism-matched existence test is built around divisive normalization; it is
not a generic task benchmark
\citep{carandini2012normalization,holt1997shunting}.

Here, D1, D2 and D3 denote physical stage counts
$D_{\mathrm{p}}=1,2,3$. They are architecture labels rather than dendritic
compartment names. D1 $[8]$, D2 $[2,3]$ and D3 $[2,1,2]$ each contained eight
nonsomatic branch units per soma, 14,336 active contacts, 21,760 candidate
slots, 66,178 trainable parameters and 2,944 persistent state scalars. Only
the number and placement of serial divisive stages differed
(Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepth}a).

With backpropagation and aligned gain sensors, mean test accuracy increased
from 0.6096 at D1 to 0.6752 at D2 and 0.9182 at D3. The paired D3-minus-D1
effect was 30.86 percentage points (95\% seed-bootstrap interval
30.45--31.30; 10/10 positive pairs; exact sign-flip $P=0.002$;
Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepth}c,g). Exploratory signal, coupling and gain ladders
were used transparently to select the operating point, which was then fixed
before the ten inference seeds were run (Methods and Supplementary Fig.~S15).
The 31-point contrast should therefore be interpreted as a replicated effect
at a calibrated operating point.

Physical depth had little effect when the sensors were independent
(0.02 points) or trial shuffled ($-0.01$ points), and it reduced accuracy
after an exact-resource reversal of fine and global tree placement
($-0.43$ points, $-0.67$ to $-0.15$). The corresponding
aligned-minus-control interactions were 30.84, 30.87 and 31.28 points,
respectively, and all were positive in 10/10 paired seeds. The effect therefore
requires correspondence among task factors, branch-local sensors and physical
ordering; additional stages alone are insufficient.

The form of the nonlinearity also mattered at this operating point. Under
aligned raw-additive BP, accuracy decreased from 0.5733 at D1 to 0.5383 at D2
and 0.5190 at D3, whereas the corresponding shunting series increased
(Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepth}e). This control supports a divisive
hierarchical-composition mechanism in this task. It does not imply that
additive or expanded point networks are generally incapable of solving the
problem.

A same-task architecture ladder sharpened this boundary
(Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepth}f,h and Supplementary Fig.~S18a,b). An all-active
grouped-star model retained every branch module, mask, parameter, contact and
persistent state but removed serial child-to-parent composition. It matched D1
($-0.005$ points) and remained near 0.610 at every depth. Consequently, it
fell below the serial tree by 6.54 points at D2 and by 30.81 points at D3
(30.41--31.26; 10/10 positive). Reversing fine and global placement changed
the D3 contrast to $-0.43$ points, producing a 31.24-point alignment
interaction (30.65--31.77; 10/10 positive). The large effect therefore
requires serial composition, not merely the same collection of branch units.

This result is not an unconstrained capacity advantage. Point MLPs matched for
active or total parameter count reached 0.9861 and 0.9902, respectively, and
exceeded the serial D3 tree by 6.79 and 7.20 points in every paired seed
(Supplementary Fig.~S18b). The tree provides a task-matched structured
resource, not a computation that a flexible point network cannot learn.

Local credit assignment (LocalCA), which uses the factorization-derived
eligibility, retained the aligned depth benefit. With one decoder-derived
somatic coordinate, accuracy increased from 0.6083 at D1 to 0.7778 at D3
(16.95 points, 16.37--17.44; 10/10 positive). With exact path transport, D3
accuracy reached 0.8873, corresponding to a 27.90-point increase
(27.24--28.53; 10/10 positive; Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepth}d,g).
Exact-resource reversal removed both effects. The matched-minus-reversed depth
interactions were 17.72 points (17.27--18.18; 10/10 positive) for the shared
coordinate and 28.68 points (28.07--29.27; 10/10 positive) for path
transport.

A separate replay at the 30 aligned BP checkpoints reproduced autograd under
exact path transport (minimum gradient cosine 0.9999999999994). In contrast,
mean shared-soma cosine fell from 0.946 at D1 to 0.767 at D3. Together, the
training and checkpoint results show that local eligibility can access a
physical hierarchy when the transported error is sufficiently specific. They
do not show that exact path transport is biologically available.

An autograd teaching-coordinate ladder localized the remaining difference
(Supplementary Fig.~S28a,b). Replacing each compartment's loss derivative with
the derivative of its owning soma, while retaining autograd eligibility and
the BP optimizer, reduced D3 accuracy by only 0.64 points
(0.15--1.17; 8/10 positive; $P=0.033$). This arm and LocalCA initially used
different optimizer groups, so a transparently post-outcome diagnostic repeated
soma broadcast with the frozen LocalCA optimizer. Under that optimizer,
soma-broadcast autograd matched shared-soma LocalCA
($-0.06$ points, $-0.23$ to 0.09; $P=0.492$). Changing only the optimizer of
the broadcast arm accounted for 13.47 points (12.89--14.05).

Within LocalCA, path transport recovered 10.96 points relative to the shared
coordinate (10.34--11.58), while full BP retained a 3.08-point advantage over
path LocalCA (2.48--3.79). In this calibrated task, the main BP--local
differences therefore arise from the optimizer regime and the specificity of
the within-tree coordinate, rather than from the tested local eligibility
expression itself.

An alignment dose response tested whether the depth effect emerged before the
fully matched endpoint (Supplementary Fig.~S18c--e). Intermediate sensor
alignments $\alpha\in\{0.25,0.50,0.75\}$ were registered after the
$\alpha=0$ and $1$ outcomes were known. This analysis is therefore a
prospective interpolation rather than an independent confirmation. Here,
$\alpha=0$ denotes independent sensors and $\alpha=1$ denotes matched
sensors.

The D3-minus-D1 effect increased from 0.02 points at $\alpha=0$ to 0.21, 0.82
and 3.13 points at the intermediate levels, then rose to 30.86 points at full
alignment. Every seed had a positive within-seed linear slope
(mean 25.84 points per unit $\alpha$, 95\% interval 25.43--26.26; exact
sign-flip $P=0.002$), but the response was strongly nonlinear. The increase
from $0.25$ to $\alpha=0.75$ was only 2.92 points (2.65--3.17), whereas the
final increment from $0.75$ to $1.00$ was 27.73 points (27.35--28.11). The
depth effect therefore has a steep alignment boundary near the fully matched
task rather than a generic linear dose response.

We next strengthened the point control and changed the task hierarchy
(Supplementary Fig.~S18f--k). The earlier grouped-star control retained
serial-shaped child aggregators as independent arms. The stricter literal
grouped-point emulation is a trained nonserial control. It is distinct from the
implementation-equivalent grouped-point representation in the subtree
factorial, which matches the tree by construction. In the literal control,
each aggregator was replaced with a parameter-matched direct projection to the
soma. Every nonsomatic level retained its local branch module, mask and
shunting computation, but received the external streams independently and
never inherited the state of another branch. The replacement copied the
corresponding initialized raw conductances, making D1 an exact initialization
control.

On the existing $H=3$ task, literal grouped-point accuracy remained near 0.610
across D1--D3 (aligned D3-minus-D1, $-0.01$ points,
$-0.06$ to 0.04). At aligned D3, the serial-minus-literal-grouped-point
contrast reached 30.87 points (30.48--31.31; 10/10 positive; exact sign-flip
$P=0.002$). After placement reversal, the same contrast was $-0.44$ points
($-0.69$ to $-0.16$), yielding a 31.31-point alignment interaction
(30.73--31.84; 10/10 positive). The earlier grouped star and literal grouped
point differed by only 0.06 points at aligned D3
(0.01--0.13; 7/10 positive; $P=0.084$). The conclusion therefore does not
depend detectably on the choice of nonserial parameter-matched readout.

An independent $H=2$ hierarchy changed the sensor partition to two
32-feature tiers, the fixed branch inventory to 4/4, and the serial
morphologies to D1 $[8]$ and D2 $[4,1]$. The calibrated task family and all
other optimizer and stopping settings were retained. Ten fresh paired seeds
10300--10309 were used. Serial BP increased from 0.6588 to 0.9672, a
30.84-point D2-minus-D1 effect (30.46--31.23; 10/10 positive;
$P=0.002$). Reversing the two sensor tiers changed that effect to
$-1.56$ points and produced a 32.40-point depth-by-placement interaction
(32.10--32.70; 10/10 positive).

The literal grouped point remained flat in both placements
(matched, 0.02 points, $-0.02$ to 0.06; reversed, $-0.02$ points,
$-0.06$ to 0.02). At aligned D2, serial BP exceeded it by 30.82 points
(30.41--31.23), with a 32.36-point architecture-by-placement interaction.
Shared-soma LocalCA retained a 21.20-point aligned depth effect
(19.34--23.00), and exact-path LocalCA retained 31.38 points
(30.95--31.83). Placement reversal made both effects negative, producing
22.93- and 33.36-point interactions, respectively. Every positive interaction
had the predicted order in 10/10 paired seeds. This is an independent
hierarchy-depth replication within the same synthetic mechanism-matched task
family. It is not a second dataset or evidence that natural tasks generally
favor serial trees.

A frozen $H=4$ factorial tested the stronger prediction that the trained
optimum would continue from D3 to D4
(Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepthh4}b,c). D1--D4 again contained eight nonsomatic
units, 66,178 trainable parameters, and identical contact, slot and state
budgets. Under aligned serial BP, mean accuracy was 0.5963, 0.7112, 0.8671 and
0.8525. D4 remained 25.63 percentage points above D1
(24.71--26.40; 10/10 positive; exact sign-flip $P=0.002$;
BH-adjusted $q=0.0039$), but it was 1.46 points below D3
($-1.74$ to $-1.16$; 0/10 positive; $q=0.0039$;
Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepthh4}d). This result falsified the simple
one-stage-per-task-level prediction: performance saturated at D3 instead of
tracking $H=4$. Reversed placement remained near 0.60. Consequently, the
D4-versus-D1 depth-by-placement interaction was 25.94 points
(25.06--26.66; 10/10 positive), whereas the incremental D4-versus-D3
interaction was null ($-0.36$ points, $-0.70$ to 0.04).

The H4 controls preserved the conditional point-versus-dendrite result but did
not rescue the failed depth prediction. Literal grouped-point BP was flat
across D1--D4. At aligned D4, serial BP exceeded it by 25.60 points
(24.69--26.37), with a 25.91-point architecture-by-placement interaction
(25.06--26.62); both effects were positive in 10/10 seeds. Shared-soma LocalCA
increased by 5.27 points from D3 to D4 (4.84--5.72; 10/10 positive), whereas
exact-path LocalCA increased by only 0.60 points and failed the frozen
positive-pair gate (7/10, $P=0.086$). Raw-additive BP and the
shunting-minus-additive D4--D3 interaction were null. The added stage can
therefore change restricted-credit optimization after exact-BP performance has
saturated, but it provides no evidence for a further gain in forward
computation.

Finally, all 430 load-bearing H2/H3 fits were rerun from the same immutable
source commit used for H4. Every validity gate passed. The clean-source H2 and
H3 aligned BP effects were 30.84 and 30.86 points, and the corresponding
architecture-by-placement interactions were 32.43 and 31.27 points
(Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepthh4}e and Supplementary Fig.~S26). These same-seed
reruns confirm implementation concordance; they are not independent task or
biological replications.

Rather than adding another depth condition, we broadened the task family at
fixed D3. A 360-fit factorial crossed three data generators, sensor alignment
$\alpha\in\{0,0.5,1\}$, serial versus literal grouped-point composition, and
BP versus path-transport LocalCA. All fits passed the frozen finite-value,
completeness, resource and no-fallback gates.

Under BP at $\alpha=1$, the serial-minus-literal-grouped-point contrast was
30.32 percentage points for nested factors
(95\% paired-seed bootstrap interval 30.02--30.62), 22.20 points for flat
factors (21.73--22.64), and zero for local ratios. The corresponding
$\alpha=1$ minus $0$ architecture interactions were 30.41, 22.26 and
1.30 points; paired intervals for all interactions are reported in Source
Data. Removing nested spatial covariance therefore reduced, but did not
eliminate, the serial advantage. When excitation and inhibition already
provided a local ratio, both BP architectures reached ceiling and serial
composition added nothing (Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepthh4}f).

Restricted credit preserved the positive nested-factor crossover but revealed
a sharper boundary. At $\alpha=1$, the serial-minus-literal-grouped-point
contrast was 27.59 points for nested factors (26.61--28.45) and 12.21 points
for flat factors (11.19--13.34), but $-5.32$ points for local ratios
($-9.02$ to $-2.19$). The LocalCA alignment interactions were therefore
27.70, 12.37 and $-11.68$ points, respectively, and every interaction had the
same sign in all ten paired seeds. Relative to the flat-factor control, the
nested-family interaction was larger by 8.16 points under BP and 15.33 points
under LocalCA, with both contrasts positive in 10/10 seeds. Serial dendrites
therefore have no generic advantage. Alignment determines the magnitude of
the benefit, and a task that makes the required ratio locally available
removes the BP advantage and reverses it under the tested restricted-credit
rule (Fig.~\ref{fig:physicaldepthh4}g,h).

These controls distinguish four explanations that the original depth
comparison could not separate. First, parameter-matched point networks reject
a dendrite-specific expressivity claim. Second, the serial-minus-star and
serial-minus-literal-grouped-point interactions identify ordered divisive
composition, rather than branch count, parameter count or a particular
nonserial readout, as the source of the matched forward advantage. The H2--H4
cohorts show that this conclusion is not limited to the original three-stage
inventory, while H4 shows that the gain saturates. Third, the
optimizer-matched soma-broadcast comparison detects no penalty from the tested
local eligibility expression, and path-specific transport recovers most of the
shared-coordinate deficit. The main local-credit limitation in this task is
therefore the teaching coordinate and its optimization regime. Finally, the
alignment interpolation shows that the forward depth advantage emerges only
near a strongly matched task--sensor hierarchy. Dendritic structure thus
provides a useful computation and address system under matched conditions, but
neither depth nor dendritic material alone guarantees better learning.

\subsection*{Reconstructed arbors provide sparse, mostly coarse route capacity}

The preceding evidence comes from synthetic trees with hand-specified
topology. We therefore asked whether reconstructed cortical arbors provide
comparable addresses. From eight layer 2--5 intratelencephalic neurons in
MICrONS materialization 1822, we constructed ancestry dictionaries and mapped
28,669 classified inputs to collapsed cable segments from one mouse.
Electrical parameters were treated as normalized passive proxies, and
reciprocal axial coupling was solved globally. This analysis complements
earlier connectomic proposals for anatomical credit assignment and the larger
MICrONS morphology map
\citep{kornfeld2020anatomical,weis2025morphology}.

For neuron $u$, we define a $K$-channel feedback dictionary
$\Phi_{u,K}\in\mathbb R^{N_u\times K}$. Rows correspond to synaptic or
compartment locations, and columns correspond to available feedback fields.
An \emph{address} is one column whose support and gain specify the locations
that can share a task-dependent coefficient. A point neuron has only the
shared column $\bm 1$. Depth bins provide coarse columns, an ancestry
dictionary provides nested subtree columns, and an unrestricted dense
rank-$K$ subspace serves as a descriptive upper bound. This definition
separates neuronal ownership from within-tree addressing and emphasizes that
dendrites provide structured rather than arbitrary credit bandwidth.

We quantify this distinction as follows. For a fixed family of
$K$-column dictionaries $\Phi_{u,K}$ and a distribution of exact
task-dependent coefficient fields $\bm c_u$, let $P_{\Phi_{u,K}}$ be the
$W$-orthogonal projector onto $\operatorname{col}(\Phi_{u,K})$. For a tolerated
unexplained field-energy fraction $0<\epsilon<1$, the
$\epsilon$-address dimension is

\begin{equation}
d_{\epsilon,u}=\min\left\{K:
\frac{\mathbb E\lVert P_{\Phi_{u,K}}\bm c_u\rVert_W^2}
{\mathbb E\lVert\bm c_u\rVert_W^2}\geq1-\epsilon\right\}.
\label{eq:addressdimension}
\end{equation}

A point neuron supplied with one neuronal teaching coefficient has a rank-one
task-dependent address space, regardless of its number of synapses. A
$K$-route dendritic dictionary has address rank at most
$\operatorname{rank}(\Phi_{u,K})\leq K$ and can express $K$ structured
coefficient fields without assigning the routes to $K$ separate neurons. This
is a resource statement, not a claim that dendrites create arbitrary feedback
bandwidth. A point-neuron system can reproduce the same field if it receives
$K$ separately routed teaching coefficients or an equivalent gated feature
expansion (Supplementary Information).

For an excitatory site $i$ and candidate inhibitory route $k$, the direct
topological route matrix was
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{ik}=A_{ik}\beta_k,
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
where $A_{ik}$ indicates route ancestry and
$\beta_k=g_k^I/g_k^{\mathrm{tot}}$ is the normalized inhibitory gain. This
matrix is a route-sensitivity proxy rather than the exact passive-cable
derivative used below. Its nested domains addressed a median 2.67\% of mapped
excitatory input and had a mean participation rank of 20.81, compared with
rank one for a scalar broadcast
(Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}a,b and Supplementary Fig.~S20c,d).

We measured the fraction of a modeled field retained by $K$ feedback
channels. For the weighted field $\overline{\bm g}=W^{1/2}\bm g$ and dictionary
$\overline\Phi_{T,K}=W^{1/2}\Phi_{T,K}$, let $\overline P_{\Phi}$ be the
Euclidean projector onto $\operatorname{col}(\overline\Phi_{T,K})$. Field
capture is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_K(\bm g;\Phi,W)=\frac{\lVert \overline P_{\Phi}\overline{\bm g}\rVert_2^2}
{\lVert\overline{\bm g}\rVert_2^2}
=1-\frac{\lVert\overline{\bm g}-\overline P_{\Phi}\overline{\bm g}\rVert_2^2}
{\lVert\overline{\bm g}\rVert_2^2}.
\label{eq:capture}
\end{equation}
Here, $W$ contains summed mapped excitatory area, so
Eq.~\ref{eq:capture} is primarily an anatomical field-energy measure. Only
when $\overline{\bm g}$ is an exact gradient in the explicitly transformed
coordinates $\overline{\bm w}=W^{-1/2}\bm w$ and the transformed loss has
$L_W$-Lipschitz gradients does $\mathcal C_K$ also equal the ratio of projected
and full one-step descent guarantees at step $1/L_W$ (Supplementary
Information).

As a model-matched ceiling, we generated perturbation fields directly from
the route kernel $\Gamma$. At eight channels, ancestry routes captured 0.487
of the energy in these model-derived route-perturbation fields while using
6.92\% of the connections in a dense feedback matrix. This value was 85.0\%
of the field energy captured by an unconstrained dense eight-channel oracle.
Random paths captured 0.285, depth bins 0.222, and ancestry-shuffled routes
0.198. The ancestry-route advantage over each control was positive in every
cell, and hierarchical cell-and-stream intervals excluded zero
(Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}d and Supplementary Fig.~S20f,g). The ordering also
held when the analysis was restricted to directly typed presynaptic E/I
inputs: ancestry routes captured 0.696, compared with 0.347 for random paths,
0.447 for depth bins, and 0.308 after ancestry shuffling
(Supplementary Fig.~S20h).

Because these fields were sampled from the same kernel that defined the
routes, they provide a ceiling rather than the primary evidence. The primary
test used an independent generator: exact state-matched responses to focal
shunts in the reciprocal cable model. At eight channels, ancestry routes
captured 0.458, compared with 0.363 for random paths and 0.249 after row
shuffling. Both advantages were positive in all eight cells. Differences from
depth bins (0.014) and degree- and depth-matched surrogate trees (0.035) were
inconclusive. For the latter comparison, the cell-bootstrap interval excluded
zero, but the primary paired Wilcoxon test did not ($P=0.078$). These results
support sparse structure beyond random assignment but do not demonstrate an
advantage of fine ancestry over coarse depth and degree
(Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}c and Supplementary Fig.~S20i,j).

Normalizing the ceiling comparison by wiring makes the resource claim
explicit. Per unit wiring, ancestry routes captured 14.2 times as much field
energy as the dense oracle (95\% cell-bootstrap interval 10.7--18.2). The
ancestry-shuffled control, evaluated at the same 6.9\% wiring density, reached
5.3-fold. The anatomy-specific advantage at matched density was therefore
2.7-fold, while the remaining gain came from sparsity itself
(Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}e,f).

In 47 additional cells, ancestry routes captured 0.756 using 8.08\% of dense
wiring, compared with 0.302--0.441 for structural controls. Every within-cell
contrast was positive (Supplementary Fig.~S10). This nucleus-disjoint cohort
came from the same mouse and from a convenience-derived historical
reconstruction.

We repeated the model-matched structural endpoint in the biologically
independent Pinky v185 mouse. An outcome-independent 12-cell spatial sample was
fixed before mesh or synapse outcomes were opened. All cells were processed
without error, and ten passed the direct-type QC gate. At four channels,
ancestry routes captured 0.902 of modeled field energy, close to the dense PCA
ceiling of 0.926. Random routes captured 0.374, depth bins 0.316, and shuffled
ancestry 0.245.

The ancestry-route advantages were 0.528 relative to random routes
(descriptive 95\% cell-bootstrap interval 0.386--0.641; 9/10 cells), 0.586
relative to depth bins (0.484--0.696; 10/10), and 0.657 relative to shuffled
ancestry (0.585--0.734; 10/10; Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}g and Supplementary
Fig.~S27). The effect therefore had the same direction in both animals.
Because only one volume was available per animal and the target fields
remained model matched, this result is a cross-animal structural replication,
not population inference or evidence that the routes conveyed task credit in
vivo.

We also asked whether the unbalanced reconstructed trees realize the
coarse-to-fine coordinate hierarchy assumed by the theory. A deterministic
weighted tree-Haar construction separated the non-scalar ancestry-route field
into coarse, intermediate and fine anatomical contrasts
(Supplementary Section~S7 and Supplementary Fig.~S25). In the disjoint
47-cell cohort, coarse modes contained 0.407 of route-field energy while
comprising 0.192 of the non-scalar dimensions. This corresponds to a
2.35-fold enrichment relative to isotropic coordinate noise
(95\% cell-bootstrap interval 2.00--2.88; 47/47 cells).

However, coarse energy exceeded a column-permuted ancestry control by only
0.0345, and the interval crossed zero
($-0.0025$--0.0713; 31/47; $P=0.078$). The original eight cells had shown a
positive excess. Irregular arbors therefore provide coarse multiscale
coordinates, but the apparent fine-anatomy-specific enrichment did not
replicate.

All comparisons used the same number of channels. They demonstrate anatomical
compression of modeled fields, not the presence of task gradients or the use
of these routes by the animal.

The route analysis above used inferred inhibitory distributions. We therefore
asked whether synapse-resolved inhibitory connectivity is organized in a way
that could support branch-level control. This question builds on physiological
and modeling studies of tunable dendritic inhibition and inhibitory plasticity
\citep{lovettbarron2012regulation,vogels2011inhibitory}. We joined the disjoint
reconstruction cohort to the published MICrONS inhibitory census
\citep{schneidermizell2025inhibitory}. Stable nucleus identity linked twenty of 47
target cells across releases; five had different root IDs after
re-segmentation. The source tables contained 116,401 input locations and 2,789
known contacts from 116 inhibitory cells. Of these, 114,775 inputs and 2,637
contacts from 114 inhibitory cells mapped within $5\,\mu$m. This is a
structural analysis from one mouse. It does not identify a learning signal,
and the packaged census lacks a per-target proofreading-status field.

For each inhibitory cell--target connection, we retained one representative
contact from every independent axonal clump. The 402 connections with at least
two clumps provided a within-axon test. Repeated contacts from the same
inhibitory axon were 12.0~$\mu$m closer along the target tree than
compartment- and path-distance-matched input locations
(95\% bootstrap interval $-21.5$ to $-4.2$~$\mu$m; 15 of 20 target means
were negative; one-sided Wilcoxon $p=0.0032$). They also shared 0.0357 more
normalized path (0.0091--0.0766; 15 of 20; $p=0.0028$) and addressed
descendant input domains with 0.0322 greater weighted cosine overlap
(0.0060--0.0743; 14 of 20; $p=0.0053$;
Supplementary Fig.~S12d--f).

These target-level comparisons do not distinguish dendritic organization from
repeated sampling of the same axons or from local tissue geometry. Averaging
first within each of 92 presynaptic axons preserved the tree-distance effect
($p=0.0008$), whereas the shared-path and domain-overlap effects were not
reliable ($p=0.104$ and $p=0.393$). When candidate locations were jointly
matched for path distance and three-dimensional proximity, none of the three
contrasts was reliable ($p=0.108$, $0.826$ and $0.54$, respectively).
Repeated contacts are therefore spatially co-targeted, but these data do not
establish geometry-independent organization over descendant credit domains.

Inhibitory classes operated at different spatial scales. After retaining one
contact per axonal clump, distal dendrite-targeting contacts addressed a
smaller fraction of target-cell input than perisomatic-targeting contacts in
all 20 cells. The mean within-target difference between class medians was
$-0.0162$ ($-0.0231$ to $-0.0105$; one-sided
$p=9.5\times10^{-7}$; Supplementary Fig.~S12g). This class effect is not a
global placement advantage. Across all inhibitory contacts, descendant-domain
size did not differ reliably from matched generic input locations
(mean difference 0.0016, $p=0.546$; Supplementary Fig.~S12h).

Finally, we measured the internal compressibility of the observed inhibitory
route dictionary. At eight sites, the highest-leverage columns captured 0.743
of the same weighted dictionary, close to the dense singular-vector oracle
value of 0.774. Random actual sites captured 0.379, depth bins 0.397, and
depth-preserving ancestry shuffling 0.369. Every selected-route contrast was
positive in all 20 targets (one-sided $p=9.5\times10^{-7}$ for each;
Supplementary Fig.~S12i,j).

Leverage selection is an anatomical compression oracle. It measures
redundancy within a dictionary using columns from that same dictionary; it
does not demonstrate independent credit routing or show that the animal
selected those sites. The census therefore establishes class-dependent spatial
scales and available nested domains, while the axon-clustered and
three-dimensional matching controls prevent a stronger functional-routing
interpretation.

\subsection*{Shunting regulates route gain only in permissive electrotonic regimes}

Topology alone does not show that conductance can selectively control the
available routes. At the textbook passive calibration, the primary contrast
was effectively null. Localization emerged only in permissive normalized or
higher-conductance regimes, as quantified below. We therefore specified a
focal intervention before examining its outcome. This adjoint-credit question
extends earlier analyses of spatial inhibitory domains in dendrites
\citep{gidon2012inhibition}.

Let $G$ denote the passive conductance matrix of a reconstructed tree, with
voltage $\bm V=G^{-1}\bm b$, where $\bm b$ is the current-source vector. Let
$\bm e_s$ and $\bm e_k$ be standard basis vectors at the soma and focal
compartment $k$, respectively. For a somatic loss, the adjoint error is
$\bm q=G^{-1}\bm e_s\,\partial\mathcal L/\partial V_s$. A shunt of
conductance $\kappa_k$ at compartment $k$ changes the transport operator
according to
\begin{equation}
\bm q'=\bm q-
\frac{\kappa_k q_k}{1+\kappa_k(G^{-1})_{kk}}G^{-1}\bm e_k.
\label{eq:sherman}
\end{equation}
Writing $\bm c_k=G^{-1}\bm e_k$ and $r_k=(G^{-1})_{kk}$ gives
$\Delta\bm q=-a_k\bm c_k$, where
$|a_k|=\kappa_k|q_k|/(1+\kappa_kr_k)$. The dose controls response amplitude,
which increases monotonically and saturates at $|q_k|/r_k$, while the cable
column $\bm c_k$ controls spatial spread. For descendant set $D(k)$ and
matched comparison set $C(k)$, the transport-only selectivity criterion is

\begin{equation}
S_k=\frac{\operatorname{median}_{i\in D(k)}|c_{k,i}|}
{\operatorname{median}_{j\in C(k)}|c_{k,j}|}>1.
\label{eq:selectivitycriterion}
\end{equation}

Equation~\ref{eq:selectivitycriterion} separates electrotonic selectivity
from perturbation strength. It is necessary for descendant enrichment of
absolute adjoint change under this median contrast, but it is not sufficient
for the synaptic-gradient statistic, which also depends on baseline adjoints
and local driving forces. A first-order-current-matched additive perturbation
changes the voltage-driving term but leaves $G^{-1}$ unchanged. This provides a
direct test. After a state-matching compensatory current restores somatic
voltage and scalar output error, conductance shunting should alter exact
synaptic gradients more strongly among descendants than among depth-matched
unrelated sites. Reciprocal coupling can still change credit outside the
descendant set.

We tested 101 depth-stratified focal sites across the eight cells
(Fig.~\ref{fig:focal}b). At the unit dose, the localization index, defined as
the descendant median log-gradient attenuation minus the corresponding median
at depth-matched unrelated sites, was 0.104 for shunting and 0.035 for the
matched additive perturbation. The cell-level paired difference was 0.069 log
units (95\% bootstrap interval 0.053--0.086), and it was positive in all eight
cells. The contrast increased monotonically with dose, from 0.018 at 0.25
local-conductance units to 0.128 at two units. True focal relations also
exceeded relations sampled from other focal sites by 0.080
(0.059--0.098), again in all eight cells. The comparison was stratified by
topological-depth quartile whenever another site was available
(Fig.~\ref{fig:focal}b,c and Supplementary Fig.~S21c).

Because a synaptic gradient is the product of adjoint error and local driving
force, we algebraically fixed each factor in turn. Keeping the full post-shunt
voltage and driving force but replacing the post-shunt adjoint with its
baseline value produced localization of 0.026. Restoring the post-shunt
adjoint increased localization to 0.104, an exact transport-specific
difference of 0.079 that was positive in all eight cells
(Fig.~\ref{fig:focal}d). Because the post-shunt driving-force field is fixed,
this comparison isolates the effect of changed error transport without relying
on first-order current matching. The complementary adjoint-only state produced
localization of 0.130.

A two-factor Shapley sensitivity analysis assigned 0.087 of the planned
shunt-minus-additive contrast to the adjoint change and $-0.017$ to the
driving-force change. These components reconciled exactly to the 0.069 net
contrast at every site (Supplementary Section~S8). The factor substitutions
are algebraic controls, not separate biological interventions.

The somatic state-matching residual was numerically zero, all conductance
matrices remained positive definite, and adjoint gradients agreed with finite
differences to a maximum relative error of $1.03\times10^{-6}$. The principal
contrast was also robust to changes in normalized conductance scale and
inhibitory reversal potential (Supplementary Fig.~S21f). Restricting both
synaptic weighting and focal sites to directly typed inputs produced a
shunt-minus-additive contrast of 0.101 (0.075--0.129), positive in all eight
cells (Supplementary Fig.~S21g). In the disjoint sensitivity cohort, the same
contrast was 0.138 (0.116--0.162) across 235 sites in 45 eligible cells and was
positive in every cell (Supplementary Fig.~S10f). These results identify a
conductance-specific gradient-localization mechanism at fixed somatic state in
the normalized proxy model.

We then calibrated axial and leak conductances from reconstructed radii and
lengths using explicit specific resistances. At
$R_a=150\,\Omega\,$cm and $R_m=15{,}000\,\Omega\,$cm$^2$, the median
axial-to-leak ratio was 68.0. Under this calibration, the
shunt-minus-additive contrast was $-0.0019$ in the original cohort
(two of eight cells positive) and 0.0024 in the v661 cohort
(45 of 45 positive). Lower effective membrane resistance, representing a
progressively higher-conductance state, restored a larger effect. In the
original eight-cell cohort, the contrast was 0.0164 at
$R_m=1{,}000\,\Omega\,$cm$^2$ and 0.0674 at
$R_m=300\,\Omega\,$cm$^2$, with all eight cells positive in both conditions
(Fig.~\ref{fig:focal}f).

Low input resistance and conductance-driven gain modulation are established
features of in-vivo-like high-conductance states
\citep{chance2002gain,destexhe2003highconductance}. The present values use an
explicit relative calibration of synapse size because MICrONS synaptic area is
not a direct conductance measurement. The focal signature is therefore a
model prediction that depends on electrotonic regime. It is not a universal
property of the reconstructed arbors and does not show that inhibitory
synapses carried endogenous teaching signals in the animal.

We next froze a passive sensitivity matrix that removed the relative-dose
confound. Across the same 101 sites and eight cells, the matrix crossed three
membrane resistances, three levels of distributed leak-like background
conductance, and relative-local, fixed-absolute and
input-conductance-normalized doses. This yielded 16,362
site--condition--intervention rows.

At a fixed 0.5-nS shunt, the shunt-minus-additive localization contrast was
0.0104 at $R_m=300\,\Omega\,$cm$^2$, 0.0073 at
$R_m=1{,}000\,\Omega\,$cm$^2$, and $-0.0039$ at
$R_m=15{,}000\,\Omega\,$cm$^2$. The first two contrasts were positive in all
eight cells, whereas only two of eight cells were positive in the last regime.
At the high-resistance setting, adding one and four times the baseline leak as
distributed background conductance increased the
input-conductance-normalized contrast from $-0.0004$ to 0.1210 and 0.2587,
respectively. Every tested descendant gradient was attenuated in magnitude;
none was enhanced or changed sign. Passive shunting therefore provides graded
attenuation rather than signed reversal. Its conductance-specific spatial
advantage requires both sufficient dose and a selective electrotonic column
(Supplementary Fig.~S11).

Finally, we tested whether localization persists with weak active
conductances rather than a purely passive operator. The ensemble was evaluated
at the permissive electrotonic operating point identified above,
$R_m=1{,}000\,\Omega\,$cm$^2$ with one baseline-leak unit of distributed
background conductance (Methods). It therefore extends the localization result
within that regime rather than restoring the effect at the high-resistance
calibration.

For each of the eight cells, we solved 64 channel-density draws containing
voltage-dependent Na, K, Ca, HCN and NMDA currents, then linearized the exact
nonlinear steady-state residual to obtain its local Jacobian. All 512
equilibria were accepted, every Jacobian remained positive definite, and the
maximum equilibrium residual was $9.5\times10^{-11}$. The draws were nested
sensitivities rather than independent cells. At the unit
input-conductance-normalized dose, focal shunting produced descendant
localization of 0.529 (95\% cell-bootstrap interval 0.513--0.541), attenuated
every modeled descendant gradient, caused no sign flips, and reduced descendant
gradient energy to 0.330 of baseline
(Fig.~\ref{fig:focal}e,g and Supplementary Table~S7). Localization exceeded
the state-matched additive control by 0.382 (0.356--0.409), with a positive
difference in all eight cells (two-sided Wilcoxon $P=0.0078$). The effect
increased monotonically across the three doses. This active steady-state
ensemble strengthens the local-Jacobian prediction but is not a simulation of
channel kinetics or spiking.

\subsection*{Measured responses reveal a boundary for morphology-specific alignment}

The structural-capacity analysis used ancestry-kernel perturbations, and the
focal analysis used modeled adjoints. We then asked whether the same routes
correspond to measured functional inputs and response-derived task gradients.
A MICrONS/DANDI join identified seven reconstructed targets and 136 repeated
visual conditions per scan. The original one-scan-per-target cohort contained
69 connected, imaged excitatory partners.

After controlling for Euclidean separation and depth, functional similarity
did not increase consistently with shared ancestry. The mean target-level
partial rank effect was $-0.069$ (95\% target-bootstrap interval
$-0.255$ to $0.108$), with a positive effect in three of seven cells
(Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}a and Supplementary Fig.~S22a). This estimand asks
whether within-tree position adds organization beyond geometry and complements
the broader like-to-like wiring principle reported in the same MICrONS volume
\citep{ding2025wiring}. Applying the deterministic rule ``all
automatic-conservative scans with at least five connected imaged partners''
expanded the analysis to 13 scan observations. After scans were averaged
within each target, the partial shared-path effect was $-0.048$
($-0.224$ to 0.118), with a positive effect in three of seven cells. The
scan-complete analysis removed selection ambiguity without changing the null
conclusion.

For each postsynaptic response, we trained a branch surrogate and projected its
exact error into four-channel dictionaries. Each partner was mapped through
its dominant contact to one nonlinear state on the first major branch below
the soma, so all sites on that branch shared one task-dependent error. Because
each target had only 3--8 occupied major branches, this model cannot resolve
within-branch credit or provide a strong test of fine topology. Stimulus
identities were held out.

Mean held-out field capture was 0.999 for a training-only dense oracle, 0.475
for ancestry routes (labelled ``morphology'' in the display items), 0.564 for
nonempty random paths, 0.621 for depth bins, 0.446 for ancestry-shuffled paths,
and 0.222 for a scalar broadcast (Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}b). The
ancestry-route-minus-ancestry-shuffled capture difference was 0.028
(95\% interval $-0.146$ to $0.206$), with three of seven target cells
positive.

Learning outcomes showed the same boundary. Mean normalized held-out error was
0.778 for exact backpropagation, 0.781 for the dense oracle, 0.791 for depth
bins, 0.801 for scalar broadcast, 0.834 for random paths, 0.837 for ancestry
routes, and 0.842 after ancestry shuffling. Ancestry routes were therefore
close to the ancestry-shuffled control and did not outperform the simpler
depth-bin or scalar dictionaries on this task. The paired ancestry-route
improvement was 0.004 normalized-error units
(95\% interval $-0.021$ to $0.027$), with a positive difference in four of
seven targets (Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}c).

A pooled capture--performance association disappeared after centering within
target ($\rho=-0.150$; label-permutation $P=0.419$;
Supplementary Fig.~S22g). Within targets, higher capture predicted lower error
at one channel ($\rho=-0.621$, $P=0.0006$) and eight channels
($\rho=-0.480$, $P=0.0104$), but not at two or four channels
(Source Data). Channel-wise ancestry-minus-shuffle contrasts
(Supplementary Fig.~S22h), reliability thresholds and a matched subset also
failed to establish a morphology-specific benefit.

We next replaced the coarse branch surrogate with an exact passive solve on
each complete compressed reconstructed tree. Every imaged partner supplied a
trainable nonnegative conductance at its mapped segment, and the exact local
rule used the trial-specific soma-to-segment adjoint. Restricted rules used
only scalar output error, local eligibility and a fixed four-channel feedback
vector calibrated from topology before learning. All 13 eligible scans and ten
held-out-stimulus splits per scan completed without exclusion, yielding 520
method fits. Split replicates were averaged within each scan, and scans were
then averaged within each of the seven target cells.

Exact compartment error achieved a mean normalized held-out MSE of 0.788.
The ancestry-route rule (labelled ``topology'' in Source Data), random-route
rule and site-shuffled rule reached 0.832, 0.833 and 0.839, respectively
(Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}d). Relative to site shuffling, ancestry routes
improved MSE by 0.0070 units
(95\% target-bootstrap interval $-0.0207$ to 0.0321). Relative to random
routes, the improvement was 0.0013 ($-0.0090$ to 0.0113). Neither contrast
provided reliable evidence of an advantage.

At the common exact checkpoint, ancestry routes captured 0.232 of the
eligibility-weighted update field, below site-shuffled routes at 0.439 and
random routes at 0.358 (Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}a). Restricted dictionaries had
identical effective rank in every scan--split run, and exact conductance
gradients agreed with finite differences to a maximum relative error of
$3.1\times10^{-7}$.

Route availability therefore did not imply route use in either the coarse or
segment-resolved task. In the coarse model, four dense dimensions nearly
matched exact learning. On the full tree, however, the anatomical routes did
not correspond reliably to measured-response credit.

The dense-oracle capture curve explains why the coarse-model null is expected
under the credit-operator account. A single channel already captured 0.965 of
this response family's credit energy, corresponding to an effective rank of
1.07. A four-channel budget therefore exceeds the task's credit rank, placing
every equal-rank dictionary in the span-saturated regime where the theory
predicts a tie (Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}b; the synthesis plane in
Fig.~\ref{fig:creditphase}g places this cohort in that regime). In the coarse
model, oracle calibration makes each restricted dictionary a capacity ceiling.
The full-tree rules, by contrast, were calibrated from anatomy alone and then
held fixed during learning. These null results do not exclude other response
windows, greater input coverage or electrophysiologically fitted
conductances.

Measured responses provided no reliable evidence of morphology-specific
alignment. The conditional theory nevertheless predicts that the same
dictionary should become useful when task credit rotates into its span. To
test this prediction directly, we generated alignment-controlled quadratic
objectives on each reconstructed tree. Exact-gradient fields were rotated from
the span of the morphology dictionary into its orthogonal complement while
channel count, dictionary, gradient energy and loss curvature were held fixed.
The same fields were evaluated with morphology, random-path, depth-bin and
ancestry-shuffled dictionaries using norm-matched or iterative projected
descent (Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}e,f and Supplementary Fig.~S5).

The morphology dictionary captured exactly the imposed aligned energy, ranging
from 0 at complete orthogonality to 1 at complete alignment. At 40\%
alignment, it captured 0.400 and supported 0.615 of the loss reduction from a
norm-matched full-gradient step. Its capture advantage over the three controls
ranged from 0.203 to 0.228 and was positive in all eight cells. At complete
orthogonality, morphology lost to every control in all eight cells. At complete
alignment, it exceeded the controls by 0.710--0.799 and won in all eight.
Thus, the same fixed anatomy changes from an unhelpful to a useful feedback
basis when only the correspondence between its span and task credit is
changed. Across the three control dictionaries and all alignment levels,
initial capture tracked 20-step progress within each cell
(median Spearman $\rho=0.990$, range 0.969--0.996). The one-step and 20-step
relations are shown in Supplementary Fig.~S5.

This constructive sufficiency test imposes alignment, fits oracle projection
coefficients and uses a quadratic objective. It links field capture to
optimization under controlled conditions, but it does not show that MICrONS
cells used these gradients. The independently measured responses provided no
reliable morphology-specific benefit.

The theory also assumes that a neuron-specific teaching coordinate is
available. We tested this assumption using published source data from a
six-animal brain--computer-interface experiment in which the experimental
mapping fixed the causal signs of two selected neurons, P+ and P-
\citep{francioni2026vectorized}. Before inspecting the sign, we defined one
contrast: the standardized dendritic population residual during error
reduction minus the residual during error increase. If the dendritic signal
tracks causal credit rather than a common scalar error, this contrast should
be positive around P+ and negative around P-.

The P+ contrast was positive in five of six animals
(mean 0.0753; 95\% bootstrap interval 0.0295--0.1214; exact one-sided
random-sign permutation $P=0.0313$). The P- contrast was negative in all six
(mean $-0.1498$; $-0.2054$ to $-0.0866$; $P=0.0156$). Their signed
separation was positive in every animal
(mean 0.2251; 0.1416--0.3108; $P=0.0156$;
Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}g). A decomposition of the six animal contrast vectors
showed that 83.7\% of pooled squared projection energy lay in the signed P+/P-
mode, compared with 16.3\% in a common scalar mode. The animal-bootstrap 95\%
interval for signed-mode energy was 60.6--95.7\%, and the
leave-one-animal-out range was 76.5--90.2\%
(Supplementary Fig.~S17c). Descriptive neuron-level residual distributions
from the published source table are shown in Supplementary Fig.~S17d.

With only six animals, these exact permutation tests lie at or one step above
their resolution floor. The analysis independently supports the first level
of the theory, signed neuron identity, but does not determine how the signal is
transported within a dendritic tree or uniquely implicate shunting.

Taken together, the experiments occupy a common
alignment-by-bandwidth plane (Fig.~\ref{fig:creditphase}g). Low bandwidth loses
neuronal identity, whereas high bandwidth can make equal-rank spans coincide.
Between these limits, ancestry routes help only when enough task credit falls
inside their span. The trained $K=4$ interior optimum, the spectral and
controlled-alignment sweeps, the credit-reversal task and the coarse
measured-response null all fall on the predicted side of this boundary. The
full-tree null is not plotted because its task rank was not estimated in a
comparable way (Methods). The shaded regions denote theory-derived regimes;
they are not a fitted classifier of the experiments.

\section*{Discussion}

Every exact synaptic gradient separates into two components: locally available
eligibility, determined by presynaptic activity, driving force and input
resistance, and a transported compartment error. Dendritic learning can
therefore be framed as a specific approximation problem: recover a defined
credit field from restricted biological signals. Point-neuron theories ask how
an error reaches a neuron or gates its eligibility. A dendritic theory must
also determine which subtree receives that coordinate and how strongly the
signal is conveyed. Learning can fail at any of these levels by losing neuron
identity, assigning credit to the wrong tree or descendants, or misestimating
route gain.

The prospective experiments separate these failure modes quantitatively. One
coordinate per neuron closes most of the gap produced by scalar feedback,
whereas deranging the neuron-to-tree map at matched bandwidth creates a smaller
but reproducible cost. The 2,700-fit credit-reversal factorial then shows that
the value of within-neuron structure depends on bandwidth. Correct ancestry is
inferior to unrestricted low-rank routes at one and two coordinates, gains a
small but reliable advantage over the best matched non-anatomical control at
four coordinates, and ties at full rank. It also outperforms a degree- and
depth-matched rewired tree at intermediate budgets.

Dendritic, flat, grouped-point and explicitly gated point implementations are
identical when they receive the same routed fields. The benefit therefore
resides in structured address resources rather than in dendritic material
itself. Exact transported feedback also makes the derived local eligibility
sufficient to approach matched backpropagation. The detached clean audit found
no material exact-transport--backpropagation difference, which supports
empirical agreement but not formal equivalence. Gradient correspondence, in
turn, predicts immediate descent.

The credit-operator bound provides a common explanation for these results.
Route quality is determined not by capture alone, but by retained task signal
relative to finite-step gain effects and admitted stochastic noise. This
utility predicted the completed factorial more accurately than spectral
capture alone. In the independent phase experiment, ancestry became useful as
task covariance rotated into its span, routed depth was best when it matched
task hierarchy, and projection improved a noisy gradient only when rejected
noise exceeded discarded signal.

These findings do not provide a way for a local rule to outperform exact
full-batch backpropagation on the same objective. Instead, they explain how a
restricted stochastic rule can improve on another noisy or bandwidth-limited
rule. Backpropagation followed by the same projection remains the appropriate
upper control. The interior-optimum reanalysis further sharpens this
distinction. The bound located the trained depth optimum whenever such an
optimum existed, whereas the trained $K=4$ anatomical advantage emerged beyond
one-step initialization geometry (Supplementary Fig.~S16).

The comparisons can therefore be ordered explicitly. A restricted rule does
not outperform exact full-batch backpropagation at matched step norm. It can
outperform stochastic backpropagation when rejected noise exceeds discarded
signal. It can outperform a bandwidth-matched non-anatomical rule when the
task covariance falls sufficiently inside the anatomical span. Finally, any
within-tree address can improve on scalar or neuron-only feedback when exact
credit varies across the tree in an addressable way. Reliability-weighted
route gains strengthen these comparisons when branch signal-to-noise ratios
are heterogeneous. Figure~\ref{fig:creditphase}g places every
bandwidth-varied experiment in the resulting alignment-by-bandwidth plane,
including the measured-response null in the regime where the theory predicts
a null. In operator terms, topology sets the span of addressable fields,
conductance sets the metric that weights their use, and the task determines
whether either resource is useful.

Depth can influence learning through at least three distinct mechanisms.
First, nested branch points reuse route coordinates across many leaves and
provide progressively finer addresses; the matched-depth phase experiment
isolates this role. Second, serial dendritic stages can compose nonlinear
forward operations, a mechanism deliberately absent from the quadratic phase
experiment. The exact-resource positive-rate task isolates one such case.
Aligned divisive composition improved accuracy by about 31 percentage points
in both the $H=2$ and $H=3$ variants, whereas literal grouped-point models
remained flat and reversed placement made serial depth harmful. Independent
and shuffled-sensor controls removed the H3 benefit, and the raw-additive
series worsened with depth. These findings support hierarchical shunting in a
matched, calibrated task family; they do not imply that deeper trees are
generally better.

Third, deeper physical routes convey a shared coordinate through increasingly
differentiated branch states. Consistent with this transport cost, the
shared-soma gradient cosine fell from 0.946 at D1 to 0.767 at D3, while exact
path transport reproduced autograd at every checkpoint. The earlier additive
fixed-contact control also showed no intrinsic benefit of depth when terminal
branches and contacts were fixed. The primary requirement is therefore a
signed coordinate for the correct neuron, followed conditionally by enough
task-aligned address resolution and computational depth. Depth alone is not
sufficient.

The $H=4$ extension strengthens this qualification. D4 remained far above D1
but fell reliably below D3 under exact BP, falsifying a one-to-one relation
between trained physical depth and task hierarchy. The benefit of serial
composition therefore saturates in this task family, even though a restricted
shared-coordinate rule continues to change when another stage is added. The
fixed-D3 task-family factorial shows that the serial benefit is graded rather
than specific to one generator. It is largest for nested multiplicative
factors, smaller when their spatial covariance is flattened, and absent under
BP when the required ratio is already local. Path-transport LocalCA preserves
the nested and flat benefits but reverses the local-ratio contrast, showing
that task computation and restricted credit can interact adversely at the same
physical depth.

The additional controls also define the boundary between point and dendritic
models. The serial tree outperformed a resource-identical nonserial star only
under the aligned hierarchy, and it outperformed the stricter literal
grouped-point model at both H2 and H3. Ordinary parameter-matched point MLPs,
however, exceeded the tree by about seven points. Dendritic depth is therefore
a structured inductive and wiring resource in this task, not an absolute
expressive separation. The credit ladder defines the complementary boundary
between BP and local learning. Soma-broadcast autograd nearly matched BP under
a common optimizer, optimizer choice explained the largest raw difference
from shared-soma LocalCA, and path specificity recovered most of the remaining
local-rule deficit.

At matched resources, the tree's contribution can thus be divided into three
terms: a forward-composition term isolated by the backpropagation depth
contrast, an address-resolution term isolated by the bandwidth factorial, and
a transport cost reflected by the declining shared-coordinate cosine. Local
learning measures the interaction among these terms.

Real dendrites constrain how a neuronal coordinate can be distributed.
Reconstructed arbors compress both model-matched fields and independently
generated cable fields into sparse ancestry-defined supports while using only
a small fraction of dense feedback wiring. When normalized by wiring, ancestry
provides an order-of-magnitude increase in captured energy per feedback
connection over a dense oracle, including a 2.7-fold anatomy-specific
contribution at matched density (Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}e,f). In this account,
a dendritic tree multiplexes $K$ addressable fields within one arbor. An
equivalent point implementation requires the same $K$ separately addressable
teaching coordinates, and its wiring cost depends on how those coordinates are
delivered.

Fine morphology, however, did not reliably outperform controls matched for
depth and parent degree. The synapse-resolved inhibitory census likewise
supports coarse co-targeting and class-specific spatial scales, but its
shared-path effects do not survive axon-level aggregation and joint geometric
matching. The independent Pinky result reproduces the direction of the
model-matched capacity endpoint across animals; the inhibitory and functional
measurements remain one-mouse analyses. These data establish the availability
of candidate addresses, not their use in endogenous learning.

Shunting has a specific and limited role in this hierarchy. Unlike models in
which inhibitory circuits compute an error
\citep{rossbroich2023disinhibitory,galloni2026cellular,greedy2026celltype},
the focal conductance studied here changes the sensitivity of routes that pass
through a compartment. Exact factor freezing attributes the positive
localization effect in the normalized proxy mainly to altered adjoint
transport, partly offset by the change in synaptic driving force. Physical
calibration shows that the effect can become very small in passive regimes
with high axial-to-leak coupling and reappear in higher-conductance states.
The focal Green's-function column and cable length constant determine this
spatial boundary: strong axial coupling spreads the perturbation and weakens
the contrast between descendants and matched off-path sites. The passive
inverse-operator corollary is used only as a global magnitude bound,
$\lVert\Delta G^{-1}\rVert_2\leq
\kappa\lVert G^{-1}\rVert_2^2/[1+\kappa/\lambda_{\max}(G)]$; it does not
predict spatial selectivity.

Shunting is therefore a state-dependent route-gain mechanism, not a universal
optimizer or evidence that inhibition carries the teaching signal. The
active-channel ensemble extends this conclusion beyond a passive operator.
Exact linearization at 512 accepted Na/K/Ca/HCN/NMDA steady states preserved
descendant-enriched attenuation without reversing gradient signs. This remains
a local steady-state result rather than a claim about channel kinetics or
spiking.

The reliability phase suggests one possible computational use for this
attenuation. Fixed-step branch gains
$\min\{1,S_b/[c(S_b+N_b)]\}$ suppress unreliable credit and outperform one
global gain when reliability differs across branches. The state-matched
positive-conductance experiment connects this prediction to a physical
input-resistance mechanism in a trained branch model. Reliability-aligned
shunts improved the immediate credit step and final loss relative to a global
shunt, but they did not improve final loss relative to unshunted noisy
learning. An independently computed point gate reproduced the result to
numerical precision, showing that the operation is not mathematically unique
to dendrites.

The online extension removed direct access to $S_b$ and $N_b$ by estimating
them from paired noisy branch-credit samples. It recovered enough relative
reliability structure to outperform global and shuffled adaptive gains, but
its final loss remained worse than both the fixed initial-oracle shunt and
unshunted noisy learning. Local conductance can therefore implement an
estimated route-wise ordering, but the present experiments do not establish a
net adaptive-learning benefit. This differs from the sibling gain--load study,
which asks when branch-local inhibition improves forward signal robustness.
Here, the forward state is matched and conductance weights backward credit
\citep{safaai2026gainload}. The fixed-gain mechanism experiment used
oracle-supplied branch reliabilities and shunt placement, while the adaptive
extension still required paired noisy teaching samples, a known step fraction
and a conductance-to-shunt map. In both experiments, a compensating current
removed forward state changes. The results therefore validate a
conductance-based, state-dependent approximation to a reliability factor, not
autonomous inhibitory plasticity or a complete biological causal chain.

Anatomical capacity is useful only when it matches the task. Neither the 13
eligible visual-response scans nor the 520 learning fits on complete compressed
segment trees revealed a reliable morphology-specific advantage for the
derived objective. By contrast, rotating exact task credit into or out of the
same anatomical span reversed its learning benefit. The six-animal reanalysis
supports the availability of signed neuron-specific coordinates but does not
identify how they are transported within a tree. Route overlap and off-route
leakage also predict one-step interference (Supplementary Fig.~S6). These
results replace the idea that more dendritic structure is inherently better
with a conditional conclusion: route capacity matters when the task requires
credit fields that the arbor can express.

This alignment requirement has a population-level analogue. Motor learning is
faster for perturbations that remain within an existing neural manifold than
for perturbations outside it \citep{sadtler2014constraints}. Our claim is a
subcellular counterpart, not an identification of the population mechanism:
a pre-existing ancestry span helps only when enough of the task-gradient field
falls inside it.

The findings also refine three strands of earlier work. First, studies of
dendritic nonlinearity show that branch-local processing expands the forward
capacity of a single neuron
\citep{poirazi2003pyramidal,london2005dendritic,branco2010single,chavlis2025dendrites}.
Here, forward resources were deliberately matched or budgeted, and the tree's
contribution was measured in feedback bandwidth. The two accounts are
therefore complementary rather than competing.

Second, dendritic-error and segregated-compartment learning models deliver a
teaching signal to one or a few named compartments
\citep{urbanczik2014dendritic,guerguiev2017segregated,sacramento2018dendritic,payeur2021burst}.
The present framework asks how far such a signal can be distributed usefully
within a complete arbor and answers with an explicit
bandwidth--alignment condition rather than a preferred architecture.

Third, observations of branch-specific plasticity and structured synaptic
placement show that learning is spatially organized in vivo
\citep{cichon2015branch,bloss2016structured,bicknell2021synaptic}. Our analysis
specifies what this organization must provide to constitute credit routing
rather than forward gain control: nested addresses whose span corresponds to
task-gradient covariance, together with a perturbation signature that
distinguishes the two mechanisms.

Bio-inspired machine-learning systems similarly use explicit context gates,
active dendritic segments or localized dendritic updates to allocate learning
capacity \citep{sezener2021gated,iyer2022activedendrites,lv2025dendritic}.
These algorithms demonstrate useful engineered routing schemes. The present
theory instead predicts when a restricted biological route should help or fail
at fixed bandwidth.

The same accounting suggests why a circuit might route credit through trees.
With a fixed budget of teaching coefficients, the circuit can allocate one
coordinate to each of many neurons or finer within-tree addresses to fewer
neurons. These implementations are mathematically interchangeable, but their
wiring costs differ. Per-neuron coordinates require long-range feedback axons,
whereas per-branch routes can reuse local inhibitory synapses on existing
arbors. The wiring-efficient allocation therefore depends on whether task
credit varies mainly across neurons or hierarchically within them. From this
perspective, a tree offers an inexpensive way to increase feedback bandwidth,
which is a question of circuit economics rather than computational
exclusivity.

The framework yields a branch-resolved experimental prediction. After somatic
voltage, output and global error are matched, focal dendrite-targeting
inhibition during learning should alter subsequent plasticity more strongly at
spines below the perturbed branch than at equidistant spines on sister
subtrees. The contrast should increase with local input resistance and
perturbation conductance, and a current-matched additive perturbation should
not reproduce it. Simultaneous dendritic recording, focal inhibition and
longitudinal spine measurements could distinguish credit routing from forward
gain control \citep{francioni2026vectorized,cichon2015branch}.

Several limitations define the scope of these conclusions. The theory assumes
steady-state, non-spiking trees with unique directed paths, and the
reconstructed-cell analyses are sensitivity models rather than cell-specific
physiological fits. The primary, sensitivity and inhibitory cohorts come from
one mouse. Pinky contributes one independent mouse only for the model-matched
structural-capacity endpoint. The functional cohort contains seven targets,
projection and transport coefficients are oracles, and the external
six-animal analysis is retrospective.

The trained within-neuron factorial uses one synthetic eight-context hierarchy,
analytically controlled route fields and implementation-equivalent linear
parameterizations. The active extension linearizes steady states rather than
simulating channel kinetics. Temporal eligibility, spikes, recurrent paths and
long-term network adaptation remain outside the model. In particular, branch
plateau potentials and behavioral-timescale synaptic plasticity provide
documented dendritic teaching signals beyond this steady-state theory
\citep{bittner2017btsp,magee2020plasticity}.

The hierarchy-depth phase experiment uses a fixed-state quadratic family and
a prescribed task covariance. The nonlinear physical-depth bridge tests
$H=2$, $H=3$ and $H=4$, three fixed sensor inventories and one calibrated
operating-point family. It does not span the full
$D_{\mathrm r}\times H\times K\times\rho$ design, infer sensor alignment, or
establish prevalence beyond the synthetic task. The positive-conductance
reliability experiment trains excitatory branch weights but fixes oracle
shunts and uses a compensating current clamp. It therefore does not
demonstrate autonomous estimation or learning of shunt placement. We also
establish no memory, latency or benchmark advantage over backpropagation.

Within these limits, the study identifies an information hierarchy for
cellular learning: coordinates identify neurons, ancestry partitions synapses,
conductance regulates routes, and task alignment determines their utility.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Directed conductance-tree network}

Each compartment combines nonnegative excitatory and inhibitory synaptic
conductances with leak and child-to-parent dendritic coupling. Excitatory and
inhibitory reversal potentials were fixed within each experiment, and raw
trainable parameters were transformed into nonnegative conductances. Before
transmitting activity to its parent, a compartment could apply the monotone
branch-output nonlinearity $a_n=f_n(V_n)$. This mechanism is called
``reactivation'' in the implementation; $f_n$ was the identity when
reactivation was disabled. Supplementary Section~S1 gives the complete forward
equations, derivatives with respect to raw parameters, and temporal order of
the local update.

Let $p(n)$ denote the parent of compartment $n$. The chain rule gives the
compartment-error recursion
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial V_n}
=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial V_{p(n)}}
f_n'(V_n)R_{p(n)}^{\mathrm{tot}}g_{n\to p(n)}^{\mathrm{den}}.
\end{equation}
Multiplying this relation along the unique path gives
Eq.~\ref{eq:pathgain}, and differentiating Eq.~\ref{eq:voltage} locally with
respect to $g_i$ gives Eq.~\ref{eq:factorization}. We checked the analytical
gradients against PyTorch automatic differentiation on fixed batches and
checkpoints. Parameter tensors, rather than individual elements, served as the
units of the reconstruction diagnostic.

\subsection*{Local learning and feedback fields}

The three-factor update was computed as the batch average
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\nabla}_{g_i}\mathcal L
=x_iR_n^{\mathrm{tot}}(E_i-V_n)\widehat\delta_n.
\end{equation}
The tested fields $\widehat\delta_n$ included a global scalar, the
matched-width/scalar-fallback implementation, neuron-indexed sharing,
low-rank random feedback, selected paths and exact path transport. The
neuron-indexed field repeats one somatic coordinate across all descendants of
that soma; it does not provide an independent error to every compartment.
When $\widehat\delta_n=\partial\mathcal L/\partial V_n$, the three-factor rule
equals the exact conductance gradient. The exact-transport experiment therefore
separates feedback approximation from local eligibility. Four- and five-factor
variants multiply the three-factor update by bounded, slowly estimated
stage-level reliability terms. We treat these terms as preconditioners rather
than theoretically required teaching signals.

Regular-tree feedback comparisons used MNIST classification with matched
$[3,3]$ morphologies, optimizers, decoders, schedules and paired
initializations within each architecture. The feedback-definition experiment
used 15 independently initialized seeds per architecture and condition. The
exact-transport experiment used five matched shunting seeds per rule and
decoder-update condition. Test data remained hidden during checkpoint
selection. Supplementary Section~S5 and the accompanying machine-readable
configurations report the full hyperparameters and provenance.

This feedback-definition cohort re-executed the released implementation with
the width- and connectivity-matched additive architecture. The original
conference cohort gave
$90.75\pm1.01\to97.15\pm0.12$ for shunting and
$91.58\pm0.61\to97.14\pm0.10$ for additive networks, consistent with the
values reported here \citep{safaai2026localcredit}.

The Fashion-MNIST replication changed only the dataset. It retained the same
regular $[3,3]$ architecture, 128 somata, contact counts, three-factor rule,
local decoder, architecture-specific initialization, optimizer and
validation-based checkpoint selection. Images were flattened to 784 inputs
without normalization or augmentation. For each fresh seed 10200--10209, the
60,000-image training set was divided into 48,000 training and 12,000
validation examples. The official 10,000-image test set was evaluated only
after checkpoint selection. These seed labels are also used by the unrelated
physical-depth cohort, but the experiments are independent.

Sixty fits crossed two architectures with scalar fallback, neuron-indexed
feedback and exact path transport. The primary replication criterion required
the paired neuron-indexed-minus-scalar interval to exclude zero and at least
8/10 seed differences to be positive in each architecture. Intervals used
50,000 paired-seed bootstrap draws, and exact two-sided sign-flip tests
enumerated all $2^{10}$ assignments. The artifact gate required complete,
finite results and checkpoints, the registered seeds and no fallback warning;
all gates passed. A concurrent edit caused the frozen-source guard to divide
each architecture across two source manifests. The exact diff added only an
optional rule override that no resolved configuration enabled, and the audit
retains both manifests.

\subsection*{Regular-tree regime and one-step analyses}

The task, inhibition-dose, depth, broadcast-noise, rule-family, error-source,

Supplementary Section~S5 describes the synthetic context-gating and

Configurations fixed the decoder, optimizer, training horizon, reactivation

\subsection*{Trained within-neuron subtree-address experiment}

The two-stream support-task configuration, task parameters, ten confirmatory
seeds and artifact gates were frozen before the canary outcome was inspected.
Each example contained two 12-dimensional sibling-stream inputs, a binary
context and a balanced binary label. In the context-selected stream, the input
mean was the label sign multiplied by a seed-specific unit teacher vector. The
other stream had the opposite sign at 0.75 times that amplitude. Independent
Gaussian noise with standard deviation 1.15 was then added. Every training and
test split contained identical counts for all four context--label
combinations. Context therefore had zero correlation with label, both streams
were active, and the inactive stream was a structured distractor rather than a
zero mask.

For branch weights $\bm w_A,\bm w_B$, the somatic logit was
\begin{equation}
z=\mathbb 1[c=0]\bm w_A^{\mathsf T}\bm x_A+
\mathbb 1[c=1]\bm w_B^{\mathsf T}\bm x_B.
\end{equation}
All conditions shared the paired initialization, mini-batches and forward
model. The one-neuron and depth-shared fields applied the logistic output error
to both branches. Correct $K=2$ feedback applied the error only to the selected
sibling, whereas fixed derangement applied it to the other sibling. The random
rank-two control rotated the two context coordinates using a seed-fixed
orthogonal matrix. Exact transport and analytic backpropagation used the
selected-branch derivative. The gated-point emulation used the same two
parameter groups and context-gated input eligibility, providing the explicit
point-model resource needed to reproduce the dendritic address.

Each seed used 2,048 training examples and 2,048 independent test examples.
Mini-batch SGD used batch size 64, learning rate 0.3 and 60 epochs, with
Gaussian weight initialization of standard deviation 0.03. Switch interference
was measured after 12 additional epochs on 1,024 balanced-label examples from
context 1 only, without resetting the optimizer or weights. Gradient cosine
and scaled capture used concatenated per-example parameter gradients. For the
one-step analysis, each candidate batch gradient was norm matched to the exact
gradient before a fixed step of 0.25 was applied.

Canary seed 4099 was used only to enforce balance, nondegenerate capture,
minimum exact-route accuracy and exact--BP equivalence. The unchanged code and
configuration were then run for confirmatory seeds 4100--4109. The independent
seed was the paired inferential unit. Intervals used 20,000 paired bootstrap
resamples, and exact two-sided Wilcoxon tests are reported in Source Data.
This experiment tests $K=2$ in one shallow, matched forward architecture and
served as the support check for the larger factorial.

The full subtree-address protocol assigned eight contexts to the leaves of a
balanced binary hierarchy and used eight input streams, each with eight dimensions.
Every context--label cell was exactly balanced. The selected stream carried a
unit label signal. Nonselected streams carried an opposite-sign distractor
whose amplitude increased with tree distance: 0.15 for a sibling, 0.45 within
the same half and 0.75 across halves. Independent Gaussian noise with standard
deviation 1.1 was then added. Each seed supplied 2,048 training, 2,048 test and
1,024 switch examples. Full-batch gradient descent used learning rate 0.3 for
80 training epochs and 20 switch epochs. Confirmatory seeds 4200--4219 were
fixed before outcomes were inspected; canary seed 4199 was excluded from
inference.

Five representations were crossed with $K=1,2,4,8$: the task-matched
dendritic tree, an explicitly context-gated point model, a flat-compartment
model, grouped point subunits, and a degree- and depth-matched tree with a
seed-fixed leaf permutation. The first four implementations deliberately
received identical routed coefficient fields and served as computationally
equivalent controls. Every representation had 64 trainable forward weights,
64 active input contacts, one output nonlinearity and one scalar decoder.
Route fields were normalized to unit Euclidean norm for each context.

The six route families were correct contiguous ancestry subtrees, a fixed
within-neuron derangement, depth-interleaved bins, random sparse groups, an
unrestricted random rank-$K$ projector and a training-derived rank-$K$ PCA
upper bound. Shared neuronal feedback, exact compartment transport and
analytic backpropagation provided references. Random controls were keyed only
by seed, family and bandwidth, which ensured identical stochastic
realizations across implementation-equivalent representations.

The frozen canary required exact context--label balance, zero context--label
correlation, neuron-shared capture below 0.20, exact capture above
0.999999999, an exact--backprop endpoint difference below $10^{-12}$, exact
implementation equivalence below $10^{-12}$ and zero fallbacks. All gates
passed before the 2,700 confirmatory fits were run with unchanged script and
configuration hashes. Outcomes were paired by seed. Intervals used 20,000
paired bootstrap draws, and two-sided Wilcoxon tests used the 20 seed
differences. The per-seed best matched non-anatomical control was prespecified
across route derangement, depth-interleaved, random-sparse and random-rank
conditions. The learned PCA route remained explicitly labelled as an upper
bound.

The trained partition-residual reanalysis deterministically reconstructed all
2,700 fits and evaluated the shared-coefficient partition residual both at
initialization and at the trained checkpoint. Supplementary Section~S5
specifies its construction and statistics, and Supplementary Fig.~S23 shows
the results.

\subsection*{Credit-operator phase experiment and frozen-factorial reanalysis}

The phase experiment used 16 Euclidean coordinates represented in the
orthonormal Haar basis of a balanced binary tree, ordered from the global mode
(level 0) to individual sibling contrasts (level 4). The configuration,
50 confirmatory seeds 7000--7049, two artifact-only canary seeds and numerical
tolerances were frozen before confirmatory outcomes were inspected. The canary
required basis orthogonality, projector idempotence, static-gain span
invariance and exact agreement with the explicit point gate. Confirmatory
execution required unchanged SHA-256 hashes for the configuration, runner and
numerical reference. Every expected row was present: 3,750 spectral, 1,800
depth, 3,750 projection and 1,500 reliability rows.

In the spectral screen, task covariance assigned total energy
$(0.15,0.35,0.30,0.20,0)$ to tree levels 0--4. An alignment coefficient
$\rho\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}$ mixed this covariance with an independently
rotated covariance having the same eigenvalues. Ancestry, random rank-matched
and dense principal-eigenspace projectors were compared at
$K\in\{1,2,4,8,16\}$. The dense value was also calculated as the Ky--Fan sum
of the leading $K$ eigenvalues divided by total covariance energy. Because the
endpoint covariances had equal trace, ancestry-minus-random capture varied
affinely with alignment. Endpoint differences therefore gave the exact
per-seed crossover; seeds already favoring ancestry at zero alignment were
assigned threshold zero. Capture was $\tr(P\Sigma_g)/\tr(\Sigma_g)$.

The same-span diagnostic compared raw nested indicators, their tree-Haar basis
and indicators multiplied by fixed nonzero lognormal column gains. Rank,
coherence, positive-spectrum Gram condition number and capture were calculated
directly.

In the depth screen, a unit-norm target was sampled from modes at levels
$0$ through $H$, with $H\in\{1,2,3,4\}$. The gradient-noise standard deviation
at level $\ell$ was $0.65[1+1.5\max(\ell-H,0)]$. Starting from zero, 80
common-noise updates of size 0.12 used the projector onto levels $0$ through
$D_{\mathrm r}\in\{1,2,3,4\}$. A fixed leaf permutation provided the rewired
control, and the identity provided stochastic backpropagation.

The projection screen independently set retained signal $r$ and retained noise
$n$ to $\{0.25,0.5,0.75,0.9,1\}$. It compared an unprojected stochastic
gradient, backpropagation followed by the same projection, and a routed
estimator with an added total coefficient-noise fraction of 0.05. For a unit
step on the identity-curvature quadratic, expected losses were calculated
analytically and stochastic losses were recorded from paired draws.

In the reliability screen, eight disjoint blocks had signal and noise energies
proportional to $\exp(hx_b)$ and $\exp(-hx_b)$, respectively, with equally
spaced $x_b\in[-1,1]$ and heterogeneity
$h\in\{0,0.5,1,1.5,2\}$. We compared no attenuation, the best common gain,
$S_b/(S_b+N_b)$, seed-shuffled gains, reversed gains and a point gate receiving
the same branch factors. Intervals used 20,000 resamples of the 50 paired
seeds, and paired tests were two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests.

The secondary analysis reconstructed the frozen training data,
initialization and route matrices for all 2,700 completed subtree-factorial
fits. For each of 20 seeds, 64 independently sampled minibatches of size 64
estimated gradient-noise energy at initialization. The exact full-data gradient
provided the signal term, the largest logistic Hessian eigenvalue provided
local smoothness, and Eq.~\ref{eq:phaseutility} provided utility. Correlations
were restricted to the 540 dendritic-tree route conditions, and uncertainty
resampled whole seed blocks. This is an initialization diagnostic of an
existing experiment, not a second confirmatory training cohort.

\subsection*{Positive-rate nonlinear physical-depth experiment}

To connect the fixed-state hierarchy result to the original rate-based
network, we used the registered
\texttt{hierarchical\_\allowbreak gain\_\allowbreak load} generator and the
production population-network implementation, DendriNet. Inputs were ordered
as nonnegative excitatory and inhibitory rate streams. For binary label $y$,
the distal excitatory signal had the schematic form
\begin{equation}
E_j=(e_0+y\Delta+\epsilon_j)
\exp\!\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\sigma z_{\ell,a_\ell(j)}
-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\sigma^2\right\},
\end{equation}
where $a_\ell(j)$ indexes the fine, coarse or global latent factor containing
coordinate $j$. Three disjoint inhibitory blocks measured these factors
separately. At sensor alignment $\alpha$, their log gains were
$\sigma[\alpha z_{\ell,a_\ell(j)}+
\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}z'_{\ell,a_\ell(j)}]$. Thus, $\alpha=1$ supplied matched
branch-local nuisance sensors, whereas $\alpha=0$ preserved the complete
factor marginals but made the sensors independent. A trial-shuffled control
cyclically permuted the sensor field after class balancing, preserving its
marginal distribution while removing example-wise pairing.

At every physical stage, the production shunting equation was
\begin{equation}
V_n=\frac{E_n+C_n}
{1+E_n+I_n+G_n+\epsilon},
\end{equation}
where $C_n$ and $G_n$ denote child current and child conductance. All rate and
conductance terms were nonnegative. Reactivation was the identity, making the
divisive branch equation the only intermediate nonlinearity. D1 $[8]$, D2
$[2,3]$ and D3 $[2,1,2]$ contained one, two and three nonsomatic stages,
respectively. Each architecture retained eight nonsomatic branch units per
soma and the same 4/2/2 fine/coarse/proximal sensor inventory. Every model had
64 somata, 16 excitatory and 12 inhibitory contacts per branch, 14,336 active
synapses, 21,760 candidate slots, 66,178 trainable parameters and 2,944
persistent state scalars per sample. Reversing the fine and global feature
ranges supplied the same-resource tree-placement control.

The operating point used signal contrast $\Delta=0.80$, matched per-factor
train/test log-gain standard deviation $0.25$, child conductance 16 and
mechanism-neutral initialization. It was selected transparently after the
original severe-shift canary failed at every depth and two-seed exploratory
gain, coupling and signal ladders identified an accessible, non-ceiling
boundary (Supplementary Fig.~S15). No pilot seed entered inference.

Fresh confirmatory seeds 10200--10209 crossed aligned, zero-alignment,
trial-shuffled and reversed-placement shunting BP; aligned raw-additive BP; and
aligned or reversed-placement shunting LocalCA with shared-soma or exact-path
transport, for 270 fits. Adam ran for at most 180 epochs with
validation-based early stopping. The learning rate was 0.003 for BP and 0.0008
for LocalCA, and all comparisons were paired by seed.

The prespecified primary effect was aligned D3-minus-D1 accuracy. Topology
specificity required subtracting the corresponding zero-alignment,
shuffled-sensor or rewired-tree depth effect from the aligned depth effect. A
positive LocalCA claim separately required both a positive depth effect and a
positive aligned-minus-rewired interaction. Intervals used 50,000
seed-paired bootstrap draws, and exact two-sided sign-flip tests enumerated all
$2^{10}$ paired sign assignments.

The artifact gate required all 270 results to be finite, no LocalCA fallback
message, exact resource equality, at least nine of ten aligned BP seeds above
0.55 at every depth, and condition means below 0.98. A dedicated checkpoint
replay measured branch voltage, total conductance, input resistance,
activation derivative, path-gain dispersion and local-versus-autograd
conductance-gradient cosine on a fixed held-out batch.

\subsection*{Point--dendrite and BP--local-credit controls}

For the teaching-coordinate control, the forward pass and synaptic autograd

Architecture, alignment-interaction and credit contrasts were computed within

gate required 200 finite new fits, no fallback pathway, seeds 10200--10209 and
exact equality of trainable parameters, active contacts, candidate slots and

\subsection*{Physical-depth alignment dose response}

The interpolation extension retained the generator, shunting-BP model,
operating point, early-stopping rule, resources and paired seeds from the
physical-depth endpoint experiment. It crossed
$\alpha\in\{0.25,0.50,0.75\}$ with D1--D3 for 90 new fits and combined these
runs with the unchanged $\alpha=0$ and $1$ endpoints. The dated
contract was frozen after the endpoint results were available but before any
intermediate run was inspected. Its primary summary was the within-seed
ordinary least-squares slope of D3-minus-D1 accuracy on alignment. The
$\alpha=0.75$ minus $0.25$ change and adjacent-level changes were used to
assess linearity. Intervals used 50,000 paired-seed bootstrap draws, and exact

\subsection*{Fixed-depth task-family and alignment factorial}

To test task specificity without adding physical depth, we fixed every network
at D3 $[2,1,2]$ and crossed three data-generating families, sensor alignment
$\alpha\in\{0,0.5,1\}$, serial versus literal grouped-point composition, and
BP versus path-transport LocalCA. The nested-factor positive control retained
the calibrated distal signal and three multiplicative gain factors. If
$z_{\ell}$ is the true zero-mean Gaussian log-gain factor at level $\ell$, the
signal-bearing excitatory coordinate was proportional to
\begin{equation*}
 (b_E+y\delta)\exp\!\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}
 \sigma_{\ell}z_{\ell}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\sigma_{\ell}^{2}\right),
\end{equation*}
where $b_E$ is the excitatory baseline, $y\in\{0,1\}$ is the class label,
$\delta$ is the class-signal contrast and $\sigma_{\ell}$ is the log-gain
standard deviation at level $\ell$. All three levels used
$\sigma_{\ell}=0.25$. Subtracting half the summed variance keeps the expected
multiplicative gain equal to one.

Inhibitory sensor block $\ell$ measured
\begin{equation*}
 s_{\ell}(\alpha)=\alpha z_{\ell}
 +\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}\,\xi_{\ell},
\end{equation*}
where $\xi_{\ell}$ is an independent standard-normal nuisance factor. Here,
$\alpha\in[0,1]$ controls task--sensor alignment: $\alpha=0$ makes the observed
sensor independent of the latent gain, whereas $\alpha=1$ reveals it exactly.
Changing $\alpha$ therefore changes trial-wise task--sensor correspondence
while preserving each sensor's marginal log-gain variance. The flat-factor
control retained the factor product and level-wise marginal variances but
replaced nested cross-feature covariance with eight unrelated groups per
level. The local-ratio control instead multiplied excitation and inhibition at
each level by correlated cumulative hierarchical fields, favoring a local
divisive readout rather than serial cancellation of separate factors.

All task families used 10,000 balanced examples, 64 excitatory and 64
inhibitory features, a distal-only excitatory class signal, signal contrast
0.80, gain SD 0.25, input-noise SD 0.08, and the previously selected optimizer,
stopping and conductance settings. Serial and grouped-point models had
identical branch modules, contacts, candidate slots, trainable parameters and
input tensors; only child-to-parent composition differed. Ten fresh paired
seeds 10500--10509 produced 360 fits.

Frozen estimands were the within-seed serial-minus-grouped contrast, its
$\alpha=1$ minus $0$ interaction, the nested-family interaction minus each
mismatch-family interaction, and the corresponding LocalCA-minus-BP credit
interaction. Directional claims required a 95\% paired-seed bootstrap interval
excluding zero and at least eight of ten seed differences in the predicted
direction. The complete family scan was reported regardless of direction.
Configurations wrote only local artifacts on the
\texttt{kempner\_project\_b} filesystem, and external experiment tracking was
disabled in both configuration and runtime.

\subsection*{State-matched positive-conductance reliability experiment}

The locked mechanism experiment used eight branches, four nonnegative rate
inputs per branch, 512 training examples, 2,048 test examples and one learned
positive excitatory conductance per input. Branch voltage was
$V_b=g_b^E/(g_b^L+g_b^E)$. A nonnegative shunt $\kappa_b$ with reversal zero
was paired with the oracle current $\kappa_bV_b$, making the post-intervention
voltage exactly
$(g_b^E+\kappa_bV_b)/(g_b^L+g_b^E+\kappa_b)=V_b$. At the same time, local
eligibility was attenuated by
$(g_b^L+g_b^E)/(g_b^L+g_b^E+\kappa_b)$. This state clamp therefore separated
physical input-resistance attenuation from suppression of the forward
feature.

Initial full-data gradients defined branch signal energy $S_b$. Independent
coefficient noise was scaled so that
$N_b=S_b\exp(-2hx_b)$ for eight equally spaced $x_b\in[-1,1]$ and
$h\in\{0,0.5,1,1.5,2\}$. With the actual step $\eta=c/L$ and $c=0.5$, shunts
were fixed from the initial oracle attenuation
$\min\{1,S_b/[c(S_b+N_b)]\}$ or from the step-consistent best global,
shuffled or reversed control. The shunts were not learned. Additional controls
were clean exact backpropagation, noisy credit without a shunt, a state-matched
additive current, an explicit point gate receiving the same factors, and
aligned attenuation with clean credit.

Forty common-noise full-data updates used one half of the numerically estimated
initial smoothness limit. This correction was frozen after the preceding pilot
was found to pair that half step with the $c=1$ gain. Two new artifact-only
canary seeds preceded 50 fresh paired confirmatory seeds 8100--8149. Frozen
gates required nonnegative inputs and shunts, voltage matching and
independently computed point/additive equivalence within $10^{-12}$, and
relative finite-difference errors below $10^{-5}$ for both the unshunted
objective and the shunted frozen-clamp surrogate. The observed maxima were
$2.22\times10^{-16}$ for state mismatch,
$4.00\times10^{-15}$ and $1.31\times10^{-14}$ for the two equivalence gates,
and $3.60\times10^{-8}$ for the physical finite-difference error. Intervals
used 20,000 resamples of paired seeds, and paired tests were two-sided
Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. The configuration, runner hashes, all 2,250
seed--condition rows and prespecified contract are supplied with the Source
Data.

The adaptive extension retained the same task, model and state clamp but
replaced oracle moments with running estimates from paired, conditionally
independent noisy observations of the local branch gradient. The design was
frozen before any canary or confirmatory outcome was inspected. Fifty fresh
paired seeds completed all 1,050 planned outcomes, and an independently
computed adaptive point gate again matched to numerical precision.
Supplementary Section~S4 (``Adaptive local reliability estimation'') specifies
the estimator, gain bounds, seed protocol and complete statistics; seed-level
outcomes are provided in the Source Data.

\subsection*{Same-span noisy coefficient-learning experiment}

The rate-based coefficient experiment fixed a 16-coordinate balanced-tree
space and the rank-eight span through depth three. Three parameterizations
shared the same projector: eight orthonormal Haar columns, 15 redundant raw
nested indicators, and the same indicators multiplied by fixed positive gains
ranging from $\exp(-3)$ to $\exp(3)$. Their positive Gram condition numbers
were 1, 15 and 388.52. Each seed supplied a unit-norm target in the common span
and 80 common noisy target estimates. Observation standard deviation was
$2/\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ for
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}\in\{4,16,64,256\}$. Vanilla coefficient descent used 0.2
of each dictionary's stability limit. A separate oracle control multiplied
the coefficient gradient by the pseudoinverse Gram matrix, producing the same
projected field step in all three parameterizations.

The design and predictions were frozen before canary seeds 9098--9099 and 50
paired confirmatory seeds 9100--9149 were run. All 4,800 checkpoint rows
completed. Projectors agreed to $9.44\times10^{-16}$, the target address
residual was at most $1.45\times10^{-30}$, and Gram-preconditioned trajectories
agreed to $1.16\times10^{-15}$. The prediction of an unconditional
orthonormal-coordinate advantage failed in the low-data regime. We therefore
report both the prespecified contrast and the exact post-result bias--variance
decomposition rather than relabelling the outcome as confirmatory. Intervals
used 20,000 resamples of paired seeds, and tests were two-sided Wilcoxon
signed-rank tests.

The historical inhibitory-dose cohort used the noise-resilience task and
crossed two neuron models, five inhibitory-contact counts per branch
(0, 5, 10, 20 and 40), backpropagation or one of three local feedback fields,
and ten paired seeds, yielding 400 runs. Its prespecified endpoint was the
shunting-minus-additive contrast. Because every shunting cell used the invalid
signed-input convention, the full family remains in the run-validity ledger
but is excluded from figures and inference.

The replacement audit crossed exact transport and backpropagation over two
tasks, two cores, depths one through four and paired seeds 42--51, yielding
320 runs and 160 method pairs. Training used detached tracked-clean source
commit \texttt{74792ca} and nested synthetic-data commit
\texttt{4f3612a}. The additive core retained signed inputs on the noise task,

Acceptance required resolved configurations and seeds, complete learning

The fixed-topology cohort contained 320 depth-four runs crossing two tasks,

\subsection*{MICrONS morphology processing}

Cells were drawn from the functionally co-registered MICrONS cohort and
resolved from stable nucleus identifiers to roots in materialization 1822 of
\texttt{minnie65\_public}. Skeletons were obtained through the MICrONS skeleton
service, and incoming synapses came from \texttt{synapses\_pni\_2}. The
original eight cells formed a convenience pilot selected under an earlier
analysis contract rather than a random or representative sample. Anatomical
claims from this cohort are therefore explicitly single-animal and
descriptive.

Degree-two dendritic chains were collapsed into branch-to-branch segments.
Synapses were assigned to the nearest dendritic cable node and retained when
the distance was at most $5\,\mu$m. Presynaptic coarse type provided E/I
identity where available. Otherwise, a high-confidence target-structure
prediction supplied a secondary proxy from the in-development
\texttt{synapse\_target\_predictions\_ssa\_v2} table. This table does not yet
have a stable public release identifier and is not required for the
direct-type-only or disjoint-v661 analyses. The direct-type-only analysis
removed all proxy-classified contacts.

Raw axial coupling was proportional to radius squared divided by cable length.
It was normalized by the nonzero median within each cell and clipped to
0.05--20. Raw leak was proportional to radius multiplied by the larger of cable
length and radius. It was likewise normalized by the nonzero cell median and
clipped to 0.1--10. Excitatory and inhibitory synaptic areas were normalized
by the median nonzero total synaptic area within each cell. These quantities
serve as sensitivity weights rather than fitted physical conductances. The
soma was the root of every compressed tree. Ancestry matrices were constructed
by explicit parent traversal and checked for acyclicity and a unique path to
the soma.

For the disjoint sensitivity cohort, a 55-cell eligibility universe was frozen
before outcomes were inspected. All candidates were mapped to the public
MICrONS v661 static release, and the original eight cells were excluded by
stable nucleus identifier. The remaining 47 cells all passed the structural
criteria. Only direct presynaptic E/I annotations were used. Before spatial
mapping, direct labels were available for 11,024 of 234,301 incoming synapses
(4.71\%); 10,516 contacts mapped to retained cables, with a median of 212
directly typed synapses per cell. The cohort was not a population-random
sample and contained 34 L2 IT, 2 L3 IT, 10 L4 IT and 1 L5 ET cell. The same
structural-capacity procedure was applied to both cohorts, but their results
were not pooled.

For the independent-animal replication, we used the public MICrONS phase-1
layer-2/3 ``Pinky'' v185 release, a
$250\times140\times90\,\mu$m visual-cortex volume from a P36 male mouse that
is biologically distinct from \texttt{minnie65}
\citep{turner2022neocortex,microns_pinky_v185}. Before mesh topology or
selected-cell synapse counts were opened, the 362 rows labelled excitatory in
the archived soma-valence table were ordered along the second volume
coordinate and divided into 12 equal-count strata. Within each stratum, we
selected the cell nearest the global median of the first and third coordinates,
using root ID to break ties. This outcome-independent rule spans the volume
while reducing edge truncation; it is not a random population sample.

For each selected HDF5 mesh, we added the published
\texttt{link\_edges} and retained the largest connected component. This step
removed detached segmentation fragments before the soma root was selected.
The retained mesh was skeletonized with the meshparty TEASAR implementation
using a $7.5\,\mu$m soma-collapse radius and a $12\,\mu$m invalidation
distance. Cable radius was estimated as half the opposite-surface ray distance
at skeleton mesh anchors using an Embree ray index.

Incoming synapses came from the archived dense v185 table. E/I identity was
assigned only when the presynaptic root had an excitatory or inhibitory label
in the same soma-valence table. We then applied the same $5\,\mu$m mapping
gate, branch-chain compression, electrical normalization and route
dictionaries used in the v661 analysis, without retuning. The frozen QC gate
required at least 20 mapped typed inputs, including at least three inhibitory
inputs. All selected cells and exclusion reasons remain in the manifest. The
primary endpoint was sign agreement with \texttt{minnie65} for K=4
ancestry-route capture relative to random paths, depth bins and shuffled
ancestry. The mouse, not the 12 nested cells, is the biological replication
unit; cell-bootstrap intervals describe only within-volume stability.

\subsection*{MICrONS inhibitory-census analysis}

Before evaluating any endpoint, the inhibitory analysis joined the 47-cell
v661 cohort to the published v795 census using stable nucleus identifiers. All
20 overlapping targets were retained. We used the published cell-type table,
inhibitory-synapse table, complete input-synapse table and target SWCs
\citep{schneidermizell2025inhibitory}. Raw all-input coordinates were mapped
to the published slanted micrometre frame with an affine transformation fitted
from 80,114 synapse locations present in both coordinate systems. The maximum
transformation residual was $1.64\times10^{-12}\,\mu$m. Input and known
inhibitory locations were mapped to the nearest target skeleton node and
retained within $5\,\mu$m. Inputs without a known presynaptic inhibitory cell
are described as generic rather than excitatory because the census does not
label every incoming contact.

For each multisynaptic inhibitory connection, the largest contact was retained
within every published axonal clump. Each retained contact was matched to 64
generic input segments in the same compartment with the nearest log path
distance to the soma. Five hundred independent matched assignments estimated
the null distribution within each connection. Pairwise outcomes were
cable-tree distance, normalized shared path to the lowest common ancestor, and
cosine overlap between the two input-size-weighted binary descendant domains.
Outcomes were first averaged over inhibitory connections within each
postsynaptic target. The target, rather than an individual contact or
connection, was the inferential unit.

Two sensitivity analyses addressed dependence and spatial matching. First,
connection-level contrasts were averaged within each of 92 presynaptic axons
before inference. Second, candidate locations were ranked jointly by log path
distance and three-dimensional Euclidean proximity to the observed contact.
The ranking used the sum of the two within-pool percentile ranks, after which
the same 500 matched assignments were repeated. These analyses test whether a
target-level contrast persists after clustering repeated connections from the
same axon and after incorporating local tissue proximity into the null.

For the route-capacity analysis, rows represented segments with mapped input
and columns represented segments bearing at least one known inhibitory
contact. The binary ancestry matrix was weighted by the square root of mapped
input size across rows and known inhibitory size across columns. Actual sites
were ranked by column norm. At each budget, selected columns were used to
reconstruct the full weighted actual-route matrix. Controls comprised 200
random subsets of actual sites, depth bins, depth-preserving within-column
ancestry shuffles and an unconstrained SVD oracle.

Leverage selection is a capacity diagnostic rather than a biological learning
rule. Because both the selected columns and the reconstruction target come
from the same actual-route matrix, this endpoint measures internal dictionary
compressibility, not generalization to an independently generated credit
field.

\subsection*{Credit dictionaries and capture}

The weighted direct route matrix was $\Gamma_{ik}=A_{ik}\beta_k$. It is a
normalized route-sensitivity proxy rather than the exact derivative of the
reciprocal passive-cable solve. Perturbation fields were generated by
multiplying independent route amplitudes by $\Gamma$. At each channel budget,
ancestry routes were ranked by $\beta_k$ multiplied by the fraction of total
mapped excitatory synaptic area among their descendants. Controls were an
unconstrained principal-component basis, random nonempty real paths, cable-depth
bins and column-wise ancestry shuffles. Channel count and the number of
evaluated examples were matched. Feedback wiring density was defined as the
fraction of nonzero entries in the site-by-channel dictionary.

For each dictionary, weighted least squares provided the optimal projection
after both the field and dictionary were mapped into the synaptic-area-weighted
coordinates of Eq.~\ref{eq:capture}. Numerical sensitivity was assessed with
twenty independent Monte Carlo streams, each containing 384 perturbation fields
and 64 random or shuffled dictionaries per cell and channel count. Streams
were not treated as biological replicates. Primary comparisons were averaged
within each cell before paired testing.

For the independent-generator control, the reconstruction target was not
sampled from $\Gamma$. We added a small focal shunt at every
inhibitory-bearing segment of the reciprocal passive-cable system, restored
baseline somatic voltage with an independently solved state-matching
compensatory current, and finite-differenced the log magnitude of the exact
excitatory-conductance gradient at every excitatory-bearing segment. The
derivative dose was $10^{-3}$ times the focal segment's
membrane-plus-synaptic conductance. This procedure produced a weighted response
operator $H$ whose columns were exact focal responses.

For a dictionary $\Phi$, expected field-energy capture was calculated without
Monte Carlo sampling as
\begin{equation}
\frac{\lVert P_{W^{1/2}\Phi}W^{1/2}H\rVert_F^2}
{\lVert W^{1/2}H\rVert_F^2},
\end{equation}
where $W$ contains mapped excitatory synaptic area and $P$ denotes the
orthogonal projector onto the stated column space. In addition to the primary
controls, 200 surrogate trees per cell reassigned child subtrees among parent
slots at the preceding depth. This preserved all node and edge attributes,
each node's topological depth, and each parent's out-degree while changing
ancestral membership. A separate row shuffle preserved selected route values
and sparsity but broke their assignment to excitatory segments.

The coarse-to-fine analysis of the non-scalar ancestry-route field
(Supplementary Fig.~S25) used a deterministic weighted tree-Haar construction
on each unbalanced reconstructed arbor. Supplementary Section~S7 defines the
construction, column-permuted ancestry control and cohort statistics.

\subsection*{Focal conductance perturbations}

Before the focal analysis, we fixed the site-selection rule, primary dose,
comparison groups and localization endpoint. A depth-stratified rule selected
sites with at least three excitatory descendants and three comparison sites,
with at most 16 sites per cell sampled across topological-depth quartiles.
Primary excitatory and inhibitory synaptic scales were both 0.35, with
normalized reversal potentials $E_E=1$ and $E_I=-0.2$. Conductance doses were
0.25, 0.5, 1 and 2 times the local membrane-plus-synaptic conductance. The
additive control injected the first-order current
$\kappa_k(E_I-V_k)$ at the same node without changing $G$. After either
intervention, an independently solved compensatory current restored baseline
somatic voltage. This intervention is not the derivative of a continuously
adjusted voltage-clamp controller.

The objective was squared somatic error, with target voltage set one normalized
unit below baseline. Excitatory-conductance gradients were obtained by an
adjoint solve, with the solved compensatory current treated as fixed during
the post-intervention derivative, and were checked by finite differences. Let
$d_i=E_E-V_i$ and $d'_i=E_E-V'_i$. The post-shunt gradient $q'_id'_i$ was
decomposed algebraically into an adjoint-only substitution $q'_id_i$ and a
driving-force-only substitution $q_id'_i$, alongside the full-shunt and
matched-additive gradients.

Two-factor Shapley values allocated the nonlinear
full-shunt-minus-additive localization payoff between adjoint and driving-force
changes and reconciled exactly to the observed contrast at each site. Because
the matched-additive reference is not a literal no-factor physical
intervention, we also calculated an unperturbed-reference allocation as a
sensitivity analysis. These substitutions are algebraic controls rather than
independent biological interventions.

Synapses were classified as descendants, ancestors, sister-subtree sites or
unrelated sites. Within each template, unrelated synapses were matched to
descendant cable depth. For baseline gradient $g_i$ and perturbed gradient
$g_i'$, we defined
$m_i=\left|\log[(|g_i'|+\varepsilon)/(|g_i|+\varepsilon)]\right|$, where
$\varepsilon=10^{-15}\max(1,\max_i|g_i|)$. The frozen site-level localization
index was the median $m_i$ among descendants minus the median among
depth-matched unrelated synapses. Site effects were averaged within cells, and
the primary paired contrast used the eight cell means. A topology control
sampled 200 relation templates from other focal sites, stratified by focal-site
topological-depth quartile when another site was available; otherwise, it used
another site in the same cell. Supplementary Section~S6 specifies axial and
leak normalization and clipping.

We also calibrated the passive cable in physical units. Nonroot segments were
modeled as cylinders with axial conductance $\pi r^2/(R_a\ell)$ and leak
conductance $2\pi r\ell/R_m$, while the root used spherical membrane area
$4\pi r^2$. Conductances were expressed in nS. Because synaptic areas are not
conductance measurements, excitatory and inhibitory synaptic conductances
retained their prespecified relative scale and were referenced to the
cell-median physical leak conductance.

We evaluated axial resistivities
\[
R_a\in\{100,150,250\}\,\Omega\,\mathrm{cm}
\]
at $R_m=15{,}000\,\Omega\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. In a separate sweep with
$R_a=150\,\Omega\,\mathrm{cm}$, membrane resistivity took the values
\[
R_m\in\{300,1000,3000,5000,15{,}000,30{,}000\}\,
\Omega\,\mathrm{cm}^2.
\]
The same sites, state-matching current, adjoint
gradient, unit relative dose and additive comparison were retained. Two
zero-length nonroot segments in the v661 source skeletons used their radius as
the cable length. This fallback and all electrotonic ratios are recorded in
the Source Data.

The frozen passive focal-sensitivity matrix used the original eight cells at
$R_a=150\,\Omega\,\mathrm{cm}$. We crossed membrane resistivities of 300,
1000 and $15{,}000\,\Omega\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ with distributed leak-like
background conductance equal to 0, 1 or 4 times each segment's baseline leak.
At every selected site, three dose schemes were evaluated: 0.25, 1 and 2 times
local membrane-plus-synaptic conductance; fixed doses of 0.05, 0.5 and 5 nS;
and 0.25, 1 and 4 times local input conductance
$[(G^{-1})_{kk}]^{-1}$. Rank-one voltage and adjoint updates were calculated
exactly with Sherman--Morrison, followed by the same state-matching somatic
current.

In addition to absolute localization, frozen outputs included signed
log-magnitude change, attenuated and enhanced fractions, sign-flip fraction,
descendant gradient-energy ratio, local input resistance and $S_k$ from
Eq.~\ref{eq:selectivitycriterion}. Site values were averaged within cells
before eight-cell intervals and shunt-minus-additive contrasts were computed.
A numerical canary required positive-definite matrices and a somatic residual
below $10^{-10}$ before the full matrix was run with unchanged code and
configuration.

For the active steady-state sensitivity, voltage-dependent Na, K, Ca, HCN and
NMDA currents were added to the physical passive system at
$R_a=150\,\Omega\,$cm, $R_m=1{,}000\,\Omega\,$cm$^2$ and one
baseline-leak unit of distributed background conductance. Channel activation
used sigmoidal steady-state gates with channel-specific half-activation,
slope, power and reversal values fixed in the machine-readable protocol.
Global and segment-level maximal-conductance multipliers were independent
lognormal draws. Newton iterations with a residual-reducing line search solved
the full nonlinear equilibrium.

Draws were accepted only when voltage remained within the prespecified range,
the residual was below $10^{-10}$ and the symmetrized local Jacobian was
positive definite above $10^{-8}$. With purely local voltage-dependent gates,
off-diagonal Jacobian entries are the reciprocal axial couplings. The
steady-state Jacobian is therefore symmetric, so the symmetrized gate and the
plain inverse used below are exact rather than approximate.

For every accepted equilibrium, the adjoint used the exact inverse of the
local Jacobian. Focal rank-one shunts and first-order-current-matched additive
inputs were evaluated at 0.25, 1 and 4 times local input conductance, followed
by the same state-matching compensatory current at the soma. We required 64
accepted draws in every cell, allowing up to 256 attempts; all 512 first
attempts were accepted. The 64 channel draws and up to 16 focal sites were
averaged within each reconstructed cell before bootstrap intervals and paired
tests. The active ensemble is a steady-state local-linearization sensitivity
analysis, not a simulation of channel kinetics or spikes.

The focal analysis was repeated after all proxy-classified contacts were
removed. In the disjoint v661 sensitivity cohort, 45 of 47 cells provided 235
eligible focal sites for the primary shunt--additive comparison. Forty cells
with at least two eligible sites provided the reassigned-relation control.
Cells, not focal sites, remained the inferential units.

\subsection*{Measured-response analyses}

Functionally imaged presynaptic partners were linked to anatomical contacts
through the MICrONS/CAVE and DANDI mappings. The original frozen one-scan
cohort contained 69 partners across seven targets. The scan-complete analysis
deterministically retained every automatic-conservative
target--session--scan cohort with at least five unique connected imaged
presynaptic roots, yielding 13 scan observations nested within the same seven
targets. Each scan contained 464 trials, 280 stimulus identities and 136
repeated identities.

Multiple contacts from one presynaptic partner were pooled, and its analysis
site was the segment with the greatest summed contact area. Functional
similarity was the Pearson correlation of condition-mean responses over
repeated identities. For each target, the partial-rank analysis rank
transformed functional similarity and shared ancestry, residualized each
against log Euclidean separation and path-depth difference, and correlated the
two residuals. Scan-level statistics were averaged within targets before the
target bootstrap and signed-rank test. The target neuron, not an individual
scan or synaptic pair, was the inferential unit.

For task-derived credit, a small branch model predicted each postsynaptic
response from the responses of its connected presynaptic partners. Complete
visual-stimulus identities were assigned to held-out splits. Exact branch
errors were calculated on training trials, and dictionary projectors were fit
without using held-out identities. Matched models were then trained from the
same initialization with exact or projected branch errors. Ten fixed
split/initialization replicates were averaged within each of seven target
cells.

Because the projection coefficients used exact errors, these analyses estimate
the capacity of a feedback family rather than a biological encoder. Only
input-weight errors were projected; branch-readout weights and bias received
exact gradients. Normalized MSE was held-out MSE divided by the held-out error
of the training-mean predictor. Supplementary Section~S9 provides complete
model and optimizer specifications.

For the segment-resolved analysis, each complete compressed reconstruction
was instantiated as a reciprocal passive conductance system. A trainable
nonnegative excitatory conductance was placed at the dominant mapped contact
of every imaged partner. Exact gradients of somatic squared prediction error
used the trial-specific inverse conductance operator and factorized into
$x_i(E_E-V_i)$ multiplied by the segment adjoint.

Restricted methods first projected the baseline soma-to-site transfer vector
into one of three four-channel dictionaries: conductance-weighted ancestry, a
column-value-preserving site shuffle, or an equal-rank random set of nonempty
anatomical routes. This vector was calibrated once without target responses
and then frozen during learning. Each restricted update used only scalar
output error, the fixed site vector and local eligibility. All methods used
exact readout and bias gradients.

Ten seed-fixed splits held out complete stimulus identities within every scan.
Replicates were averaged within scans, and all eligible scans were then
averaged within targets before seven-cell inference. Finite differences
audited one exact conductance coordinate per run, and input and runner hashes
were archived. Any absent route, rank mismatch, nonfinite value or incomplete
scan would have caused exclusion; none occurred.

The capture--learning association excluded exact and dense oracles. Both
variables were centered within targets before Spearman's $\rho$ was computed.
Its interval used 20,000 target-level bootstrap draws. The two-sided null used
20,000 independent reassignments of learning outcomes among method labels
within each target.

\subsection*{Alignment-controlled optimization}

For each reconstructed cell, we rebuilt the conductance-weighted ancestry
kernel and, before generating gradient fields, selected eight routes according
to inhibitory fraction multiplied by descendant excitatory synaptic-area
fraction. Segment coordinates were weighted by the square root of summed
mapped excitatory synaptic area. If $Q$ is an orthonormal basis for the
selected routes, each unit-energy exact gradient was constructed as
\begin{equation}
\bm g(a)=\sqrt{a}\,\bm u_{\parallel}+\sqrt{1-a}\,\bm u_{\perp},
\end{equation}
where $\bm u_{\parallel}\in\operatorname{span}(Q)$,
$\bm u_{\perp}\perp\operatorname{span}(Q)$ and
$a\in\{0,0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8,1\}$. Directions were paired across alignment
levels. Controls were eight random real paths, eight cable-depth bins and
row-permuted ancestry routes. The permutation preserved column values and
nonzero count while breaking ancestry.

Each cell contributed 40 nested Monte Carlo streams with 256 gradients per
stream. The objective was a positive diagonal quadratic with an
anatomy-independent curvature spectrum from 0.5 to 1.5, permuted within each
stream and held fixed across methods and alignment levels. One-step updates
had a norm equal to 0.1 times the exact-gradient norm. Iterative projected
descent used learning rate 0.25 for 20 steps. Both outcomes were normalized by
the corresponding full-gradient progress. Streams and gradient fields assessed
numerical sensitivity, while cell-level means served as the inferential
units.

\subsection*{External animal-data reanalysis}

Source data from Francioni et al. were obtained from the public Nature record
associated with DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10190-7. The archived workbook was
verified by SHA-256 before analysis. Extended Data sheet 13E provided one P+
and one P- contrast per animal, defined in the source study as the change in
soma--dendrite residual between error-reduction and error-increase periods. We
retained the source signs. Signed separation was calculated as P+ minus P- and
checked against the value reported in the workbook.

The common basis was $(1,1)/\sqrt{2}$ and the signed basis was
$(1,-1)/\sqrt{2}$; squared projection norms were divided by total
two-coordinate energy. Sheet 5E supplied neuron-level distributions for
visualization only. Directional animal-level hypotheses followed the known
causal mapping. We report exact random-sign and Wilcoxon tests together with
animal-bootstrap intervals based on 50,000 draws.

\subsection*{Static task-interference calculation}

The task-interference identity follows from an exact quadratic expansion around
a Task-A optimum, where the linear term is zero. In the equal-route
illustration, each task occupied 1,000 coordinates, route overlap ranged from
zero to one, and the Task-B target opposed Task A on shared coordinates.
Off-route leakage supplied an update of relative magnitude $\lambda$ on
Task-A-only coordinates. We evaluated a $101\times101$ grid over overlap and
leakage at step size 0.2 and compared explicit vector updates with the closed
form at every grid point. This analysis contains no temporal or spiking neural
dynamics. The published motor-learning study is used only for qualitative
cross-study interpretation.

\subsection*{Alignment-by-bandwidth synthesis plane}

The synthesis panel in Fig.~\ref{fig:creditphase}g is a deterministic
secondary display assembled from frozen outputs; it does not rerun any
experiment. Its horizontal coordinate is task--anatomy alignment, defined as
the fraction of task-gradient or field energy in the anatomical route span.
Its vertical coordinate is relative feedback bandwidth
$K/r_{\mathrm{eff}}$ on a logarithmic scale, where effective task-credit rank
is
$r_{\mathrm{eff}}=(\sum_i\lambda_i)^2/\sum_i\lambda_i^2$ for the relevant
credit spectrum. Both coordinates are estimated separately for each
experimental family.

For the 2,700-fit factorial, alignment is the mean initial exact-gradient
capture of the correct-ancestry span in the dendritic-tree implementation, and
$r_{\mathrm{eff}}=7.99$ comes from the frozen task spectra. For the spectral
sweep, alignment is the imposed covariance-mixture coefficient $\rho$, and
$r_{\mathrm{eff}}(\rho)$ is estimated within seeds from dense-PCA cumulative
capture. For the alignment-controlled MICrONS experiment, alignment is the
imposed aligned-energy fraction $a$. Its bandwidth coordinate is derived
analytically from the documented field generator as the geometric mean across
cells of $a^2+8(1-a)^2/(n-8)$, where $n$ is the cell's weighted coordinate
count; it is not read from a results table. For the coarse measured-response
null, alignment is mean held-out field capture by ancestry routes, and
$r_{\mathrm{eff}}=1.07$ is estimated from dense-oracle capture at one through
eight channels. For the two-stream credit-reversal task, alignment is the
initial scaled capture of correct two-subtree routing, and
$r_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx2.0$ follows from the algebraic rank-two expression
$1/[c^2+(1-c)^2]$, with $c=0.510$ denoting shared-coordinate capture.

The full-tree measured-response null is not plotted because its task rank was
not estimated comparably. Marker fill records each family's frozen win, tie or
loss for the ancestry-versus-control contrast, and region tints are derived
from theory rather than fit to the results. Every coordinate, definition and
source is listed in
\texttt{source\_data/credit\_phase\_plane/points.csv}.

\subsection*{Interior-optimum and wiring-normalized reanalyses}

Two deterministic secondary analyses of frozen outputs support
Supplementary Fig.~S16 and Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}e,f. Neither analysis alters
or reruns a training outcome. For the interior-optimum analysis, per-seed
initialization utility from Eq.~\ref{eq:phaseutility} was taken from the
archived operator metrics of the 2,700-fit factorial and from the frozen
depth-phase tables. The predicted optimum was the maximum utility over routed
depth, or the maximum ancestry-minus-best-control utility contrast over
budget. The observed optimum was the minimum final population loss, or the
maximum corresponding held-out-accuracy contrast. Before reporting any new
quantity, the pipeline reproduced the published $K=4$
ancestry-minus-best-control accuracy contrast
(0.0127; 15 of 20 seeds) from the frozen seed outcomes.

For the wiring-normalized analysis, capture per unit wiring was defined as
weighted field-energy capture divided by feedback wiring density. It was
calculated within cells from frozen routing-capacity curves after Monte Carlo
streams were averaged within each cell. The published eight-channel capture
and wiring anchors were reproduced before any ratio was formed. Intervals are
95\% seed- or cell-bootstrap intervals based on 20,000 resamples. The
laminar-capture proposition and marginal utility condition
(Supplementary Section~S4) are verified numerically in the released test
suite.

\subsection*{Statistics and reproducibility}

Unless a directional hypothesis had been frozen in advance, all tests were
two-sided. Central comparisons are reported with effect sizes and 95\%
intervals. Reconstructed-cell intervals resampled cells first and, when
appropriate, one Monte Carlo stream within each cell. Textual
focal-attribution intervals used a hierarchical bootstrap over cells and
sites. By contrast, the dose-response display in
Fig.~\ref{fig:focal}c shows cell-bootstrap intervals after site values were
averaged within each cell. Measured-response intervals resampled target cells,
whereas artificial-network means and standard deviations used independent
initialization seeds. Exact Wilcoxon signed-rank tests were used for small
paired cell cohorts. No site, trial, epoch, or Monte Carlo stream was treated
as an independent biological replicate. Null results are described as a lack
of reliable evidence rather than as proof of equality.

Bootstrap intervals used 20,000 draws unless stated otherwise in the Methods.
The Fashion-MNIST ladder, physical-depth, H4, task-family, and external-animal
analyses used 50,000 paired draws. Benjamini--Hochberg correction was applied
within each frozen primary family that prespecified it: the four-budget subtree
scan, the six bandwidth-matched routing contrasts, and the frozen H4 primary
family. Other parameter sweeps and sensitivity analyses are labeled secondary
and are not treated as independent confirmation.

Seed counts reflected the evidential role of each cohort rather than a
single pooled power calculation. Three to five seeds were reserved for
numerical or mechanism diagnostics, ten for fresh paired confirmatory
replications with interval and sign-agreement gates, 15--20 for inherited
trained factorials, and 50 for phase-boundary and crossover distributions.
Because effect sizes were unknown before the new simulation families were
run, we did not use a prospective parametric power calculation. Positive
confirmatory claims required both a paired 95\% interval excluding zero and
the prespecified fraction of concordant seed signs. Diagnostic cohorts were
not promoted to inferential evidence. A machine-readable table in Source Data
maps every headline endpoint to its independent unit and identifies nested or
descriptive observations.

The analysis code records configurations, seeds, software versions, source
tables, and machine-readable summaries. Numerical source data are organized by
figure and panel in the accompanying package. The test suite covers tree
construction, projection identities, and exact-gradient checks.
