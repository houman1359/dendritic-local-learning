# Conditional sensitivity of the seven-target ancestry-response test

This is an explicitly model-based, exploratory sensitivity analysis requested after the original null result and after the referee report. Its input choices and analysis rules are frozen before simulated detection outcomes. It does not alter the original measured correlations or establish sensitivity to unobserved partners.

## Fixed observed inputs

Use exactly the 13 automatic-conservative scans and seven targets in the published all-scan analysis. Preserve all 125 partner observations, 102 unique presynaptic roots, the 20 target/partner combinations repeated across scans, the two roots shared across targets, and exact imaging-recording identities. Each scan has 136 repeated conditions: 130 presented twice and six presented ten times. Use the actual condition hashes to match stimuli across scans, actual dominant-contact ancestry/geometric covariates, and actual per-recording odd/even split-half Spearman reliability. No pairs, scans or targets are excluded by observed effect or reliability. The two negative reliability estimates become zero reliable variance in this conditional Gaussian model; raw estimates remain archived.

The missing pair-level tables, partner contacts and minimal trial responses are copied from the existing public credit-routing results into this Source Data directory, with original file hashes and extraction definitions. Response averages are supplied fluorescence interval averages; this analysis does not label them raw fluorescence, deconvolve them, or change their original preprocessing.

## Valid joint response model

For each observed arbor, form a Brownian shared-path kernel between unique mapped partners: shared soma-to-LCA path length divided by the geometric mean of their soma-to-contact path lengths. This is a positive-semidefinite correlation kernel. A contact at zero soma-to-contact depth contributes an independent diagonal component, because no shared path length can be normalized there; no arbitrary stem length is introduced.

Embed the seven kernels in the common 102-root coordinate system, sum them, and normalize by the square roots of their diagonal membership counts. This remains positive semidefinite and gives each recorded presynaptic root one consistent latent response even when it appears in several scans or two target arbors. At each repeated stimulus, draw independent Gaussian unit-specific tuning U and joint ancestry-kernel tuning A. At signal fraction lambda, latent tuning is sqrt(1-lambda) U + sqrt(lambda) A. Values of lambda are 0, 0.05, ..., 1. Lambda is the fraction of reliable tuning variance assigned to the specified ancestry kernel; it is not a universal biological partial correlation or a fitted property of the recorded cells.

Latent tuning for the same presynaptic root and condition hash is shared across recordings. Measurement noise is shared only for an identical optical recording and condition. All pairwise similarities are Pearson correlations computed from these joint response vectors. Pairs are never sampled independently. The existing shared-ancestry fraction, ranked Euclidean separation and ranked contact-depth difference are kept fixed. The original partial-rank statistic is computed in every scan, averaged across scans within target, then tested across seven targets.

## Measured reliability attenuation

For an observed split-half Spearman reliability r_s, set r_p = 2 sin(pi max(0,r_s)/6), clipped to [0,1]. This is the Gaussian-copula conversion to a Pearson split-half correlation. Let h be the mean inverse half-repeat count over the 136 repeated conditions; here halves have one or five repeats. A trial response is sqrt(r_p) times latent tuning plus noise with variance (1-r_p)/h. For condition means, divide noise variance by the actual number of repeats. This exactly matches the pooled second-moment split-half correlation under the Gaussian moment approximation and respects the different repeat counts. The mixture of repeat counts means its expected Spearman reliability is approximate, not algebraically identical; a separately seeded 1,000-replicate calibration audit will report discrepancies without retuning parameters.

The primary scenario uses these measured reliabilities. A prespecified perfect-reliability scenario removes measurement noise while keeping the exact same observed partners, stimuli, kernel and inference. It is a comparison for this sampling/model, not a universal upper bound for any possible biological effect. Uncertainty in the estimated reliabilities and arbitrary correlated biological noise are not eliminated by this calculation.

## Replication, exact inference and detection rule

Use 4,000 independent global simulated datasets, divided into 20 fixed chunks of 200. Seed each chunk with 2026090700 plus the chunk index. Reuse its Gaussian draws across lambda values and reliability scenarios to make the comparisons paired. The calibration-audit seed is 2026090799.

For each dataset, perform the exact two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the seven target effects by enumerating all 128 sign assignments, with zero effects omitted and average ranks for ties. Detection of positive alignment requires P <= 0.05 and a positive mean target effect. For seven nonzero untied values, the minimum two-sided P is 2/128 = 0.015625; six of the 128 sign assignments reject at 0.05 under the exact sign-exchangeable null. There is no universal power ceiling below one caused solely by n=7. Report the attainable P lattice and the observed model-specific detection probability at lambda=1, with its Monte Carlo interval. Do not describe a normal approximation or an independent-pair test as the seven-unit test.

Report detection probability at every lambda with a Wilson 95% Monte Carlo interval, plus the mean simulated seven-target partial-rank effect. Report the smallest tested lambda at which detection is at least 80% and stays at least 80% for all larger tested values; also report its bracketing grid interval. A linear interpolation within that bracket is a descriptive estimate only, and its corresponding mean partial-rank effect is conditional on this model and sampling pattern. If 80% is not reached, report it as unreachable within the tested signal family, not as an invented detectable correlation. Also show whether the lower Monte Carlo confidence bound reaches 80%.

## Model checks and limitations

Audit zero-signal type I error and its Wilson interval rather than assuming exact nominal calibration under all shared-root dependencies. Report a warning if the lower interval exceeds 0.05; do not silently adjust the test or choose a different simulation family after seeing outcomes. Retain all target and scan statistics, exact P values, seeds and code/input hashes. Quantify shared-recording/root overlap and correlations among target statistics at zero signal; verify positive-semidefinite response covariance and exact replication of the observed original partial-rank statistics from the archived inputs.

The simulation conditions on the 13 observed scans and the seven sampled targets. It assumes Gaussian stimulus tuning, independent stimulus draws, a specified Brownian ancestry component, and measurement noise calibrated through the stated reliability approximation. It preserves known shared-unit dependence but cannot account for unknown between-target biological covariance. It does not simulate missing partners as if they had been measured, supply a universal power calculation for the full arbors, establish absence of ancestry alignment, or infer an endogenous learning signal. A successful simulated detection threshold is sensitivity to this explicit injected signal, not validation of the biological null.
