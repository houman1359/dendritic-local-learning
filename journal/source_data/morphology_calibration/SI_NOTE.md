# Concise Methods and results for supplementary integration

We generated 80 new unit-variance multilinear targets in 20 paired seed blocks, crossing random signed/amplitude matching, quartet, nested-prefix and uniformly sampled random-interaction functions. A Lasso over all 255 nonconstant Walsh monomials received only finite noisy input/output observations; it received no family label or planted support. Five separate development seeds selected its regularization scale and fixed/rank-only baselines. At the primary condition, 192 of 256 calibration observations fitted the estimator, while 64 independent observations evaluated a two-sweep pilot. Both used the same total query budget. The noise SD was 0.5. Candidate scores were the maximum estimated centered-cut tail, with summed tails and lexicographic names used only for declared ties. An estimated score is not a certified bound on the true noisy task.

All choices were sealed before confirmatory training/test outcomes. Each candidate then received an independent 1,024-row noisy training sample (SD 0.3), 32 alternating least-squares sweeps and two random restarts. A separate 512-row validation sample selected the final restart; 4,096 independent inputs evaluated clean-target NMSE. Repeated input patterns across finite-domain streams carried independently drawn observation noise. The twelve fixed trees plus estimated and true-coefficient adaptive references gave 2,240 fits. True coefficients and the exact 256-point domain were used only after sealing for privileged references and secondary evaluation. Two pre-fit decoder/launcher errors were corrected through hashed amendments, with no changes to frozen choices or scientific rules.

Primary regret was 0.02736 for estimated cuts, 0.15610 for the development-best fixed/rank-only rule and 0.08723 for the pilot. Paired improvements were 0.12874 (97.5% CI 0.08259–0.17714) and 0.05987 (0.02677–0.09386). These two comparisons met the declared 0.01-NMSE margin and adjusted-interval criterion. Bootstrap resampling used 20 seed blocks after averaging the four families within each seed. Other calibration conditions and individual families are secondary. Adaptive construction had mean NMSE 0.07086; its structured-family errors were approximately 0.0013, while random interactions remained at 0.27937. Thus the result supports a finite-data construction method in the declared sparse algebraic class, with a clear limit on unstructured targets.
