\paragraph{Trained intermediate dictionaries and image-task optimization controls.}
We extended the MNIST ladder with a trained three-column subtree dictionary and a decoder-only reference, retaining the original forward network and data protocol. Each architecture had one layer of 128 neurons with three proximal and nine distal compartments per neuron. Input contacts comprised 40 excitatory and 20 inhibitory contacts per nonsomatic compartment, with no somatic input contacts: 92,160 active contacts in total. The model contained 2,414,602 parameter slots, including 1,290 decoder parameters. Shunting networks used analytical gate initialization; the raw-additive control used the original occupancy-quantile calibration. The 60,000 official training images were split by seed into 48,000 training and 12,000 validation examples; all 10,000 official test images remained held out. Pixels were divided by 255 and flattened, without further normalization or augmentation.

The delivered variable in this implementation is the error with respect to a compartment's output activation. Its activation derivative remains part of the local eligibility, including the derivatives used to update gate parameters. Exact transport first computes these activation-space errors. The new dictionary then replaces the four errors within each proximal compartment and its three distal children by their mean, giving three nonoverlapping spatial profiles per neuron. A projected-$K=1$ control averages all 12 nonsomatic errors instead. At a fixed network state, both projections preserve the exact field's mean across the 12 sites; the three-profile rule additionally preserves differences between subtree averages. Their comparison therefore isolates the spatial distinctions retained by the dictionary while using the same exact field to acquire its coefficients. The somatic error remains exact. This coefficient calculation uses the exact field, so the experiment tests delivery through an intermediate dictionary; it does not provide a local biological encoder. All rules retain the same local eligibility calculation. The strict-scalar arm reduces the neuronal error vector to one scalar for every core compartment, including the soma; the neuron-shared arm retains the exact error of each soma and repeats it within that tree. Decoder updates are exact for the linear readout in all arms. The decoder-only reference skips core and gate gradient writeback while retaining the complete data stream and decoder training procedure; initial and final core hashes verify that the core remains unchanged.

All conditions used Adam with $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.999$, $\epsilon=10^{-8}$ and no weight decay, with batches of 256 for 180 epochs. After minibatch averaging, gradients of input synapses, intercompartmental weights, gate parameters and the linear readout were clipped elementwise to $[-5,5]$ immediately before the Adam update; the decoder-only reference applied the same rule to its readout gradients. The original rates were $1.5\times10^{-3}$ for input-synapse parameters and decoder parameters, $7\times10^{-4}$ for internal branch weights and $5\times10^{-4}$ for reactivation gates. We compared common multipliers of 0.3, 1 and 3 on all four groups. Three development seeds per architecture, rule and multiplier gave 108 fits across six rules. The projected-$K=1$ control was specified in a separate frozen addendum before any fresh-seed outcomes; its tuning protocol matched the original five rules. Each rule's multiplier was selected by mean minimum validation loss across these seeds; test results were not used. Ten new paired seeds per architecture and rule then evaluated the selected multiplier and, when different, the original common multiplier of one. Coincident selected/common conditions reused the same fit. All 180 epochs were retained and minimum validation loss selected the reported checkpoint. Twelve three-epoch implementation canaries were excluded from selection and scientific summaries. Seed pairing covered initialization, contact construction, data split and minibatch order. Paired differences use descriptive 95\% intervals from 50,000 whole-seed bootstrap resamples. These are comparisons within the stated training budget and rate grid.

We also measured dictionary capture at initial and exact-trained checkpoints on the first 2,048 official test images. For each example and neuron, the field contains the 12 nonsomatic errors. The primary capture measure is the mean of the nonzero field-wise squared-energy ratios; the ratio of total captured to total field energy is retained separately. Activation-space capture matches the trained delivery dictionary. Voltage-space capture, before the local activation derivative is factored out, is reported separately to preserve the coordinate convention of the historical atlas. The two measures need not agree. Checks against two historical checkpoints reproduced their archived voltage-space captures to numerical precision, and instrumentation left model outputs unchanged.
