# Irregular-tree wavelet results

Date completed: 19 August 2026

All 55 frozen reconstructed cells passed the full-rank, orthonormality and
energy-decomposition gates. The maximum orthonormality error was
`4.44e-16`; the maximum relative decomposition error was `3.98e-16`.

## Primary 47-cell replication cohort

After removing the scalar mode, conductance-weighted ancestry-route fields
placed 0.4065 of their energy in coarse irregular-tree wavelets, although
coarse modes comprised only 0.1923 of the available non-scalar dimensions.
The resulting signal-to-isotropic-noise enrichment was 2.351 (95% cell
bootstrap interval 1.995--2.880); all 47 cells exceeded one and the two-sided
Wilcoxon test against one gave `P=1.42e-14`.

The anatomy-specific comparison was weaker. Coarse energy exceeded the mean
column-permuted ancestry control by 0.0345, but the 95% cell-bootstrap interval
crossed zero (-0.0025--0.0713), only 31/47 cells were positive, and the
two-sided Wilcoxon test gave `P=0.0780`. Intermediate and fine actual-minus-
permuted contrasts were also null. Thus the independent cohort supports coarse
route-field structure relative to isotropic coordinate noise, but not a
replicable excess attributable specifically to the observed ancestry layout
over the density-preserving permutation control.

Fine modes showed the complementary depletion: mean enrichment 0.342, with
0/47 cells above one (`P=1.42e-14`). Intermediate enrichment was modest at
1.134 (30/47; `P=0.0160`).

## Original eight cells

The original cohort showed coarse enrichment of 2.475 (2.131--2.899; 8/8;
`P=0.00781`) and an actual-minus-permuted coarse excess of 0.0677
(0.0495--0.0900; 8/8; `P=0.00781`). The failure of the latter endpoint in the
larger disjoint cohort means it must not be used as evidence for a fine
morphology-specific routing advantage.

## Interpretation

Irregular reconstructed trees supply a mathematically valid multiscale address
basis, and their modeled non-scalar route fields are biased toward coarse
scales relative to isotropic credit noise. The ancestry-specific excess seen in
the original eight-cell pilot does not replicate. This is therefore a useful
boundary result: it supports coarse multiscale capacity, not endogenous credit
alignment, learning use, or a dendrite-exclusive linear operation.
