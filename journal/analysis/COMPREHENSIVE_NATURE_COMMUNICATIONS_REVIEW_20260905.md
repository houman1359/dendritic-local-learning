**Comprehensive review of the dendritic learning manuscript — 5 September 2026**

My assessment is that the project contains a substantial conditional theory of dendritic credit routing, but I would revise it substantially before submitting this version to Nature Communications. The strongest existing contribution is a framework for asking which spatial distinctions a restricted learning rule must preserve, with unusually extensive controls separating feedback assignment, forward computation and anatomical capacity. The intended stronger contribution—predicting an optimal physical morphology from task rank or complexity—is not yet demonstrated.

The main problem is not a shortage of experiments. It is that the manuscript presents several different mathematical objects and evidential levels as one predictive morphology theory before establishing their equivalence. A more focused paper could make the existing work considerably more persuasive. A genuinely prospective morphology-selection experiment would provide the clearest route to the stronger paper you described.

This review covers the current canonical main manuscript and Supplementary Information, all nine main figures and all 31 supplementary figures, supplementary tables, the submission documents, and selected code/source-data checks needed to resolve specific issues. It is an editorial and scientific review, not an independent rerun of the entire simulation program or verification of every citation. The manuscript and figures have not been edited.

1. **State the paper’s central contribution in one precise sentence.** A defensible formulation is: “Dendritic morphology constrains a dictionary of spatial credit patterns, whose usefulness depends on alignment with task credit, feedback resolution and learning noise.” Make this the organizing claim in the title, abstract, last Introduction paragraph, first Discussion paragraph and cover letter. At present the reader encounters factorization, vectorized feedback, an operator bound, task hierarchy, physical depth, connectomics, shunting and biological nulls before knowing which result is the main discovery.

2. **Separate optimal feedback allocation from optimal physical morphology.** Choosing coefficients on a fixed tree, choosing a subset of its routes, choosing a route budget K, and choosing the tree itself are different optimization problems. The present results mostly address the first three. A tree’s topology, input placement, branch lengths, compartment count and conductances are not identified by selecting a K-dimensional subspace. Say “morphology-constrained feedback” or “feedback resolution” where that is what was optimized. Main 597–618 and 2338–2368 currently move too quickly between these levels.

3. **Define “optimal” with an objective, feasible set and resource constraint.** An optimum could mean maximum field capture, maximum guaranteed one-step progress, best finite-time loss, best held-out accuracy, or best performance under a wiring budget. These can select different solutions. Specify whether optimization ranges over coefficients, dictionaries, topologies, gains or all four. If retaining morphology optimality, write a constrained selection problem over candidate trees and their available dictionaries, with a stated cost. Report potentially tied optima or a Pareto frontier; do not assume one unique morphology.

4. **Remove the strong “one dictionary per task” conclusion from the current evidence.** Main 1181–1192 and 2353–2368 foreground agreement in 25 architecture-by-family cells. Main 4073–4085 explicitly states that these are only five distinct utility curves repeated across architectures, and all nondegenerate maxima occur at K=8, the largest tested budget. That is an endpoint-ranking result, not 25 independent successful predictions of an optimal interior resolution. SI 841–858 also reports only 135/200 seed–task optimum matches, a missed group optimum at the finest hierarchy, and failure to predict the central K=4 ancestry advantage in most seeds. Report those results together in the main text. Suggested replacement: “The score captured large route-quality differences and favored full resolution in the trained factorial, but did not predict its small intermediate-bandwidth ancestry advantage.”

5. **Do not infer a morphology law from K=8 matching effective rank 7.99.** The spectrum used for that rank is constructed from one-hot context coefficients multiplied by the output error. Eight balanced contexts and near-zero initial logits make a nearly isotropic eight-coordinate spectrum unsurprising. This comparison is largely built into the task. Show the actual spectrum, its coordinate definition and how it changes with training, then test rank predictions on independent task families. Main 2363–2366; score construction in scripts/analyze_credit_phase_existing.py, around lines 199–209.

6. **Define task complexity more narrowly than dataset difficulty.** Credit rank depends on the model, parameterization, state, loss, coordinate weighting and sampling distribution. It is not an intrinsic scalar property of MNIST or another task. Distinguish input rank, label complexity, compositional hierarchy and credit-field effective rank. Two tasks with identical eigenvalues but different eigenvectors can favor different spatial dictionaries; different noise can favor different resolutions even at the same rank. The operational dimension needed to capture a specified fraction of credit energy is often more interpretable than participation-ratio rank alone.

7. **Clarify external error dimension versus fixed spatial delivery capacity.** In the directed single-output tree, the exact compartment field is one somatic error multiplied by state-dependent path gains. Conditional on the forward state, the independent external task-error value remains scalar. A high ensemble rank measures how many fixed spatial profiles are needed across states, not necessarily how many independent errors must be communicated on every trial. State precisely which local computations and state-dependent gates are unavailable in the restricted model. Main 463–476, 556–596 and 2566–2578.

8. **Repair the universal parameter-gradient operator claim.** The fixed-M theorem is valid under its assumptions, but not every central experiment admits a fixed mapping from exact parameter gradient to routed update. In the context-gated task, changing an unselected distractor leaves the exact gradient unchanged, while it changes the shared-feedback update through that distractor’s eligibility. Therefore the same exact gradient can produce different routed updates. The valid conditional output-error-to-update pipeline is not automatically a fixed operator in parameter-gradient coordinates. A clean repair is a general estimator-moment bound, with the fixed-M result as a special case. If d is the routed update estimator, m=E[d], V=Cov(d), and g is the true gradient:

   E[L(w−ηd)] ≤ L(w) − η gᵀm + (Lη²/2)(‖m‖² + tr V).

   Its optimized nonnegative-step guarantee is [gᵀm]₊²/[2L(‖m‖²+tr V)], with the zero-denominator case stated separately. Then identify the experiments where m=Mg and V=MΣMᵀ actually hold. Main 679–700, 1005–1029; SI 711–753.

9. **Separate hypothetical minibatch noise from the noise used during training.** The eight-context factorial used full-batch gradient descent, but its utility reanalysis estimates variance from resampled minibatches of size 64. That can be a useful diagnostic, but it does not show that rejecting that minibatch noise caused the trained differences. Compare the score against its deterministic V=0 version, gradient cosine, bandwidth and initial loss. A prospective noisy-training comparison would test the noise mechanism directly. Main 3021–3023 versus 3118–3127.

10. **Do not call initial local curvature a general smoothness guarantee.** The logistic reanalysis uses the largest Hessian eigenvalue at initialization. A guaranteed finite step needs a curvature bound along the update segment, not only at its starting point. Use an appropriate global logistic bound or a validated segment bound, or call the quantity an initialization-curvature score. Also distinguish “zero certified progress” from “no bandwidth can rescue learning”: failure of a lower bound to certify progress is not proof that improvement is impossible. Main 3122–3124; scripts/analyze_credit_phase_existing.py around lines 85–95 and 219–220.

11. **Correct the expectation-of-capture statement.** Main 659–667 defines a ratio for each field, then calls a ratio of expected energies its expectation. In general E[‖Pg‖²/‖g‖²] differs from E[‖Pg‖²]/E[‖g‖²]. For equally likely fields 10e₁ and e₂ projected onto e₁, these are 1/2 and 100/101. Define “mean per-field capture” and “aggregate energy capture” separately. The MNIST gallery averages normalized field fractions, whereas the anatomical response-operator analysis aggregates energies. Also call E[ggᵀ] a second moment unless the mean is zero. SI 605–613 repeats the issue.

12. **Reframe branch conflict as a branch-selection requirement in the tested model.** Only the selected branch contributes to the logit; the other exact branch gradients are zero. The experiment does not show simultaneous nonzero, opposite exact compartment errors in a conductance tree. It shows that active distractor eligibilities contaminate learning when the context selector is omitted. The selector can reside in feedback or eligibility, as Main 1308–1314 already acknowledges. Carry that qualification into the subsection heading, abstract and figure title. This is a substantive result, but a narrower one than “independently communicated branch errors are necessary.”

13. **State the assumptions behind the conflict threshold and distinguish it from chance crossing.** The compatibility matrix gives a useful analytic prediction under centered class-evidence and symmetry assumptions. Logistic derivatives, branch weights and conditional image statistics evolve during training. Derive the exact regime in which χc=B/[2(B−1)] is a descent boundary. Plot empirical exact-versus-shared gradient alignment at initialization and later checkpoints, alongside the accuracy crossing. A trained chance crossing and an instantaneous zero-alignment boundary are different endpoints. Main 1316–1349.

14. **Report the optimizer interaction beside the physical-depth credit benefit.** The approximately 11-point exact-path advantage within LocalCA is real for that optimizer. However, SI 1522–1537 reports only 0.64 points between full BP and soma broadcast under the standard BP optimizer, and a 13.47-point cost from changing the broadcast optimizer. Put all three comparisons together in the main Results and the relevant figure. The conclusion should be that path resolution interacts with optimization in this task, rather than that three stages inherently require an extra 11 points of branch-specific credit. Main 1546–1550 and 1727–1741 currently emphasize the larger number.

15. **Choose a more specific title while keeping its claims defensible.** The current title is concise and appropriately cautious, but does not advertise the basis formulation. My preferred option for the present evidence is “Dendritic morphology as a basis for local credit assignment.” Alternatives are “Task structure constrains dendritic credit assignment” or “Task aligned dendritic feedback for local learning.” Reserve titles promising prediction of optimal morphology for a version that directly tests that prediction.

16. **Rewrite the abstract around the question, construction, main learning result and biological boundary.** It is within the nominal word limit, but contains too many compressed concepts: factorization, neuron identity, address, gain, dictionary, operator, utility, argmax, hierarchy and reconstruction. Remove the 25-cell optimum claim and the final comparison with “replacing backpropagation.” Give the reader the biological/computational question before a textbook definition of credit assignment.

   A possible abstract, conditional on correcting the theory/application issues above:

   “Dendritic trees compartmentalize synaptic inputs, but when their branching structure improves learning remains unclear. Here we represent feedback over a dendritic arbor as a dictionary of spatial credit patterns. Exact conductance gradients separate local synaptic eligibility from a compartment-specific learning signal, providing a reference for evaluating restricted feedback. A signal–noise analysis identifies conditions under which restricting credit improves expected one-step learning. Across image-classification tasks, one signal per neuron recovers most of the benefit of exact feedback. Controlled context-dependent tasks reveal when branch-selective information is needed and when task-aligned subtree routes help under limited feedback. Serial conductance models further distinguish the demands of forward computation from those of credit distribution. Reconstructed mouse cortical arbors provide sparse candidate routes, and focal shunting modulates modeled gradient transport in high-conductance states. Measured visual responses, however, provide no evidence of preferential alignment with these routes in the analyzed cohort. These results define conditional links between task-credit structure, feedback resolution and dendritic organization, while distinguishing anatomical capacity from biological use.”
