# Inhibition, forward computation and local credit

This study extends the mechanism of Figure 5 without replacing its frozen
cohort. The first question is whether a branch's inhibitory state supplies
useful spatial information for learning after its forward effects are held
fixed. The second is whether the result transfers from one conductance neuron
to a population of production DendriNet neurons. The final analyses ask which
parts of the proposed mechanism can be assessed with the anatomical and
functional observations already used in Figures 7–9.

## Models and tasks

`model.py` instantiates the repository's actual `DendriNet`, `TopKLinear` and
`BlockLinear` modules. Fixed connection masks assign two terminal compartments
to each proximal branch. All active excitatory, inhibitory and intercompartment
conductances, and the affine readout, learn. The core uses identity branch
outputs, directed steady-state conductance balance and reversals EE=1, EI=0.
It is not a spiking, recurrent or reconstructed neuron.

The exact transfer test maps all 24 conductances of the original Figure 5
circuit into one `[2,2]` DendriNet neuron and checks voltages and gradients
against the independent NumPy implementation. The new training factorial is
not a replication of the original teacher task: it adds an identical
excitatory cue channel to every condition so that removing forward inhibitory
selection does not remove the information required by the task.

The development study uses `(neurons, branching) = (1,[2,2]), (16,[2,2]),
(16,[4,2])`. The first two isolate population size; the last also increases the
number of input streams. Do not pool these cohorts or describe the last
comparison as an isolated population-size effect.

Each stream has two independent latent features z, encoded as exp(z) and
exp(-z). Its externally defined evidence is

    f_b = (-1)^b [ (tanh(z_b1)+tanh(z_b2))/2
                  + tanh(z_b1)tanh(z_b2)/4 ].
    y = sum_b c_b f_b.

For selection, c is one-hot. For mixtures, c is sampled from a symmetric
Dirichlet distribution. Neither target is generated by our neuron. Context
also supplies inhibitory activity a_b=4(1-c_b). A held-out selection test
scales irrelevant features by factors 1, 2 and 3 while preserving the relevant
target. The same draws pair conditions; train, validation, test and diagnostic
sets have distinct random streams.

## Forward interventions

For a proximal compartment with child-current sum B and non-inhibitory
conductance D=1+sum(child conductances):

    Shunt:    V = B / (D + gI*a).
    Tonic:    V = B / (D + gI*mean(a)).
    Current:  V = [B - gI*(a-mean(a))*Vref] / (D + gI*mean(a)).

Vref is the mean tonic voltage at initialization on training inputs and is
subsequently fixed. Current inhibition matches the tonic operating point and
its local inhibitory driving force, not all voltages or sensitivities during
training. Mean(a)=4(1-1/B) is determined by the task distribution, not outcomes.
The current intervention is an explicit proximal-output hook; the shunting
and tonic forward paths use the production implementation unchanged.

## Learning interventions

All conditions use the same exact local eligibility and supplied neuron-specific
somatic error. Local rules detach child-to-parent graphs, then differentiate
each compartment only with respect to its own conductances. They do not
evaluate exact paths. Somatic and readout parameters use exact local gradients.

1. Exact backpropagation provides a feasibility and optimization reference.
2. Broadcast delivers somatic error to every compartment.
3. Relative-resistance gating multiplies distal error by D/(D+gI*a) of its
   parent; proximal and somatic delivery remain unchanged.
4. Swapped gating cyclically assigns the gate of another proximal branch.
5. Uniform-RMS gating uses one within-neuron RMS gate for every distal branch,
   preserving delivered-signal magnitude without branch identity.

The swapped gate is an information-matched computational control, not local
access to another branch. Under tonic and current forward modes, the cue gate
is a hypothetical supplied modulation rather than the actual ratio of local
input resistances. A factorial intervention does not establish its biological
implementation.

Signal-RMS matching does not match the parameter update norm. Separate
diagnostics compare all rules at identical weights and inputs, measuring core
gradient cosine and actual loss reduction after an equal-length step in
log-conductance coordinates. Readout parameters are fixed during these steps.
Exact gradients in diagnostics never calibrate the local training rule.

## Development and confirmation

The development matrix contains three seeds, three model sizes/geometries,
two tasks, three forward conditions, five credit rules and two rates: 540
trajectories of 1,024 updates. It is exploratory, not preregistered. Validation
selects a checkpoint including initialization; all endpoints and failures are
retained. Test results do not select rates. Each run records bound contacts,
gradient clipping, selected step, validation history, generalization and
same-state diagnostics. Conductance bounds are log(g) in [-7,7].

Before confirmation, check exact-BP feasibility and whether training is still
improving. Fix the budget, choose rates using development validation only, and
freeze a separate protocol before twenty fresh paired seeds. A failure of the
exact reference is not evidence that a task requires better local credit.
Do not automatically promote a positive development result to the paper.

All jobs use an immutable, hashed code/input snapshot and write to
`kempner_project_b`, with W&B disabled. The production repository is dirty;
the snapshot hashes, not its HEAD alone, identify what ran. Existing paper
cohorts, figures and user edits are not modified by execution.

## Existing biological data

`anatomy.py` reuses the original MICrONS pilot, disjoint MICrONS replication
and Pinky arbors. There are two animals, not three independent animal cohorts.
The existing physical cable builder sets Ra=150 ohm cm and compares
Rm=15,000 and 1,000 ohm cm². Up to sixteen eligible inhibitory sites per cell
are selected by the existing anatomical criterion, before evaluating effects.
Input-resistance-normalized doses eta*Rin=0.25,1,4 are reported alongside the
required conductance relative to the local membrane/synaptic load.

For baseline gradient q(EE-V), a focal shunt changes both q and V. Restoring
the somatic voltage and holding scalar error fixed permits four comparisons:
full shunt, transport-only substitution, driving-force-only substitution and
matched-current injection. Signed changes and descendant-minus-depth-matched
contrasts are summarized within cells. A subtree-constant resistance gate is
also compared with the exact reciprocal-cable transport field. This tests the
scope of the local approximation; it is not observed plasticity.

`functional.py` uses the released split-repeat partner responses. It asks
whether anatomically defined inhibitory domains have more similar responses
within than across domains, and whether that segregation covaries with
inhibitory contact abundance after geometric/coverage adjustment. Split-half
cross-covariance avoids reusing the same measurement noise. Sites require at
least three mapped partners on each side. Scan measurements are averaged at
the same anatomical site before descriptive target-level summaries. There
are no independent-pair tests, no causal claims and no recorded teaching
signals. Inhibitory contact size is not a measured conductance. The seven
targets are from one mouse and some recordings are shared across targets.
